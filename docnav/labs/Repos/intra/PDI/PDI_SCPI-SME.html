Section 1

Business Profile — Anchoring Your Chain

Define the start and end of your supply chain before mapping the steps in between.

Why this matters: Every supply chain has a clear starting point (the raw material or input you acquire) and a clear endpoint (the finished product or service your customer receives). Everything in between is a step in your chain. Defining both endpoints first prevents gaps and overlaps in your map.

Section 2

Chain Steps — Map Every Significant Step

A step is anything that changes what you have: a transformation, a handover, a purchase, or a delivery. Add 3–8 steps.

What counts as a step? A step begins when something meaningfully changes:
  • The physical form of your product changes (raw material becomes finished good)
  • The custodian changes — it passes from a supplier to you, or from you to a customer
  • A specific process is applied that couldn't be skipped without breaking the chain
  • A key cost is incurred (major purchase, shipping, certification)
If two things always happen together and you can't imagine doing one without the other, they are likely the same step.
▶ Show example steps (handmade candle business)
Example — Maya's Organic Candles (6 steps)
Step 1: Raw material procurement (order and receive soy wax, fragrance, wicks, jars)
Step 2: Inventory storage (store inputs in workshop; quality check on arrival)
Step 3: Candle production (melt wax, add fragrance, pour into jars, insert wicks, cure for 48hrs)
Step 4: Quality inspection and labeling (check appearance, scent, burn test samples; apply branded label)
Step 5: Order fulfilment and packaging (pack per customer order; add tissue paper and thank-you card)
Step 6: Shipping / delivery to customer (ship via USPS/UPS; hand off custody to carrier)
Section 3

Chain Health Check — Bottleneck & Visibility Assessment

Answer four questions for each step. The health score 0–10 flags where your chain is most at risk.

Each step gets a health score from 0 (severe problems) to 10 (fully functioning):
  • 8–10 (Green): Step is under control, cost-predictable, well-measured, low risk.
  • 5–7 (Amber): Step has known weaknesses. Monitor closely; consider improvement.
  • 0–4 (Red): Step is a real bottleneck or blind spot. Prioritise fixing this first.

Health check questions appear inside each Step record above when you expand it. Open any step and scroll to the "Step Health Check" panel at the bottom of its form. Scores update the phase map automatically.
