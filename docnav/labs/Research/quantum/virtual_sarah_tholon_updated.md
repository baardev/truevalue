# Where Is the Virtual Sarah? (Revised and Extended)

## Introduction

The question “Where is the virtual Sarah?” begins with a mirror. Sarah stands ten feet in front of a mirror, and her reflection appears to stand ten feet behind it. That reflected Sarah is not physically behind the wall, yet she is not meaningless either. She is a real consequence of the optical geometry. She is visible, structured, predictable, and perfectly constrained by Sarah’s position and the mirror’s surface. In this sense, the reflection is not unreal. It is dependent. It is a virtual Sarah.

This gives us a useful definition: the virtual Sarah is not an independently instantiated Sarah, but a structurally necessary appearance produced by a system.

## The Mirror and the Virtual Tholon

In Tholonic terms:

- A real tholon (N-state) is self-sustaining  
- A virtual tholon is structurally implied but not independently closed  

Thus, the reflection is not illusion—it is **dependent reality**.

## Formal Mathematical Section: Tholon as Transformation Operator

We define a tholon as a transformation operator acting on a state in complex space.

Let initial state:

z₀ ∈ ℂ

Define transformation:

T = i²

Then:

z₁ = T(z₀) = i² · z₀ = -z₀

Thus:

- The tholon maps a state to its inversion  
- This represents a completed D → C sequence  

More generally:

Let:

z = r e^{iθ}

Then transformations are:

- D-phase: z → z·i = r e^{i(θ + 90°)}  
- C-phase: z → z·i² = r e^{i(θ + 180°)}  

Thus:

T_tholon = i²

A full system cycle:

T_full = i⁴ = 1
