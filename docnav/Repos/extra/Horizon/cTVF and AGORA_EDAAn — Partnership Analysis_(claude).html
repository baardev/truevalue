cTVF and AGORA/EDAAn: Partnership Analysis

How the Clarity True Value Framework and the University of Malaga's Agri-Food Data Space of Andalusia complement each other across four commodity systems: West African Shea, Spanish Olive Oil, Spanish Cherries, and Argentine Soy and Beef under the EU Deforestation Regulation.

01

The Core Complementarity: Infrastructure vs. Intelligence

AGORA / EDAAn — Data Infrastructure Layer

AGORA builds the sovereign, interoperable sharing platform for agricultural data. IoT sensors, satellite feeds, yield prediction, pest monitoring, and the governance architecture (IDSA, Gaia-X) that lets cooperatives, farmers, and companies share data while retaining ownership and control.

What AGORA cannot yet do: tell you which supply chain phases the data belongs to, what the D/C balance of a phase looks like, where value leaks between custody transitions, or how to generate integrated natural capital and GHG accounts that regulators require.

cTVF — Analytical Intelligence Layer

The cTVF is the analytical intelligence layer that sits on top of AGORA's data infrastructure. Phase-resolved schemas tell AGORA exactly what metrics, units, custodians, and transparency classifications matter at each supply chain stage.

The cTVF provides the "what data matters and what it means" answer. It is directly compatible with SEEA natural capital accounting, IPBES biodiversity valuation, GHG Protocol Scope 3, and the EU Economic Accounts for Agriculture.
N-D-C framing of the partnership itself: The AGORA Data Space is a Negotiation (N) state, the emergent stable product of Definition constraints (D: GDPR, IDSA standards, sovereignty rules, governance frameworks) and Contribution outputs (C: shared datasets, business models, analytics services). The cTVF provides the schema that populates the D and C measurements at each phase, allowing the N state to be quantified rather than asserted.

The Horizon Europe abstract names this correctly: the University of Malaga / AGORA role is "agrifood supply chain and value chain data model verification, Andalucian data input coordination." AGORA verifies cTVF models with real field data and coordinates the regional data input that makes the models empirically grounded, rather than analytically hypothetical.

Neither cTVF alone nor AGORA alone can deliver a credible CRCF carbon credit, a EUDR due diligence statement, or a SEEA natural capital account for Andalusian olive oil that will satisfy auditors. Together they can: AGORA provides the data governance layer (chain of custody, sovereignty, interoperability); cTVF provides the analytical accounting layer (phase-resolved GHG emission factors, natural capital valuations, equity impact metrics).

Spanish Olive Oil

Andalusia — World Production Leader — Strongest Partnership Fit
Andalusia olive production ~65% of world supply

AGORA olive demonstrators Pest monitoring, EXPOLIVA 2025
Key trade asymmetry Spain export bulk → Italy premium rebrand
Andalusia is not just a regional context for olive oil: it is the analytical center of the global olive oil supply chain. AGORA's existing relationships with Andalusian cooperatives and its demonstrators (Olive Leaf Spot monitoring, EXPOLIVA 2025) make it the natural regional data gateway for cTVF phase modeling.

The Spain-to-Italy value transfer asymmetry is the defining structural problem in olive oil economics. Spain exports bulk oil; Italy re-bottles and re-brands it as premium product, capturing a substantial margin that is invisible to Spanish cooperative members. Mapping this phase-by-phase (cultivation, milling, bulk export, Italian blending, retail packaging, consumer) with cTVF's D/C balance metrics makes the value leakage quantitative, not anecdotal.

AGORA's relationships with Cooperativas Agro-alimentarias de Espana (a named TrueVal-Chains consortium partner) are the data access path into cooperative production records. The ROBIN Horizon Europe project (Andalusia governance, named in the TrueVal-Chains abstract as a complementary partner) adds a policy layer to the data infrastructure.

Olive mill waste (alpeorujo, vegetation water) represents a circular bioeconomy stream. This is a classic cTVF Contribution measurement: the waste outputs of Phase 2 (milling) become the Definition inputs of a parallel bioeconomy phase (energy, compost, cosmetics). AGORA's sensor infrastructure can instrument this flow; cTVF quantifies whether the D/C balance of the waste valorization phase is sustainable or structurally fragile.

Spanish Cherries

EU Horticultural Benchmark — Transparent, Domestically Bounded Supply Chain
Spanish cherry area 29,000 ha
Annual production ~121,000 t/yr
Harvesting cost share ~60% of total production cost
UFO system productivity gain +72% vs traditional vase

Cherries offer a clean, domestically bounded horticultural system where AGORA's demonstrators map directly onto supply chain phases. Unlike olive oil (complex international trade) or shea (multi-continental supply chain with high opacity), the cherry case is relatively transparent and short, making it ideal as a cTVF calibration benchmark.

The cTVF phase structure maps onto AGORA's existing capabilities with high precision. Phase 0 (biological inputs: rootstocks, varieties, propagation) has Definition constraints in chilling hours, soil pH, limestone tolerance, and vigor compatibility. AGORA's climate and satellite data feeds this directly. Phase 1 (orchard cultivation and growth) is where AGORA's yield prediction and water stress monitoring are already operational. Phase 2 (harvest and post-harvest) is where the 72 percent UFO system productivity gain is a measurable cTVF Contribution metric. Mechanization readiness (Darwin-type equipment, robotics trajectories) and sensor integration are shared ground between AGORA's AgroTech Innovation Enabler programme and cTVF phase measurement.

The cherry case is primarily valuable as a comparative benchmark: it is a high-transparency EU horticultural system where cTVF's phase classification should show consistently high N states. This baseline makes the lower-transparency phases of shea and Argentine beef analytically legible by contrast. AGORA's EUFRIN network access (18-country trial data from 2025) can extend the cherry phase model beyond the Andalusian context.

cTVF Provides Phase schema for rootstock selection through cold chain export. D/C balance metrics for Mediterranean soil and climate constraints vs. yield contributions. Mechanization efficiency index. Comparative transparency baseline for cross-commodity calibration.

West African Shea

GGW Corridor to EU Cosmetics and Food Manufacturers — Anchor Commodity

The shea supply chain is the most developed model in this project: supply chain dashboard, value chain, emissions analysis, phi and sqrt2 dashboards, system lifecycle, and water analysis are already built. The data challenge for shea is not in Andalusia; it is in the production corridor from Senegal through Mali, Burkina Faso, Ghana, and Nigeria. AGORA's role in the shea case is therefore structurally different from olive oil.

AGORA cannot instrument West African parkland agroforestry. Instead, AGORA provides two specific things that are critical for the EU-facing end of the shea chain.

AGORA's European Data Space governance standards (IDSA, Gaia-X) are exactly what is needed for the EU import verification layer: the point where West African shea arrives at EU cosmetics and chocolate manufacturers and their Scope 3 GHG obligations under CSRD begin. AGORA can serve as the European data space gateway, providing interoperable, sovereign data infrastructure that allows a manufacturer in Barcelona or Dusseldorf to access verified supply chain data without that data leaving the control of the West African cooperatives that generated it.

AGORA's certification of compatibility with European standards (GDPR, IDSA, Gaia-X) is the trust layer that makes cTVF's shea phase-resolved Scope 3 GHG emission factors credible and accessible to EU downstream buyers. The cTVF abstract describes these as the "first" phase-resolved Scope 3 emission factors for shea. AGORA makes them auditable and legally defensible under CSRD.

AGORA Provides EU-side data space governance (IDSA, Gaia-X) for import verification. European regulatory standards certification. Federated platform infrastructure that preserves cooperative data sovereignty while enabling EU downstream access. Potential Andalusian agri-food industry connections (chocolate, cosmetics manufacturers).
cTVF Provides First phase-resolved Scope 3 GHG emission factors for shea (Phases 0 through 7). Transparency classification for each phase. N-D-C balance metrics for the full GGW-to-EU supply chain. Value chain equity model (women cooperative capture vs. downstream margin appropriation).
Joint Product CSRD-compatible Scope 3 shea data pipeline, accessible to EU manufacturers through AGORA's data space infrastructure, enabling carbon and nature credit revenue for GGW corridor cooperative members while satisfying EU corporate supply chain due diligence requirements.

Argentine Soy and Beef — EU Deforestation Regulation

Most Novel Partnership Fit — EUDR Compliance Demonstrator
EUDR scope Soy, beef, palm oil, cocoa, coffee, wood, rubber
Compliance requirement Geo-referenced non-deforestation proof
Argentine partner CIPPEC, Buenos Aires (named consortium)
Policy partner CEPS Brussels — Mercosur Trade Agreement

The EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR) requires that operators placing covered commodities (soy, beef, palm oil, wood, cocoa, coffee, rubber) on the EU market must demonstrate through "due diligence" that the product did not originate from recently deforested land. The technical requirement is geo-referenced, supply-chain-traceable documentation linking the physical product to a specific plot of land with known forest cover status.

This is precisely a federated Data Space problem with a cTVF analytical layer. The data about Argentine soy and beef origin (satellite land-use data, slaughterhouse records, grain elevator receipts, export documentation) exists in fragmented, sovereign, multi-jurisdictional silos. AGORA's Data Space architecture (IDSA, Gaia-X) is designed to federate exactly this kind of data across jurisdictional boundaries while maintaining sovereignty.

The cTVF's N-D-C transparency classification maps directly onto EUDR's risk categories. Low-transparency phases (feedlot aggregation, grain elevator consolidation) have elevated deforestation risk until geo-referenced origin data is verified. High-transparency phases with confirmed origin data qualify for simplified due diligence. The cTVF framework gives EUDR its quantitative risk backbone; AGORA provides the federated data infrastructure to operationalize it.

AGORA's Demonstration Center can pilot this as a live EUDR compliance demonstrator: a federated Data Space connecting Argentine origin data (via CIPPEC) with EU import documentation (via CEPS, Brussels) and presenting phase-resolved deforestation risk scores through the cTVF framework. This would be the first working prototype of an EUDR-compliant Data Space for Mercosur commodities, positioned at the center of the EU-Mercosur trade agreement policy debate.

AGORA Provides Federated data space architecture for cross-jurisdictional traceability (Argentina to EU). IDSA/Gaia-X interoperability standards. Sovereignty-preserving data governance framework. EU regulatory compliance infrastructure. Demonstration Center as the pilot environment for the EUDR proof-of-concept.
cTVF Provides Phase-resolved deforestation risk index for Argentine soy and beef supply chains. Transparency classification per phase (feedlot, grain elevator, export documentation, EU customs). EUDR risk category mapping from N-D-C balance scores. Supply chain schema for multi-jurisdictional due diligence documentation.
Joint Product First EUDR-compliant Data Space compliance demonstrator for Mercosur commodities, combining cTVF's phase-resolved deforestation risk model with AGORA's federated sovereignty architecture, directly applicable to the EU-Mercosur trade agreement implementation challenge.

Structural Collaboration Mechanisms

  • 1

    Schema Contribution Flow

    cTVF's phase schemas (Phase → Metric → Unit → Source → Custodian) provide AGORA with precise data collection templates for each commodity. AGORA's demonstrators collect data against these schemas. This eliminates the common failure mode where data infrastructure collects everything but cannot answer specific regulatory or market questions because the schema was not designed with the analytical output in mind.

  • 2

    Empirical Validation Loop

    AGORA's Andalusian field data (real cooperative records, sensor outputs, olive mill throughput, cherry yield trials) validates and stress-tests cTVF's phase models. cTVF's N-D-C balance metrics tell AGORA whether its data captures the right signals or misses critical phase boundary information. The validation loop runs in both directions and improves both systems.

  • 3

    Regulatory Compliance as the Joint Product

    CRCF carbon credits, EUDR due diligence statements, and SEEA natural capital accounts all require both a data governance layer (chain of custody, sovereignty, interoperability) and an analytical accounting layer (phase-resolved emission factors, natural capital valuations, equity metrics). AGORA and cTVF each supply one layer. Together they produce what neither can produce alone.

  • 4

    Multi-Actor Panel Access

    The Horizon Europe call requires genuine co-creation with a Multi-Actor Panel. AGORA's existing relationships with Andalusian cooperatives, farmers, and regional administration (Junta de Andalucia, CAPADR) are the access route to olive and cherry cooperative representatives needed for the panel. AGORA is not just a technical partner: it is the regional legitimacy anchor for the EU commodity components of the TrueVal-Chains project.

  • 5

    European Data Space as EUDR Infrastructure

    The EUDR requires federated, sovereignty-preserving traceability across jurisdictions. AGORA's Data Space standards (IDSA, Gaia-X) are not a generic data platform: they are precisely the technical architecture EUDR due diligence requires. A joint AGORA/cTVF EUDR compliance module would be commercially viable as a service product for EU food importers and Mercosur commodity exporters seeking regulatory certainty under the trade agreement.

Positioning the Partnership

AGORA has built the data governance infrastructure that tells commodity supply chains how to share data trustworthily. The cTVF tells them what data matters, what it means, and what regulatory and market questions it can answer. Neither is useful without the other at the scale the EU's bioeconomy, EUDR, and CRCF agendas now demand.

The cleanest pitch for a Horizon Europe consortium or an initial meeting with AGORA is this: AGORA's EDAAn Data Space becomes the regional data infrastructure for the Andalusian commodity chains (olive oil, cherries); the cTVF becomes the analytical intelligence layer that converts AGORA's data into the phase-resolved, N-D-C-balanced, regulatory-grade accounts that cooperatives, policymakers, and downstream buyers actually need. The partnership is not a collaboration of convenience: it is a structural division of labour between infrastructure and intelligence.

Within the TrueVal-Chains Horizon Europe RIA, AGORA's formal role covers data model verification and Andalusian data input coordination. In practice, the relationship is broader: AGORA is the regional legitimacy anchor that gives the project its cooperative sector credibility, its field data access, and its path to the Multi-Actor Panel co-creation requirement that the call mandates.
