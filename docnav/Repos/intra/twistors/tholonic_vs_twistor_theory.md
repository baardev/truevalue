# Twistor Theory as a Tholonic Instantiation: Structural Correspondence Between Penrose's Twistor Framework and the Tholonic N-D-C Model in Four-Dimensional Lorentzian Spacetime

Supply Chain Intelligence Project / Tholonic Model Research

## Abstract

The Tholonic model proposes that all formed and growing systems, from quantum fields to ecosystems to ideas, are governed by a universal triadic structure: Definition (D, constraint and boundary), Contribution (C, flow and integration), and Negotiation (N, the emergent stable state produced by D-C interaction). This paper examines Roger Penrose's twistor theory, the most ambitious geometric reformulation of fundamental physics attempted in the twentieth century, and argues that it constitutes a domain-specific instantiation of the Tholonic structure operating within four-dimensional Lorentzian spacetime. The mapping is not analogical. The two-spinor decomposition of a twistor corresponds precisely to the D-C dyad; the twistor itself is the N-state; the null inner product condition (which selects real, physical spacetime points) is the Tholonic balance condition D = C expressed in the language of complex projective geometry; and the Penrose transform, which encodes spacetime fields as geometric structures in twistor space, is the inter-level recursion mechanism that the Tholonic model predicts at every scale. Convergent production of the constant π in both frameworks, from structurally independent routes, supports the hypothesis that both are encountering the same underlying architecture. The paper identifies the formal work required to establish this correspondence rigorously and discusses implications for the universality claim of the Tholonic model.

**Keywords:** twistor theory, Tholonic model, N-D-C dynamics, spinors, conformal geometry, emergent spacetime, structural correspondence, Penrose, relational ontology, mathematical universality

## 1. Introduction

Roger Penrose introduced twistor theory in the 1960s with a specific and radical objective: to show that spacetime, the arena within which all of known physics takes place, is not a fundamental structure. It is a derived one. The primary objects in his framework are twistors, geometric entities living in a four-dimensional complex projective space (CP³), and the points of ordinary spacetime emerge from relations among twistors rather than being given independently. Physical fields, particles, and interactions are all to be understood as geometric structures in twistor space whose projection into spacetime yields the phenomena we observe.

This paper approaches twistor theory from a different starting point. The Tholonic model (Bomer, ongoing) proposes a universal formative principle: any system that forms or grows, whether physical, biological, social, or conceptual, instantiates a triadic structure in which a definitional or constraining force (D) and a contributive or integrating force (C) interact to produce an emergent stable state (N). The model is claimed to be universal in the strict sense: not merely applicable to many domains, but constitutive of how any domain organises itself. If this claim is correct, then twistor theory, as one of the most structurally precise frameworks in all of fundamental physics, should be expressible as a particular instantiation of the N-D-C structure within its specific domain.

The argument of this paper is that it is, and that the correspondence is precise rather than metaphorical.

Section 2 briefly summarises the Tholonic model's core claims. Section 3 reviews the essential structure of twistor theory. Section 4 establishes the formal mapping between the two frameworks. Section 5 examines the convergent production of mathematical constants, particularly π, as independent evidence of structural correspondence. Section 6 compares the two frameworks' treatment of non-locality and hierarchical structure. Section 7 identifies the research required to formalise the correspondence. Section 8 discusses implications and concludes.

## 2. The Tholonic Model: Core Claims

The Tholonic model begins from the observation that the simplest possible state, the conceptual existence of nothing (zero) and something (one), immediately implies a third state: the relation between the two. Any system capable of self-description must contain at minimum a boundary-making element (what a thing IS, how it is defined and limited), a boundary-crossing element (what a thing DOES, how it contributes to and integrates with a wider context), and the stable state that emerges from their interaction. These are the D, C, and N of the model respectively.

The key structural claims are as follows.

**Triadic irreducibility.** D, C, and N cannot be reduced to a dyad without losing the structure that generates observable states. A system described only by its constraints (D alone) has no mechanism for growth or output. A system described only by its contributions (C alone) has no stable identity. The emergent state N requires both, and is therefore structurally irreducible to either.

**Recursive self-similarity.** Each N-state at level i is constituted by a D-C interaction at level i, but is simultaneously the parent that differentiates into D and C at level i-1. The full structure is: N-parent differentiates into D and C, D and C negotiate, child N instantiates, child N becomes the next parent N. This recursion is formally an endofunctor on the category of Tholonic systems. It generates a fractal thologram in which the same structural law operates at every scale.

**The balance condition.** Systems are most stable and energetically efficient when D ≈ C. Imbalance in either direction increases maintenance energy according to E_maint = |D - C|^α + E_base, where α ≥ 2. D-dominance represents over-constraint (excessive boundary-making relative to integration); C-dominance represents over-dispersal (excessive outward contribution relative to structural coherence). Both are unsustainable.

**Mathematical constants as structural necessities.** The recursion N_child = N_parent + (1/D) - (1/C), seeded with D=5 and C=3 (the first pair of primes bracketing 4, the square of the instantiation value 2), converges to π/4. Multiplied by 4, this yields π. Other prime-seed configurations yield φ (the golden ratio), e, √2, and ln(2). These constants are not parameters; they are attractors of the recursive architecture. They emerge because the structure requires them, not because they are inserted.

**Universal scope.** The model claims to describe how any system forms and grows: physical, biological, social, conceptual, or otherwise. Domain-specific frameworks, including the physical theories of fundamental science, are special-case instantiations of the Tholonic structure within their particular domains. Twistor theory is the candidate examined in this paper.

## 3. Twistor Theory: Essential Structure

Penrose's twistor theory rests on three foundational observations about four-dimensional Lorentzian spacetime.

**The primacy of null structure.** In spacetime, the most physically significant geometric objects are null geodesics: the paths of massless particles (photons). Null geodesics are governed by the conformal structure of spacetime, not its full metric structure. Two spacetime metrics that are related by a conformal rescaling (multiplication by a positive scalar function) share the same null geodesics and hence the same causal structure. Penrose argued that the conformal structure, rather than the metric, is primary.

**Twistors as primary objects.** A twistor Z^α is a four-component complex object composed of two two-component spinors:

Z^α = (ω^A, π_{A'})

Here ω^A is a two-component complex (undotted or unprimed) spinor, and π_{A'} is a two-component complex (dotted or primed) spinor. Collectively, Z^α has four complex components and lives in the complex vector space C^4. The projective version (where Z^α and λZ^α are identified for any nonzero complex λ) is the complex projective space CP³, called twistor space.

**The incidence relation and the emergence of spacetime points.** A spacetime point x^{AA'} (a point in complexified Minkowski space written in spinor index notation) corresponds to a set of twistors satisfying:

ω^A = i x^{AA'} π_{A'}

This is the incidence relation. It defines which twistors "pass through" a given spacetime point. Conversely, a spacetime point is defined by the set of all twistors satisfying this relation for that x. In twistor space, a spacetime point corresponds to a projective line (a copy of CP¹), and two spacetime points are null-separated if and only if their corresponding lines in twistor space intersect. The spacetime point does not exist independently of the twistor relations that define it. It is a derived object.

**The null inner product condition.** Given two twistors Z^α = (ω^A, π_{A'}) and W_α = (μ_A, λ^{A'}), their inner product is:

Z · W = ω^A μ_A + π_{A'} λ^{A'}

A twistor is called null when Z · Z̄ = 0, where Z̄ is the complex conjugate twistor. Null twistors correspond to real (as opposed to complex) spacetime points. The condition Z · Z̄ = 0 selects the physically real sector of the theory from the larger complex space.

**The Penrose transform.** There is a deep mathematical correspondence, the Penrose transform, between solutions of massless field equations in spacetime (electromagnetic fields, linearised gravitational fields, massless particles of any spin) and elements of sheaf cohomology groups defined on regions of twistor space. A field in spacetime that satisfies a differential equation corresponds, under this transform, to a geometric object in twistor space with no differential equation required: the field equation in spacetime becomes a purely geometric (holomorphic) condition in twistor space.

**Conformal invariance.** Twistor theory is naturally adapted to the conformal group of spacetime, SO(4,2) (isomorphic to SU(2,2)), the group of transformations that preserve angles and null geodesics but not necessarily distances. This group acts naturally and linearly on twistor space, making twistor theory the natural language for conformally invariant physics.

## 4. The Formal Mapping

With both frameworks stated, the structural correspondence can be identified at each level.

### 4.1 D Maps to the Primed Spinor; C Maps to the Unprimed Spinor

The two spinor components of a twistor have structurally distinct roles. The primed spinor ω^A encodes the internal structure of the twistor: its position in twistor space, its relationship to the origin, its self-referential boundary. It is the component that specifies what the twistor IS within its own space. The unprimed spinor π_{A'} encodes the momentum, the outward-directed, physically active component: it governs which null geodesic the twistor corresponds to and how the twistor connects to the spacetime it generates. It specifies what the twistor DOES.

This maps directly onto the Tholonic structure:

| Tholonic Component | Twistor Equivalent |
|---|---|
| D (Definition, constraint, boundary, what a thing IS) | ω^A (primed spinor, structural, positional) |
| C (Contribution, flow, integration, what a thing DOES) | π_{A'} (unprimed spinor, momentum, outward) |
| N (Negotiation, emergent state) | Z^α = (ω^A, π_{A'}) (the twistor itself) |
| Observable N-state | x^{AA'} (the spacetime point) |

### 4.2 The Null Condition Maps to the Tholonic Balance Condition

The condition Z · Z̄ = 0 selects real spacetime points. It is the condition under which the twistor generates a physically observable event. Written out in terms of its spinor components:

ω^A π̄_A - π_{A'} ω̄^{A'} = 0

This is a balance condition between the two spinor components of the twistor. When the contribution of ω to the inner product with its conjugate exactly cancels the contribution of π to the inner product with its conjugate, the twistor is null and the spacetime point is real.

The Tholonic balance condition is D = C. When the definitional and contributive forces are in exact equilibrium, the emergent N-state is maximally coherent and the maintenance energy is at its minimum. The observable state is produced at, or near, this balance point.

Both conditions assert the same structural principle: physical reality, the observable, emerges where the two constitutive forces are in balance. Imbalance produces either a complex (non-physical) spacetime point in Penrose's framework or an unstable, high-maintenance-energy state in the Tholonic framework.

### 4.3 The Penrose Transform Maps to Inter-Level Tholonic Recursion

The Penrose transform encodes a spacetime field (an N-state at one level of description) as a geometric structure in twistor space (a D-C configuration at the level above). The field equation that the spacetime solution must satisfy becomes, in twistor space, a purely geometric condition with no additional constraint required. The content of the field equation is absorbed into the geometry of the higher-level space.

This is structurally identical to the Tholonic recursion. The N-state at level i is constituted by the D-C configuration at level i, but from the perspective of level i+1, that same N-state is the D-component or C-component of the higher-level tholon. What is an observable outcome at level i is a structural element at level i+1. The Penrose transform is a formal implementation of this principle: it is the mathematical mechanism by which an observable (spacetime field) is translated into a structural element of the space one level up (twistor space cohomology).

### 4.4 Conformal Invariance Maps to Ratio-Primacy

The conformal group preserves angles and null structure but not absolute distances. Conformal transformations rescale the metric by an arbitrary positive function, leaving ratios and angular relationships invariant. What matters conformally is not the magnitude of any quantity but its relationship to other quantities.

The Tholonic balance condition is a ratio condition. A tholon with D=50, C=50 is structurally identical to one with D=500, C=500. The maintenance energy depends on |D - C| relative to the magnitudes of D and C, not on their absolute values. What breaks balance is the departure of the ratio D/C from 1. Both frameworks identify the relational ratio as the physically meaningful quantity and absolute scale as irrelevant to structural health.

### 4.5 Twistor Space CP³ as the Thologram at the Physics Level

Twistor space is not a spacetime: it is the space in which the formative structure of spacetime lives. It is one level up from spacetime in the descriptive hierarchy, in exactly the sense that the thologram at level i+1 contains the D-C structure that generates the observable N-states at level i. The relationship between twistor space and spacetime is precisely the relationship between a tholonic level and the level it generates.

### 4.6 Non-Locality as Hierarchical Constraint Propagation

In twistor theory, a single twistor corresponds not to a spacetime point but to an entire null geodesic (a light ray), which is a non-local object in spacetime. What is a point in twistor space is extended in spacetime. Quantum non-locality (entanglement, Bell inequality violations) finds a natural home in this framework: correlations that are non-local in spacetime can be local in twistor space.

In the Tholonic model, constraints inherited from a parent tholon appear, from within any child tholon, as structureless context: they are simply "the way things are," with no visible local origin. The constraint is non-local in the child's frame of description because it comes from a level up. This is the Tholonic account of exactly the phenomenon that Penrose's non-locality describes: what looks like action at a distance or contextual constraint without local cause is the signature of a higher-level tholonic structure imposing its D-state onto the child level.

## 5. Convergent Production of Mathematical Constants

One of the most significant pieces of independent evidence for the structural correspondence is the fact that π emerges as a structural necessity in both frameworks through entirely different routes.

In the Tholonic model, the recursion N_child = N_parent + (1/5) - (1/3), seeded with the first pair of primes bracketing the square of the instantiation value 2, converges to π/4. This is mathematically equivalent to the Leibniz-Gregory series:

π/4 = 1 - 1/3 + 1/5 - 1/7 + ...

grouped in pairs as Tholonic generations. The constant is not inserted; it is the unique attractor of the recursive architecture.

In twistor theory, π appears in the geometry of the Riemann sphere CP¹, the space of projective spinors (the projective version of the two-component spinors that constitute a twistor). The Riemann sphere is the simplest non-trivial complex manifold, and its topology and geometry are governed by π in multiple ways: its area in the round metric of radius 1 is 4π; its second homotopy group is Z, with generator the map whose Hopf invariant involves π; and path integrals over the Riemann sphere produce π as a geometric normalisation constant. The spinor inner product space carries a symplectic structure whose normalisation involves π.

Both frameworks require π as a structural output of their respective geometries. They are not inserting the same number by convention; they are encountering it as the natural measure of their respective balance structures. This convergence supports the hypothesis that both are instances of the same underlying architecture.

The golden ratio φ presents a further point of contact. Penrose tilings, discovered by Penrose and associated with his broader research programme, tile the plane aperiodically using two tile shapes whose ratio of occurrence is φ. The Tholonic recursion with appropriate prime seeds produces φ as an attractor. Penrose tilings are related to icosahedral symmetry, which connects to the tetrahedron (the Tholonic structural primitive in three dimensions) through the theory of root systems.

## 6. Philosophical Alignment: Relational Ontology and Mathematical Platonism

Beyond the structural correspondence, both frameworks share a philosophical stance that is itself non-trivial.

**Relational ontology.** Both frameworks reject the view that the directly observable constitutes the most fundamental level of description. In twistor theory, the spacetime point you can measure is derived from twistor relations you cannot directly observe. In the Tholonic model, the N-state you can measure is derived from the D-C interaction you can partially characterise but never directly see in its constitutive action. Both assert that the observable is a projection of a more fundamental relational structure.

This places both frameworks in the tradition of relational physics running from Leibniz through Mach to Rovelli's relational quantum mechanics. It is not a marginal position in physics; it is increasingly the majority view in foundations of quantum gravity.

**Mathematical Platonism.** Penrose argues, most systematically in "The Road to Reality" (2004), that mathematical structures are not invented but discovered; that physical reality is grounded in mathematical structure rather than the other way around; and that certain mathematical forms (complex numbers, spinors, the conformal structure of four-dimensional spacetime) are required by the structure of physical law and not merely convenient for its description. The Tholonic model makes a structurally identical claim: the mathematical constants that the recursion produces (π, φ, e, √2, ln(2)) are structural necessities of the triadic architecture, not free parameters. Both frameworks assert that mathematics is not a description of reality but a disclosure of its formative structure.

## 7. What Is Required for Formal Correspondence

The correspondence identified in this paper is structural and philosophical. Establishing it formally requires the following programme of work.

### 7.1 Expressing the N-D-C Triad in Spinor Algebra

The mapping proposed in Section 4 assigns D to ω^A and C to π_{A'}. A formal derivation would need to show that:

(a) The Tholonic balance condition D = C, when expressed in spinor algebra, is equivalent to or implies the null condition Z · Z̄ = 0.

(b) The Tholonic maintenance energy E_maint = |D - C|^α + E_base corresponds, in the twistor framework, to the departure of the twistor inner product from zero. The quantity |Z · Z̄| measures the distance from the real sector, which is the distance from physical reality.

(c) The Tholonic recursion, when restricted to the domain of four-dimensional Lorentzian spacetime, reproduces the twistor incidence relation as its balance-state equation.

### 7.2 The Tholonic Interpretation of the Penrose Transform

The Penrose transform maps H^1(U, O(-n-2)) (sheaf cohomology groups over regions of twistor space) to solutions of zero-rest-mass field equations of helicity n/2 in spacetime. A Tholonic interpretation would identify:

(a) Which level of the thologram corresponds to twistor space and which to spacetime.

(b) Whether the sheaf cohomology class is interpretable as a D-C configuration and the field solution as the corresponding N-state.

(c) Whether the holomorphicity condition (the requirement that twistor functions be holomorphic) is the mathematical expression of tholonic coherence (D-C balance) in the complex-projective setting.

### 7.3 The Conformal Group as the Symmetry of Tholonic Balance

The conformal group SO(4,2) acts on twistor space as SU(2,2), the group of 4x4 complex matrices preserving a specific Hermitian form of signature (2,2). This group is also the symmetry group of the null condition Z · Z̄ = 0. A Tholonic interpretation would examine whether SU(2,2) is the symmetry group of the D=C balance condition when that condition is expressed in the four-dimensional complex projective geometry of twistor space. If so, the conformal group of spacetime would be identifiable as the automorphism group of the Tholonic balance condition in that domain.

### 7.4 Extension to Other Fundamental Physics Frameworks

If twistor theory is a Tholonic instantiation in four-dimensional Lorentzian spacetime, other fundamental physics frameworks should also be identifiable as Tholonic instantiations in their respective domains. Candidates include:

(a) Loop quantum gravity and spin foam models: Tholonic structure in the combinatorial, background-independent setting. Penrose's original spin networks are a natural starting point, given that their most natural vertex is trivalent and therefore directly maps onto the N-D-C triad.

(b) String theory and M-theory: Tholonic structure in ten or eleven dimensions. The extra dimensions may correspond to higher levels of the thologram whose projection into four dimensions produces the effective field theories we observe.

(c) Topological quantum field theory: The TQFT axioms of Atiyah and Segal describe a functor from a cobordism category to a category of vector spaces. The functorial structure may be a formal expression of the inter-level Tholonic recursion.

(d) Causal set theory: Tholonic structure in the discrete, causal-order-first setting. The partial order of causal sets may be the discrete version of the D-C constraint propagation mechanism.

A systematic comparison across these frameworks would test whether the Tholonic model's universality claim is borne out across the full range of fundamental physics.

### 7.5 The Consciousness Extension

Penrose's own extension of his programme into consciousness (the Orch-OR theory, developed with Stuart Hameroff) claims that quantum coherence in neuronal microtubules is the substrate of conscious experience, and that the relevant quantum process is objective reduction driven by quantum gravity effects at the Planck scale. If twistor theory is a Tholonic instantiation at the level of fundamental physics, and if consciousness is also a Tholonic process (as the model claims, given its domain-universality), then the twistor-consciousness connection Penrose proposes may have a natural Tholonic interpretation: consciousness is the N-state produced by the D-C interaction at the neural and quantum levels, and the twistor and quantum-gravity mechanisms are the physics-level description of what the Tholonic model describes at the formative level.

This connection is speculative, but it is the kind of speculation that the formal programme described above would make testable.

## 8. Limitations of the Current Correspondence

Several limitations should be stated explicitly.

**Mathematical non-identity.** The formal correspondence proposed in this paper is structural, not mathematical. The Tholonic recursion as currently developed operates on real-valued sequences. Twistor theory requires complex projective geometry. Establishing a formal derivation requires either extending the Tholonic formalism to complex-projective settings or showing that the twistor algebra reduces to real-valued N-D-C dynamics in an appropriate limit. Neither step has been taken in this paper.

**Dimensional specificity.** Twistor theory is tightly coupled to the specific dimensionality and signature of four-dimensional Lorentzian spacetime. The two-component spinor algebra that underlies it is a special feature of four dimensions. The Tholonic model claims domain-universality across all dimensionalities. The correspondence proposed here is therefore, at most, a demonstration that the Tholonic model instantiates in four-dimensional Lorentzian spacetime in a form that matches twistor theory. It does not, by itself, demonstrate that the Tholonic model generates twistor theory uniquely or necessarily from its own axioms.

**Empirical status.** Twistor theory remains an incomplete programme in fundamental physics. Penrose's original hope that twistors would provide a natural home for quantum gravity has not been fully realised, though twistor methods have found remarkable success in computing scattering amplitudes in Yang-Mills theory (the Parke-Taylor formula, the BCFW recursion relation, and Witten's twistor string theory). The empirical status of the Tholonic model's universality claim is similarly at an early stage. The structural correspondence established here is a theoretical result, not an empirical one.

## 9. Conclusion

This paper has argued that Roger Penrose's twistor theory is interpretable as a Tholonic instantiation: a domain-specific expression of the universal N-D-C formative structure operating within the domain of four-dimensional Lorentzian spacetime with its specific conformal geometry.

The correspondence is identified at five structural levels. First, the two-spinor decomposition of a twistor maps the D and C components onto the primed and unprimed spinors respectively, with the twistor itself as the N-state. Second, the null inner product condition, which selects physically real spacetime points, is the Tholonic balance condition D = C expressed in complex projective geometry. Third, the Penrose transform is the inter-level recursion mechanism by which an N-state at one level becomes a structural element (D or C component) of the level above. Fourth, conformal invariance is the geometric expression of the Tholonic principle that the ratio rather than the absolute magnitude of D and C is the physically meaningful quantity. Fifth, the non-locality characteristic of twistor descriptions of spacetime is the signature of hierarchical constraint propagation from a higher tholonic level.

Independent corroboration comes from the convergent production of π. The Tholonic recursion generates it as the unique attractor of the D=5, C=3 architecture. Twistor theory encounters it as the natural geometric constant of the Riemann sphere CP¹, the spinor projective space. Both approaches reach the same constant through structurally independent routes.

The formal programme required to establish this correspondence rigorously includes: expressing the N-D-C triad in spinor algebra and showing that the balance condition implies the null condition; interpreting the Penrose transform as inter-level tholonic recursion in the language of sheaf cohomology; identifying the conformal group SU(2,2) as the automorphism group of the Tholonic balance condition in the four-dimensional complex projective setting; and extending the analysis to other fundamental physics frameworks to test whether the Tholonic universality claim is systematically borne out.

The conclusion is not that twistor theory and the Tholonic model are the same thing. It is that twistor theory looks, from the Tholonic perspective, exactly like what it should look like if the Tholonic model is correct: a precise, mathematically sophisticated, domain-specific instantiation of the universal formative structure, expressed in the language appropriate to four-dimensional Lorentzian spacetime with conformal symmetry. Penrose arrived at twistor theory by taking the conformal structure of spacetime seriously and asking what it implied. The Tholonic model arrives at the same structure by taking the triadic logic of formation seriously and asking what it implies in the physical domain. That two such different starting points converge on the same architecture is the strongest evidence yet that the architecture is real.
