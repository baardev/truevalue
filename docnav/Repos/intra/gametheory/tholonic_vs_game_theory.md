Classical game theory, grounded in Nash equilibrium and rational-actor assumptions, has dominated the formal analysis of strategic interaction in economics, supply chain management, and mechanism design for over seven decades. Yet its core predictions remain descriptive of failure rather than prescriptive of sustainability. This paper introduces the tholonic model — a triadic framework decomposing every system into Definition (constraint/structure), Contribution (integration/flow), and Negotiation (emergent equilibrium). It is shown formally that the tholonic sustainability condition generalises Nash equilibrium while embedding resource-awareness and hierarchical self-similarity absent from standard game-theoretic models. Applications to multi-phase supply chain and value chain networks demonstrate that tholonic dynamics produce cooperative equilibria that resist commons collapse and predict phase-specific bottlenecks invisible to aggregate game-theoretic analysis.
