## Abstract

This document provides a comprehensive annotated literature review supporting the N-D-C triadic framework and its application to planetary sustainability. It assembles the foundational mathematical and philosophical sources from which the N-D-C model was developed, the Earth system science literature on planetary boundaries (with a particular focus on publications in Nature and the Nature family of journals), the governance literature anchored in Elinor Ostrom's work on common-pool resources, supply chain structure and sustainability literature, and the three cross-cutting domain literatures identified as essential for Nature submission: food systems, energy and fuel transitions, and sustainable finance. For each source, a brief annotation identifies the contribution to the N-D-C framework and flags its relevance to the planned Nature paper. Sources published in Nature, Nature Communications, Nature Climate Change, Nature Food, Nature Sustainability, and Communications Sustainability are flagged with the journal name. The review is intended as a shared working document between the co-authors and as the evidential backbone for the planned Nature submission.

## 2. Foundational Mathematics and Philosophy of the N-D-C Model

The N-D-C framework is not derived from domain observation but from the structural properties of recursive triadic systems grounded in the first three prime numbers. The following sources constitute the mathematical and philosophical lineage.

### 2.1 Prime and Integer Structure

**[Har79] Hardy, G. H., and Wright, E. M. *An Introduction to the Theory of Numbers.* 5th ed. Oxford University Press, 1979.**
The standard reference for the properties of prime numbers. The identification of 2, 3, and 5 as the seed primes of the tholonic recursion draws on the irreducibility properties established here. The unique factorization theorem and Euclidean proof of infinite primes are the structural prerequisites for treating these three primes as genuinely irreducible bases rather than arbitrary choices.

**[Kos01] Koshy, T. *Fibonacci and Lucas Numbers with Applications.* Wiley, 2001.**
Documents the convergence of Fibonacci ratios to the golden ratio $\varphi = (1+\sqrt{5})/2$, which appears in the N-D-C framework as the stability threshold $B^* = 100/\varphi \approx 61.8$. The Fibonacci sequence is the simplest recursive two-term recurrence; the N-D-C framework generalizes this to three-role recursion.

**[Roy11] Roy, R. *Sources in the Development of Mathematics: Infinite Series and Products from the Fifteenth to the Twenty-First Century.* Cambridge University Press, 2011.**
Documents the historical derivation of $\pi/4$ via the Leibniz-Gregory series and establishes the convergence properties of the alternating series that emerge as fixed points of the N-D-C recursion. Essential background for Paper 1 of this series.

**[Rud76] Rudin, W. *Principles of Mathematical Analysis.* 3rd ed. McGraw-Hill, 1976.**
The standard analysis reference for fixed-point theorems and convergence conditions cited in the formal proofs in Papers 1 and 3 of this series.

### 2.2 Information Theory and Complexity

**[Sha48] Shannon, C. E. "A mathematical theory of communication." *Bell System Technical Journal* 27(3), 1948, pp. 379-423.**
Establishes the formal relationship between entropy, information, and constraint. In N-D-C terms: the D role is the channel capacity (constraint), the C role is the information flow (transmission), and the N state is the fidelity achieved. The balance condition between channel capacity and throughput is structurally identical to the N-D-C balance condition.

**[Sim62] Simon, H. A. "The architecture of complexity." *Proceedings of the American Philosophical Society* 106(6), 1962, pp. 467-482.**
Simon's foundational argument that complex systems are nearly decomposable hierarchies of simpler subsystems. The N-D-C recursive structure is the formal expression of this insight: each tholon at one level is the parent tholon for the level below, and the hierarchy is self-similar.

**[Lan90] Langton, C. G. "Computation at the edge of chaos: Phase transitions and emergent computation." *Physica D* 42(1-3), 1990, pp. 12-37.**
Establishes the edge-of-chaos hypothesis: complex adaptive behavior emerges at phase boundaries between ordered and disordered regimes. The N-D-C balance condition $B \approx 61.8$ identifies precisely this boundary. The stability threshold is not an empirical parameter but a structural feature of the recursion, consistent with Langton's finding that productive complexity is neither fully constrained nor fully unconstrained.

**[Tur52] Turing, A. M. "The chemical basis of morphogenesis." *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B* 237(641), 1952, pp. 37-72.**
The foundational paper on reaction-diffusion systems and pattern formation. Structurally, Turing's activator-inhibitor pairs map onto the D-C duality of the N-D-C model: inhibition is the D role (bounding), activation is the C role (contributing), and the spatial pattern is the N state (emergent). This connection is made explicit in Paper 3 of this series.

### 2.3 Game Theory and Equilibrium

**[vNM44] von Neumann, J., and Morgenstern, O. *Theory of Games and Economic Behavior.* Princeton University Press, 1944.**
The foundational source for strategic equilibrium theory. In N-D-C terms, the minimax theorem defines the balance condition between competing strategies as the N state. Paper 4 of this series formalizes the connection between Nash equilibria and the N-D-C balance score.

**[Wei95] Weibull, J. W. *Evolutionary Game Theory.* MIT Press, 1995.**
Documents replicator dynamics and evolutionary stable strategies, which model the long-run selection of N states under competitive pressure. Directly relevant to Paper 4 of this series and to the application of N-D-C to supply chain competition.

### 2.4 Philosophy and Metaphysics of Triadic Structure

**[Spi77] Spinoza, B. *Ethics (Ethica Ordine Geometrico Demonstrata).* 1677. Trans. E. M. Curley. Princeton University Press, 1985.**
Spinoza's substance-attribute-mode structure is the clearest pre-modern philosophical precursor to the N-D-C triad: substance (N) differentiates into attributes (D, structural definition) and modes (C, particular expressions). This connection is developed in Paper 16 of this series.

**[Lei14] Leibniz, G. W. *The Monadology.* 1714. Trans. R. Ariew and D. Garber. Hackett, 1989.**
Leibniz's pre-established harmony and his binary arithmetic are both foundational. The N-D-C recursion is grounded in binary state space (on/off, 1/0), and the monad structure anticipates the tholonic unit: a self-contained entity that reflects the whole from its local perspective. Paper 16 develops this connection formally.

**[Mil24] Milton, J. W. *Tholonia: The Existential Mechanics of Awareness.* Self-published, 2024. Available at https://tholonia.github.io/the-book/.**
The primary source for the tholonic framework as applied beyond mathematics, including the full triadic cosmology, recursive instantiation model, and philosophical grounding. All papers in this series derive their conceptual architecture from this work.

## 3. Earth System Science and Planetary Boundaries

This section covers the core literature on planetary boundaries, Earth system tipping points, and safe operating space. Nature-family publications are flagged.

### 3.1 The Planetary Boundaries Framework

**[Rock09] Rockström, J., Steffen, W., Noone, K., Persson, A., et al. "A safe operating space for humanity." *Nature* 461: 472-475, 2009.**
*[NATURE]* The founding paper of the Planetary Boundaries framework. Proposes nine Earth-system processes with biophysical thresholds defining a safe operating space for humanity. This is the primary anchor for the N-D-C paper mapping D-type and C-type boundary transgressions to structural supply chain failure modes. The nine boundaries are: climate change, biodiversity loss (biosphere integrity), biogeochemical flows, stratospheric ozone depletion, ocean acidification, global freshwater use, land-system change, novel entities, and atmospheric aerosol loading. Required primary citation for any Nature submission in this domain.

**[Stef15] Steffen, W., Richardson, K., Rockström, J., Cornell, S. E., et al. "Planetary boundaries: Guiding human development on a changing planet." *Science* 347(6223): 1259855, 2015.**
The first major update to the Planetary Boundaries framework; provides the first quantitative assessment of biosphere integrity as a distinct boundary and refines the control variables for several other boundaries. Documents that four boundaries had already been transgressed by 2015.

**[Rich23] Richardson, K., Steffen, W., Lucht, W., et al. "Earth beyond six of nine planetary boundaries." *Science Advances* 9(37): eadh2458, 2023.**
The 2023 full assessment; raises the count of transgressed boundaries to seven of nine. The addition of freshwater change and chemical pollution to the transgressed set is significant for the cross-cutting food and energy domains in this paper. This is the most recent authoritative assessment and must be cited in any submission.

**[IPCC21] IPCC. *Climate Change 2021: The Physical Science Basis.* Working Group I Sixth Assessment Report. Cambridge University Press, 2021.**
The scientific baseline for the climate boundary. Documents 1.1 C of warming already locked in, nonlinear tipping point risks, and the carbon budget remaining for 1.5 C. Establishes that the climate system is structurally in a C-dominant state (emissions exceeding the capacity of natural carbon sinks to bound them), which maps directly onto the N-D-C failure mode analysis.

**[CBD22] Convention on Biological Diversity. *Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework.* CBD/COP/DEC/15/4, 2022.**
The international governance agreement on biosphere integrity. Sets targets for 30 x 30 protected areas and supply chain disclosure requirements that form the policy context for the certification and supply chain sections of this paper.

### 3.2 Tipping Points and Non-Linear Earth System Dynamics

**[Len18] Lenton, T. M., Rockström, J., Gaffney, O., et al. "Climate tipping points: Too risky to bet against." *Nature* 575: 592-595, 2019.**
*[NATURE]* Identifies nine major climate tipping elements and argues that their activation could trigger cascading effects even within the Paris Agreement temperature range. In N-D-C terms, tipping points are N-state transitions: the system crosses the $B < B^*$ threshold and undergoes structural reorganization to a new equilibrium. This paper provides empirical evidence that the tipping-point dynamics are real and imminent, supporting the N-D-C prediction that D-C imbalance at planetary scale leads to irreversible state change.

## 4. Governance of Common-Pool Resources

### 4.1 Ostrom's Framework: Core Sources

**[Ost90] Ostrom, E. *Governing the Commons: The Evolution of Institutions for Collective Action.* Cambridge University Press, 1990.**
The foundational text. Derives eight design principles from comparative analysis of successful CPR governance institutions across six continents. Paper 17 of this series demonstrates that these eight principles divide cleanly into D-type and C-type roles in the N-D-C framework, and that the tragedy of the commons is a C-dominant partial tholon. This book is an essential citation for any paper applying governance analysis to planetary boundaries.

**[Ost05] Ostrom, E. *Understanding Institutional Diversity.* Princeton University Press, 2005.**
Develops the Social-Ecological Systems (SES) diagnostic framework: a multi-tiered variable structure for analyzing the conditions under which CPR governance succeeds or fails. The SES framework provides the ontology of governance variables that the N-D-C balance score can compress into a single scalar. Key limitation noted by Sarah: the SES framework is static and does not capture historical trajectories or long-run co-evolutionary dynamics; the N-D-C recursive structure addresses this by modeling state transitions rather than static snapshots.

**[Ost10] Ostrom, E. "Beyond markets and states: Polycentric governance of complex economic systems." *American Economic Review* 100(3): 641-672, 2010.**
Nobel Prize lecture. Develops the concept of polycentric governance: multiple overlapping centers of decision-making authority at different scales. In N-D-C terms, polycentric governance is a recursive tholonic structure: each governance unit is an N state at its level, with its own D-C balance, nested within higher-level governance N states. This framing connects Ostrom's empirical findings to the tholonic hierarchy directly.

**[Har68] Hardin, G. "The tragedy of the commons." *Science* 162(3859): 1243-1248, 1968.**
The benchmark against which Ostrom's work reacted. In N-D-C terms: the tragedy of the commons is not a failure of human nature but a structural condition in which C (collective extraction) is active without D (institutional constraint) to balance it. The tholonic model provides the formal vocabulary that Hardin's paper lacks for distinguishing this failure mode from the opposite case (over-regulation, D-dominance without C).

**[Cox10] Cox, M., Arnold, G., and Villamayor-Tomas, S. "A review of design principles for community-based natural resource management." *Ecology and Society* 15(4): article 38, 2010.**
A systematic meta-analysis of the empirical evidence for Ostrom's eight design principles across 91 studies. Confirms that principle 1 (clearly defined boundaries, D-type) and principle 3 (collective-choice arrangements, C-type) are most consistently associated with sustainability outcomes. This empirical pattern supports the N-D-C claim that both D and C roles must be present: neither alone is sufficient.

### 4.2 Critiques and Extensions of the Ostrom Framework

The following sources, surfaced by Sarah's analysis, identify the limitations of the SES framework that the N-D-C model addresses.

**[DiMB03] Dietz, T., Ostrom, E., and Stern, P. C. "The struggle to govern the commons." *Science* 302(5652): 1907-1912, 2003.**
Extends Ostrom's framework to global commons problems including climate and biodiversity. Identifies the key challenge: local governance institutions evolve slowly while global extraction accelerates rapidly (the "roving bandit" problem). In N-D-C terms: the D side of governance at local scale is structurally mismatched with the C side of extraction operating at global scale. The tholonic model locates this as a scale-mismatch in the recursive hierarchy.

## 5. Supply Chain Structure and Sustainability

### 5.1 Foundational Supply Chain Sustainability Literature

**[Seu08] Seuring, S., and Muller, M. "From a literature review to a conceptual framework for sustainable supply chain management." *Journal of Cleaner Production* 16(15): 1699-1710, 2008.**
The most widely cited supply chain sustainability review. Establishes a two-dimensional framework: pressure and performance management (D-type: standards, audits, requirements) and supplier development (C-type: capacity building, information flow). This division maps directly onto the N-D-C framework, providing empirical grounding for the claim that sustainable supply chains require balanced D and C roles.

**[Car08] Carter, C. R., and Rogers, D. S. "A framework of sustainable supply chain management: Moving toward new theory." *International Journal of Physical Distribution and Logistics Management* 38(5): 360-387, 2008.**
Develops a risk and sustainability framework for supply chains incorporating environmental, social, and economic dimensions. The three-dimensional structure parallels the N-D-C triad and provides a domain-specific instantiation that pre-dates but is consistent with the tholonic formalization.

**[Ber22] Berg, F., Kolbel, J. F., and Rigobon, R. "Aggregate confusion: The divergence of ESG ratings." *Review of Finance* 26(6): 1315-1344, 2022.**
Documents that ESG ratings from six major providers agree on only 54% of cases for the same company. This divergence is a structural consequence of lacking a phase-resolved framework: different raters weight D-type and C-type evidence differently with no formal justification. The N-D-C framework resolves this by providing a principled basis for the D/C weighting.

**[OECD22] OECD. *OECD Due Diligence Guidance for Responsible Business Conduct.* OECD Publishing, Paris, 2022.**
The international standard for supply chain due diligence. Establishes a six-step process from policy embedding to remediation. In N-D-C terms: steps 1-3 (policy, identify risks, prevent) are D-type activities; steps 4-6 (track, communicate, remedy) are C-type activities. The framework is implicitly N-D-C structured but without formal balance scoring.

**[GRI21] Global Reporting Initiative. *GRI Standards.* 2021.**
The dominant corporate sustainability reporting framework. GRI standards require disclosure of governance policies (D-type) and operational performance metrics (C-type) but do not test for structural balance between them. The TVPCI framework (Paper 2 of this series) provides this balance scoring as a complement to GRI disclosure.

**[Nij15] Nijs, L. "The challenges of applying planetary boundaries as a basis for strategic decision-making in companies with global supply chains." *Sustainability* 9(2): 279, 2017.**
Identifies three obstacles to connecting planetary boundaries to supply chain governance: scaling global thresholds to firm level, allocating fair shares of the safe operating space, and coordinating across jurisdictions. The N-D-C framework addresses the second and third of these directly through its phase-ordered structural model and hierarchical recursion.

## 6. Cross-Cutting Domain: Food Systems

**[God10] Godfray, H. C. J., Beddington, J. R., Crute, I. R., et al. "Food security: The challenge of feeding 9 billion people." *Science* 327(5967): 812-818, 2010.**
The landmark framing paper for global food security. Establishes the axes of the challenge: production increase, access and equity, and sustainability constraints. In N-D-C terms: sustainability constraints are D (planetary limits on what food systems can extract), production and access are C (what food systems must deliver), and food security is the N state that emerges when these are in balance. 7 of the 9 planetary boundaries involve food system drivers.

**[Spr18] Springmann, M., Clark, M., Mason-D'Croz, D., et al. "Options for keeping the food system within environmental limits." *Nature* 562: 519-525, 2018.**
*[NATURE]* Demonstrates quantitatively that no single intervention (dietary change, technology improvement, or waste reduction alone) can keep the food system within planetary boundaries; a combination is required. In N-D-C terms: this is the structural result that N-state stability requires both D and C to move toward balance; neither D alone (technology) nor C alone (behavior change) is sufficient. A key empirical grounding for the N-D-C claim about joint D-C interventions.

**[Willett19] Willett, W., Rockström, J., Loken, B., et al. "Food in the Anthropocene: The EAT-Lancet Commission on healthy diets from sustainable food systems." *The Lancet* 393(10170): 447-492, 2019.**
The EAT-Lancet planetary health diet report. Defines the food system safe operating space within the planetary boundaries framework. Co-authored by Rockström; directly connects the planetary boundaries logic to the food domain. The "planetary health plate" is an N-state for food: the balanced output of D (planetary limits) and C (nutritional requirements).

## 7. Cross-Cutting Domain: Energy and Fuel Transitions

**[IPCC22] IPCC. *Climate Change 2022: Mitigation of Climate Change.* Working Group III Sixth Assessment Report. Cambridge University Press, 2022.**
The authoritative assessment of energy transition pathways. Documents that all 1.5 C-compatible scenarios require rapid scaling of renewables (C side of energy supply) alongside strong carbon pricing and regulatory constraint (D side). The pathway analysis is structurally an N-D-C problem: the N state is a stable, decarbonized energy system; it is achievable only when D (carbon constraint) and C (renewable deployment) are simultaneously scaled.

**[WuLP26] Wu, L., Liu, G., Huang, Z., et al. "Policy interactions reshape the outcomes of carbon pricing policies." *Nature Climate Change* 16: 399-400, 2026.**
*[NATURE CLIMATE CHANGE]* Demonstrates that carbon pricing reduces emissions intensity by 15.4% for emissions trading systems and 8.5% for carbon taxes, but that policy interactions systematically affect these outcomes. Critically: reducing policy conflicts can raise average emission intensity reductions from 10% to 22%. This is the N-D-C balance condition applied to policy design: conflicting D-type policies (carbon price) and C-type policies (subsidies, mandates) that point in different directions reduce net effectiveness. Alignment raises it.

**[Dom24] Domguia, E. N., Nkengfack, H., Pondie, T. M., et al. "Do environmental taxes encourage the decarbonization of economies?" *Environment, Development and Sustainability* (2024). https://doi.org/10.1007/s10668-024-05571-9**
Provides econometric evidence using OECD country data that environmental taxes have a positive and significant effect on reducing CO2 output, with stronger effects in energy-intensive sectors. Uses Econometric and machine learning approaches. Relevant to the finance section for sustainability-linked bonds (demonstrating that policy instruments work) and to the energy transition section.

## 9. Certification, Transparency, and Verification

**[Zwerts24] Zwerts, J. A., Sterck, E. H. M., Verweij, P. A., et al. "FSC-certified forest management benefits large mammals compared to non-FSC." *Nature* 628: 563-568, 2024.**
*[NATURE]* Provides the first rigorously designed evidence that FSC certification delivers measurable biodiversity outcomes. Using 1.3 million camera-trap photos from 14 paired logging concessions in Gabon and the Republic of Congo (474 camera traps, 55 mammal species), the study finds that FSC-certified areas have 2.7 times higher encounter rates for mammals over 100 kg (elephants, gorillas). In N-D-C terms: FSC certification is a D-type structural constraint; the mammal abundance outcome is a C-type contribution; the certification scheme achieves a higher N-state (ecosystem health) by strengthening D. This is direct empirical evidence for the N-D-C causal claim.

**[Tarling26] Tarling, G., et al. "Forest loss persists despite certification and protection." *Communications Sustainability* (2026). https://doi.org/10.1038/s44458-026-00055-5**
*[COMMUNICATIONS SUSTAINABILITY (Nature portfolio)]* Provides the complementary and sobering finding: forest management certification (FSC, PEFC) shows no significant reduction in national-level forest loss over an 11-year high-resolution satellite dataset (2013-2023). Forest loss is lower in higher-income countries and higher where industrial roundwood and fuelwood production is greater. In N-D-C terms: certification strengthens D at the concession level (as Zwerts shows) but C (total extraction volume driven by demand and economic incentives) is not bounded at the national level. This is a scale-mismatch tholon: D and C operate at different levels of the hierarchy.

**[Moser19] Moser, C., and Leipold, S. "Toward 'postnational' sustainability governance? Analyzing the public-private forest governance nexus in the European Union timber regulation and the Forest Stewardship Council." *Ecology and Society* 24(2): 17, 2019.**
Analyzes the governance architecture of FSC and the EU Timber Regulation as a public-private hybrid. Identifies the structural tension between national sovereignty (D-type: state governance constraints) and transnational market standards (C-type: voluntary certification flows). This tension is the D-C imbalance the N-D-C framework formalizes.

## 10. Time-Series and Econometric Methods

**[Pes01] Pesaran, M. H., Shin, Y., and Smith, R. J. "Bounds testing approaches to the analysis of level relationships." *Journal of Applied Econometrics* 16(3): 289-326, 2001.**
The foundational ARDL bounds test paper. Establishes the conditions under which ARDL models can test for cointegration regardless of whether the underlying time series are I(0), I(1), or a mixture. This is the methodological reference for all ARDL applications cited in this review. The ARDL framework is relevant to the N-D-C paper because it can model the temporal lag between a structural change (D-type investment or policy change) and the realization of output changes (C-type), which corresponds to the $\ln 2$ axis in the tholonic five-constant diagnostic (transformation efficiency and temporal gap).

**[Manz25] Manzoor et al. (2025).** See Section 8. Provides a recent application of ARDL to sustainability questions in the context Sarah specifically identified.

**Synergies with the cTVF (True Value Framework):** The ARDL model captures short-run and long-run dynamics separately. In the N-D-C framework, the short-run dynamics correspond to the immediate phase-level D-C interaction (what happens within one phase), while the long-run dynamics correspond to the propagation of imbalance up the tholonic hierarchy (how a phase-level stress reaches the system-level N state). ARDL's error correction mechanism (ECM) is structurally analogous to the tholonic correction mechanism by which a Child N state that deviates from balance is drawn back toward equilibrium by the Parent N. This synergy is worth developing: ARDL could provide an empirical time-series test bed for the tholonic propagation predictions.

## 12. Synthesis: Positioning the N-D-C Framework

The sources assembled above converge on a structural gap that the N-D-C framework fills.

**The Planetary Boundaries literature** (Rockström, Steffen, Richardson) identifies where the limits are but provides no structural mechanism for connecting global thresholds to supply chain architecture. The framework monitors; it does not diagnose.

**The Ostrom governance literature** identifies the empirical conditions for self-governing sustainability but lacks a formal vocabulary for distinguishing D-dominant from C-dominant failure modes, and cannot model the recursive propagation of instability across governance scales.

**The supply chain sustainability literature** (Seuring, Carter, TVPCI) has developed phase-resolved and governance-structured frameworks but has not connected them to Earth-system boundaries or to the mathematical constants that characterize structural stability.

**The food, energy, and finance domain literatures** each identify the same structural problem (constraint/output imbalance producing unsustainable trajectories) in domain-specific vocabulary, without a cross-cutting formal framework.

**The N-D-C framework** provides the cross-cutting formal structure: a phase-resolved balance score grounded in recursive triadic mathematics, with a stability threshold derived from the golden ratio, a five-constant diagnostic that identifies five distinct failure modes, and a governance model that nests Ostrom's empirical findings within a formal hierarchical structure. Its claim to Nature is that it reframes planetary sustainability governance from a monitoring problem to a structural balance problem, opening a new intervention design space at the phase level.

## Appendix A: Extended Supply Chain Structure Literature

This appendix expands Section 5 with sources specifically addressing the structural architecture of supply chains: their phase and tier organization, governance typologies, network topology, and quantitative structural analysis. These are the sources Sarah identified as "enormously useful" for grounding the N-D-C structural claims in the established supply chain literature.

### A.1 The Value Chain Concept: Foundational Framing

**[Por85] Porter, M. E. *Competitive Advantage: Creating and Sustaining Superior Performance.* Free Press, 1985.**
The foundational text for value chain analysis. Porter's primary and support activity framework divides the firm into a sequenced set of value-adding phases. In N-D-C terms: support activities (infrastructure, HR, technology, procurement) are D-type (they define the conditions under which primary activities operate); primary activities (inbound logistics, operations, outbound logistics, marketing, service) are C-type (they constitute the actual contribution flow). The margin is the N state: the value extracted when D and C are in productive balance. Porter's framework is a pre-formal intuition of the same structure the N-D-C model formalizes.

### A.2 Global Value Chain Governance

**[GHS05] Gereffi, G., Humphrey, J., and Sturgeon, T. "The governance of global value chains." *Review of International Political Economy* 12(1): 78-104, 2005.**
The most widely cited theoretical framework for GVC governance (6,501 citations). Identifies five governance types - hierarchy, captive, relational, modular, and market - determined by three variables: (1) complexity of inter-firm transactions, (2) ability to codify those transactions, and (3) supplier capabilities. In N-D-C terms: these three variables map directly onto the N-D-C triad. Transaction complexity sets the D requirements; codifiability determines the transparency of the C flow; supplier capabilities determine whether the N state (a viable, functional supply relationship) can be achieved. The five governance types are the five N states achievable under different D/C configurations. This framing makes GVC governance a direct domain application of the N-D-C balance theory.

**[Ger20] Gereffi, G., Lim, H. C., and Lee, J. "Trade-based industrial policy in the era of global value chains: A review of the multi-disciplinary literature." *Journal of International Business Studies* 51: 1-26, 2020.**
A comprehensive review of 87 GVC studies across disciplines. Documents the shift from product-level to firm-level to network-level analysis as the field matures. The recent enterprise-level database developments (see A.3 below) are the data infrastructure for this maturation. Relevant to N-D-C because the multi-scale nesting of supply chain governance (firm within GVC within macro-environment) is structurally identical to the tholonic hierarchy.

**[GVC16] Gereffi, G., and Fernandez-Stark, K. *Global Value Chain Analysis: A Primer.* 2nd ed. Duke University Center on Globalization, Governance and Competitiveness, 2016.**
The standard practitioner introduction to GVC analysis. Defines the six analytical dimensions of a GVC: input-output structure, geographic scope, governance structure, institutional context, upgrading trajectories, and stakeholder analysis. All six map onto the N-D-C framework: input-output and governance are D; geographic scope and institutional context bound the D envelope; upgrading and stakeholder integration are C; the viable GVC is the N state.

### A.3 Quantitative Supply Chain Network Structure

**[Cha24] Chakraborty, A., Reisch, T., et al. "Inequality in economic shock exposures across the global firm-level supply network." *Nature Communications* 15: 2024. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-46126-w**
*[NATURE COMMUNICATIONS]* Maps the global firm-level supply network and quantifies economic shock exposure. Key finding: rich countries expose poor countries more strongly to systemic risk than vice versa, and this risk is highly concentrated. In N-D-C terms: systemic risk concentration in a small number of nodes is a structural signature of D-C imbalance at the network level. The most systemically risky nodes are those where C (supply flow through the node) vastly outweighs D (redundancy, substitutability, governance constraint). The paper provides empirical data supporting the N-D-C prediction that structural imbalance is both measurable and concentrated.

**[Meg25] Mealy, P., et al. "Construction of an enterprise-level global supply chain database." *Nature Communications* 16, 2025. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-66083-2**
*[NATURE COMMUNICATIONS]* Documents the first enterprise-level multi-regional input-output (EMRIO) database, covering 9,466 companies, 86,305 subsegments, and 121 countries. This database is the empirical infrastructure the N-D-C framework needs for firm-level phase-resolved analysis. The EMRIO structure (purchases and sales at firm-subsegment level) directly provides the D and C primitives for N-D-C scoring: purchases flowing in are C for the receiving node; specifications and requirements imposed are D. The paper explicitly flags supply chain transparency, life-cycle assessment, and Scope 3 emissions targets as primary applications, all of which the N-D-C framework addresses.

**[Hel26] Helmstaedter, M., et al. "Systemic risk mitigation in supply chains through network rewiring." *Scientific Reports* (2026). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-42549-1**
*[SCIENTIFIC REPORTS (Nature portfolio)]* Demonstrates that supply chain systemic risk can be reduced by 16-50% through targeted rewiring of supplier-customer pairs without reducing production output. Critically: the risk reduction is achieved through changes in connectivity that are not intuitively obvious - the key is not reducing total linkages but redistributing them to create structural balance. This is the N-D-C intervention claim stated empirically: structural rebalancing (not uniform constraint or uniform expansion) is the mechanism of resilience improvement.

**[Thu22] Thurner, S., et al. "Quantifying firm-level economic systemic risk from nation-wide supply networks." *Scientific Reports* (2022). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-11522-z**
*[SCIENTIFIC REPORTS (Nature portfolio)]* Using a complete national VAT dataset, constructs the firm-level production network of an entire economy and finds that 0.035% of companies have systemic risk impacting 23% of national economic production. Firm size does not predict systemic risk; network position does. This is the N-D-C structural claim in empirical form: the health of the N state (economic production) depends on the structural balance properties of the D-C network, not on the size of individual nodes.

### A.4 Phase Resolution and Tier-Structured Accounting

**[Dav26] Davis, S. J., Dumit, A., Li, M., et al. "The importance of multiregional accounting for corporate carbon emissions." *Nature Communications* 17: 1014, 2026. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-67759-5**
*[NATURE COMMUNICATIONS]* Demonstrates that single-region Scope 3 accounting systematically miscalculates corporate carbon footprints because it ignores regional variation in emissions intensity. Multiregional resolution substantially alters both the geography and the rank order of upstream emissions sources. In N-D-C terms: this is the phase-resolution argument stated empirically. Aggregate chain-level accounting (no phase distinction) produces structurally misleading results; phase-resolved accounting (the N-D-C approach) reveals the actual D-C imbalance location.

**[Net25] Perez-Carmona, A., et al. "Net-zero greenhouse gas mitigation potential across multi-tier supply chains." *Communications Earth and Environment* (2025). https://doi.org/10.1038/s43247-025-02173-9**
*[COMMUNICATIONS EARTH AND ENVIRONMENT (Nature portfolio)]* Finds that constraining Scope 3 reporting to tiers 1 and 2 maintains 56-70% of total coverage while substantially reducing disclosure costs and improving data quality. Also finds that deep Scope 3 emissions (beyond tier 2) represent 48-74% of total supply chain emissions in complex chains. In N-D-C terms: this is the hierarchy argument - the child N states (individual phases) carry the majority of the structural information, but the parent N state (total chain) can be partially approximated from the first two tiers. The paper also identifies where tradable market mechanisms are needed, which maps onto the e-axis of the tholonic five-constant diagnostic.

### A.5 Commodity Chain Structure: Foundational Sources

**[Ger94] Gereffi, G. "The organization of buyer-driven global commodity chains: How US retailers shape overseas production networks." In G. Gereffi and M. Korzeniewicz (eds.), *Commodity Chains and Global Capitalism.* Praeger, 1994, pp. 95-122.**
The foundational paper distinguishing producer-driven from buyer-driven commodity chains. Producer-driven chains are D-dominant: the manufacturer controls the chain structure and specifies what moves through it. Buyer-driven chains are C-dominant: large retailers and brand-name companies drive sourcing and specification from the consumer end. Neither extreme is stable; both require negotiated N states. This is the earliest empirical formulation of the D-C governance duality in commodity chains.

**[Hop94] Hopkins, T. K., and Wallerstein, I. "Commodity chains: Construct and research." In G. Gereffi and M. Korzeniewicz (eds.), *Commodity Chains and Global Capitalism.* Praeger, 1994, pp. 17-19.**
The world-systems origin of commodity chain analysis. Establishes the view of commodity chains as organized sequences of labor and production processes culminating in a finished commodity for sale. The sequential phase structure is the foundational ontology on which the N-D-C phase model builds.

### A.6 N-D-C Structural Claims in Context of Supply Chain Literature

The supply chain structure literature supports five specific N-D-C claims:

1. **Phase sequencing is real and structurally significant.** Porter (1985), Hopkins and Wallerstein (1994), and Gereffi (1994) establish that supply chains are ordered sequences of value-adding phases, not unstructured networks. The N-D-C framework formalizes this by assigning each phase its own D-C balance score.

2. **Governance is the D-C relationship formalized.** Gereffi, Humphrey, and Sturgeon (2005) show empirically that supply chain governance types correspond to different D/C configurations: hierarchy (high D control), captive (high D with weak C), relational (balanced D and C), modular (structured C with codified D), and market (minimal D). The N-D-C balance score provides a continuous scalar for this typology.

3. **Systemic risk is a structural property, not a size property.** Thurner et al. (2022) and Chakraborty et al. (2024) demonstrate that systemic risk is determined by network position (structural balance) rather than firm size. This is the N-D-C structural diagnosis claim: the balance score predicts risk, not the absolute magnitude of D or C.

4. **Phase-resolved measurement changes intervention priorities.** Davis et al. (2026) and Perez-Carmona et al. (2025) show that aggregate versus phase-resolved accounting produces different intervention priorities. The N-D-C framework makes this precise: the balance score at each phase identifies the specific D-C imbalance requiring intervention.

5. **Structural rebalancing is the mechanism of resilience.** Helmstaedter et al. (2026) demonstrate empirically that systemic risk is reduced not by adding capacity uniformly but by targeted structural rewiring. This is the N-D-C intervention principle: move D and C toward balance at the identified bottleneck phase.
