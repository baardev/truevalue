description: 'The Danube Delta and associated wetlands contain the largest continuous
  reed bed in the world, covering approximately 1,750 km2 of Phragmites australis
  (common reed) within the Danube Delta Biosphere Reserve alone, with a further 850
  km2 of reed-dominated wetland in the floodplain of the lower Danube and Danube-Black
  Sea canal zone. Total reed bed area across the basin is estimated at 2,800 km2.
  The delta reed beds produce approximately 800,000 tonnes of dry reed biomass per
  year, representing one of the largest renewable biomass resources in Europe. The
  natural service cascade runs from hydrological connectivity and nutrient loading
  (Phase 0) through reed establishment and colonisation (Phase 1), biomass accumulation
  (Phase 2), standing stock maturation (Phase 3), and harvestable biomass delivery
  (Phase 4). The primary ecological bottleneck is Phase 0: altered hydrology from
  the Chilia arm regulation and sediment reduction (57% less sediment reaching the
  delta from Iron Gate dams) is causing lateral reed bed expansion onto previously
  open water while simultaneously degrading the productivity of older reed stands.
  This subproject pairs with human_reed_industry to form the paired_reed_harvesting
  composite.

  '
