description: 'Danube floodplain forests historically covered approximately 300,000
  ha (3,000 km²) along the main river and its tributaries. River regulation, flood
  embankments, and agricultural conversion have reduced this to approximately 160,000
  ha (47% loss). The remaining forests are dominated by white willow (Salix alba),
  white poplar (Populus alba), black alder (Alnus glutinosa), common ash (Fraxinus
  excelsior), and pedunculate oak (Quercus robur) in the hardwood forests farther
  from the channel. Total carbon stock is approximately 17 million tonnes CO2 in standing
  biomass. The ecological production function cascade runs from hydrological connectivity
  (flood pulse) through soil formation, tree establishment, biomass accumulation,
  and ecosystem service delivery. The primary structural bottleneck is Phase 0: the
  annual flood pulse that delivers sediment and nutrients to the floodplain has been
  reduced by approximately 40% by the Iron Gate dams and tributary embankments, suppressing
  the natural regeneration cycle. This subproject pairs with human_floodplain_forestry_operations
  to form the paired_floodplain_forestry composite.
