description: 'The Danube River channel is the physical backbone of the entire basin
  hydrological system, providing the primary conduit for freshwater, sediment, nutrients,
  and biota from the Alps and Carpathians to the Black Sea. The river stretches 2,860
  km from source to delta, draining 801,463 km2 across 19 countries. The natural channel
  service cascade models the system from precipitation and snowmelt (Phase 0) through
  tributary confluence and flow accumulation (Phase 1), channel morphology and substrate
  function (Phase 2), sediment transport and geomorphic work (Phase 3), and multi-purpose
  flow delivery to the delta and Black Sea (Phase 4). The primary ecological bottleneck
  is Phase 3 (Sediment Transport, 72% balance): Iron Gate I and II trap approximately
  57% of sediment that historically reached the delta, permanently altering the geomorphological
  regime of the lower Danube and accelerating delta subsidence. The channel also carries
  the legacy of 20th-century channelisation: approximately 80% of the historical side-arm
  and floodplain network has been cut off, reducing channel sinuosity, increasing
  flood velocities, and eliminating lateral connectivity that supports fisheries,
  reed beds, and floodplain forests. This subproject pairs with human_commercial_navigation
  to form the paired_navigation composite.

  '
