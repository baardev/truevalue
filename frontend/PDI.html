Module 1

Anchor the Chain

Answer these seven questions once, before identifying any events.

When to use: Once, at the start — before listing events.
What it does: Establishes the two fixed endpoints of the chain (origin and market), identifies units at each end, and produces your first-pass list of physical forms and actor classes.
How: Answer precisely. Resist the urge to describe processes — that happens in Module 2. This module is only about naming states and actors.

Module 2

Event Inventory

One record per candidate event. Repeat until all events are documented.

What is an event? Anything that changes: the physical form, who holds the material, where it is, what process is applied, or who owns it. When in doubt — record it. Module 3 determines whether it becomes a phase boundary.

How to identify events: Use the physical forms (A6) and actor classes (A7) from Module 1. Each transition between forms is an event. Each transition between actor classes is an event.

Scoring: The boundary score calculates automatically as you answer B3–B11. The interpretation appears at the bottom of each event record.

Module 3

Boundary Scoring and Phase Map

Apply scoring rules to all events; produce the confirmed phase map.

When to use: After all Event Records in Module 2 are complete.
Scoring rules:
  • Score 0–1: Sub-process regardless of B11.
  • Score 2–3 + B11=Yes: Confirmed phase boundary.
  • Score 2–3 + B11=No: Sub-process — a boundary without a definable output is structurally incomplete.
  • Score 4–6: Confirmed boundary regardless of B11 (but note B11=No as opacity flag).

Module 4

Opacity Tagging

Four measurability tests per confirmed phase. One record per phase.

When to use: After the Phase Map in Module 3 is confirmed.
What it does: Assigns a High / Medium / Low transparency classification to each phase. This is a structural assessment of measurability — not a judgment about the industry.
Scoring: 4 Yes = High transparency. 2–3 Yes = Medium. 0–1 Yes = Low.
▶ Show example (Drinking water — Phase 2: Water treatment)
Example — Phase 2: Water treatment
C1: Yes — DWI annual reports publish treatment works compliance data.  C2: Yes — water utilities are publicly registered companies.
C3: Yes — DWI inspectors have statutory right of access; annual reports published.  C4: Yes — treated water quality at outlet is a legally mandated monitoring point.
Opacity score: 4 → High transparency. Water treatment is the most externally regulated phase in the chain.
