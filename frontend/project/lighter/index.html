Chain Balance (N-D-C)

The disposable lighter supply chain is worth studying because it represents a near-limit case of optimization, where extreme cost constraints force innovation across materials, design, logistics, and production. By compressing a complex, safety-critical device into a few cents, it reveals how marginal gains at every step compound into overwhelming competitive advantage, offering a clear, real-world model of efficiency, scalability, and survival in global manufacturing.

Key structural finding: The lighter supply chain is compressed to the physical and economic limit. The Wenzhou manufacturing cluster (approximately 3,000 factories; 200,000 workers) has eliminated every fraction of a cent from the production cost. The only rent remaining in the entire chain is the BIC brand premium: $0.60 per unit at a 67% markup over a functionally identical generic OEM product. This is the canonical TVPCI example: extreme supply chain optimization leaves brand abstraction as the sole available value extraction point. Primary chain bottleneck is Phase 8 (End-of-Life and Waste Management, 56.7% balance): 10 billion lighters per year with <1% recycling, 50,000 MT PP plastic waste, and 14,500 MT CO2e butane venting. No producer take-back programme. No EPR obligation for the device in any market except France (packaging only). Secondary bottlenecks: Phase 1 (Component Manufacturing, 70.0%) and Phase 2 (Lighter Assembly, 72.0%) — both opaque, private, sub-cent margin.
