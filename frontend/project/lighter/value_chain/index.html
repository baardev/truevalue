💹 Lighter — Value Chain Analysis

The value chain is kept strictly separate from the physical supply chain per Rule Set 1 (Separation of Concerns). This layer analyzes prices, margins, and the E-layer (financial abstraction) — not physical flow, custody, or transformation. All data in this layer is sourced from BIC Group Annual Report 2023, NACS State of the Industry 2023, UN Comtrade, and CME/ICIS commodity prices.

72
E-Layer Score: 72/100 (Moderate Coupling) The lighter supply chain has moderate abstract coupling overall. LPG futures (0.90), PP resin forwards (0.85), and letters of credit (0.95) are tightly coupled. The only significant decoupling is the BIC brand premium: $0.60/unit at a coupling ratio of 0.60. This is the primary TVPCI finding — brand abstraction is the sole available rent in an otherwise fully price-compressed commodity chain.
Value chain financial data status: Core value chain metrics (FOB price, distributor margin, retail margin, BIC brand premium) are populated from public sources (BIC Annual Report 2023; NACS SOTI 2023; UN Comtrade; direct observation). Full phase-by-phase financial modelling (P&L by actor; what-if simulator; NPV analysis) is deferred pending additional data. Supply lighter_value_metrics.json to activate interactive dashboards.

E-Layer Analysis — Abstract Coupling by Phase

Primary E-layer finding: The BIC brand premium (Phase 7, coupling ratio 0.60) is the only meaningful abstract value decoupling in the entire lighter supply chain. All other financial instruments are tightly coupled (0.85-0.95) to physical flows. This confirms the TVPCI theoretical prediction: in a commodity compressed to the physical limit, brand abstraction is the last available rent.

Five-Model Value Context

The five mathematical model axes (π, φ, √2, ln2, e) applied to the lighter value chain:

ModelAxisScoreValue Chain Interpretation
πOperational81.9 Phase-level D-C balance is broadly coherent. The physical manufacturing cost (D-constraints from ISO tolerances) and the contribution to value (C-outputs: FOB price, volume) are reasonably balanced across 6 of 8 phases. The two bottleneck phases (1, 2) pull operational efficiency below the ideal. Interpretation: the chain runs efficiently but leaves no margin for disruption.
φProportional75.0 Value does not scale proportionally through phase boundaries. The manufacturing cluster (Phases 1-2) captures approximately $0.03-$0.05 in aggregate margin on a product that retails at $0.89-$1.49. Golden-ratio value scaling is violated: the ratio of retail value to factory gate cost is 7-12×, but this uplift occurs entirely at the brand boundary (Phase 7), not across phases proportionally.
√2Structural78.0 Structural load is partially asymmetric. The concentration of manufacturing in Wenzhou creates a single-point custody risk that adds structural overhead far beyond the natural √2 minimum for a neutral transfer boundary. Ocean freight (Phase 5) is a well-functioning √2-symmetric conduit. The structural score of 78 reflects moderate asymmetry: most transfer phases are neutral, but manufacturing concentration creates a systemic vulnerability.
ln(2)Growth70.0 Transformation phases show moderate growth constraint. Certification (Phase 3) and retail (Phase 7) are the only meaningful value transformation nodes — the phases where the product gains economic significance beyond its physical cost. Phases 1-2 (manufacturing) are growth-neutral: they produce volume but not value growth. The ln(2) score of 70 reflects a chain where growth is compressed by competition to a single transformation point (brand at retail).
eAbstract72.0 Abstract coupling is moderate. LPG futures (0.90), PP resin forwards (0.85), and letters of credit (0.95) are tightly coupled. The BIC brand premium (0.60) is the sole significant decoupling point. The e-score of 72 reflects a chain that is mostly well-coupled physically but carries one meaningful abstract claim (the BIC brand). This is consistent with the TVPCI prediction: in a commodity this price-compressed, only one abstract rent survives.
