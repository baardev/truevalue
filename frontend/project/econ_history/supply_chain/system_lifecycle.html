Structural Interpretation

The intellectual lifecycle of economics is strongest during the period of mathematical and institutional maturation (Lifecycle Phase 2, 1871-1973), where both constraint resolution and institutional output are high. The Keynesian epoch is the individual supply chain peak (N=241, balance 91%) because a catastrophic external crisis (the Depression) compressed what might have taken decades of theoretical evolution into a decade of intensive institutional construction. D and C achieve near-perfect balance under extreme pressure, confirming the Tholonic principle that crisis-driven constraint loads, when matched by corresponding C outputs, produce the highest N states.

The current open transition (Lifecycle Phase 4) is a structural bottleneck because D has not reconstituted after the 2008 falsification. Multiple C streams proliferate without a binding constraint framework. This is structurally identical to the post-Roman collapse of Phase 1 (500-1000 CE), where empire D dissolved and medieval institutional D took centuries to fully reconstitute. The difference is that communication technology now compresses the transition timeline, but cannot eliminate the structural requirement for a new D framework to emerge before a stable child N can instantiate.

Bond intervention logic (if applied): a bond instrument targeting Phase 9 would fund the institutional infrastructure (data commons, open research platforms, central bank independence frameworks, climate accounting standards) that creates binding D constraints, helping the proliferating C outputs converge on a new coherent paradigm. The instrument would succeed not by picking a winning paradigm but by strengthening the institutional D substrate that any paradigm requires to function.
