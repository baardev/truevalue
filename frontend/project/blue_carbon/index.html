Due-Diligence Questions

Character

  • Who is the issuer or sponsor?
  • Who has legal authority to represent the project?
  • What is the delivery history of the sponsor, local partners, and technical providers?
  • Can the project explain its ecological, social, financial, and governance model clearly to investors?

Capacity

  • Is the restoration or conservation intervention technically feasible?
  • Which organizations will perform implementation, monitoring, verification, community engagement, and reporting?
  • Does the project have enough operating budget and technical staff to maintain outcomes over time?
  • Which capacity gaps require grant, concessional, or technical-assistance support?

Collateral

  • Are any guarantees, reserve accounts, offtake agreements, or concessional tranches available?
  • Are ecosystem-service revenue rights legally clear?
  • Are carbon or resilience claims transferable, pledgeable, or usable as repayment support?
  • Which collateral claims are real, and which are only narrative?

Consistency Or Certainty

  • Which standards, registries, or frameworks govern the blue carbon claims?
  • Are national policies, NDCs, NAPs, and coastal-zone rules aligned?
  • Is buyer demand stable enough to support long-term revenue?
  • Are monitoring, reporting, and verification rules stable enough for investors?

Coupon-Structure Implication

The current evidence is not enough to recommend a final coupon structure. cTVF can only say that coupon advice depends on KPI quality, ecosystem-service measurement, verification credibility, and financial mechanism design.

Defensible at this stage:

  • Fixed coupon blue bond with use-of-proceeds restrictions
  • Blended or concessional structure that reduces early-stage risk
  • Guarantee-enhanced structure if a development bank or guarantor participates
Step-up, step-down, and outcome-linked coupons should wait until KPI definitions, MRV, verification, and investor eligibility are credible and documented.

MRV Standard

Verra VCS dominates, accounting for approximately 99% of all blue carbon issuances. The primary methodology is VM0033 (Tidal Wetland and Seagrass Restoration), currently being revised to v2.0 to include conservation activities (Step 3 draft, Silvestrum Climate Associates). Other active standards include Gold Standard (2024 mangrove methodology), American Carbon Registry, Plan Vivo, and Australia's ACCU Scheme. Projects should target both registry certification and ICVCM Core Carbon Principles (CCP) approval for the highest-integrity supply pathway.
