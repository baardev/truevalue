# Blue Carbon Market Data 2025 Source Note

Compiled from peer-reviewed literature (2025-2026) and market intelligence sources
(Frontiers, Nature Communications, Dialectica, Senken, CoastalWiki, Verra, World Bank,
Systemiq, Ørsted). All figures reflect best available public data as of early 2026.

---

## Section 1: Ecological Sequestration Rates

These rates establish the physical baseline for carbon accounting. They are necessary
for calculating credit yield per hectare of project area.

| Ecosystem | Sequestration Rate | Carbon Stock | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mangrove (global mean) | 174 g C m⁻² yr⁻¹ (range 95-235) | up to 1,745 Mg C ha⁻¹ | Anthropocene synthesis 2026, CoastalWiki |
| Mangrove (broader range) | 0.6-9 t C ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹ regional variability | -- | Multiple meta-analyses |
| Seagrass (sequestration) | 160-221 g C m⁻² yr⁻¹ | 1,551 ± 40 kg C ha⁻¹ biomass | Nature Comms 2025 |
| Seagrass (burial only) | ~20% of NPP = ~1.3 t C ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹ | soil Corg up to 5.25 Mg C ha⁻¹ | Nature Comms 2025 |
| Salt marsh | 150-250 g C m⁻² yr⁻¹ | -- | Springer Discover Oceans 2026 |
| Global blue carbon total | ~80 Tg C yr⁻¹ | coastal wetlands worldwide | CoastalWiki |

Key implications for cTVF:

- Pacific and Southeast Asia have the highest mangrove carbon densities. This matters for project geography selection.
- Regional variability spans a factor of 10-100. Site-specific measurement is not optional. Global averages cannot replace a project baseline.
- Mangrove restoration can recover 50-90% of depleted carbon stocks over several decades.
- Seagrass NPP (6,696 kg C ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹) is comparable to tropical rainforests, but biomass stocks are much lower than mangroves.

## Section 2: Voluntary Carbon Market Prices for Blue Carbon Credits

These prices inform the revenue side of the value chain and the financial-mechanism
design. They are not a substitute for site-level carbon yield estimates.

| Metric | Value | Date / Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mangrove restoration VCM average | $26.03/tCO₂e | 2023, Frontiers 2025 |
| Mangrove restoration VCM recent | $32/tCO₂e | 2024, OPIS cited in Frontiers |
| Platts DBC-1 benchmark (record high) | $29.30/tCO₂e | August 28, 2025 |
| Platts DBC-1 assessed average | $25.25/tCO₂e | December 2024 |
| Delta Blue Carbon (Pakistan) auction | $27.80/tCO₂e | November 2022, CIX |
| All VCM credits average | $6.63/tCO₂e | 2023 |
| All forestry and land-use average | $9.91/tCO₂e | 2023 |
| Removal credits premium | ~381% higher than reductions | 2024 |
| Recent-vintage premium | ~217% additional | 2024 |

Market supply context (as of March 2025):

- 81 active blue carbon projects globally
- Total area: approximately 2.0 million hectares
- Annual issuance: approximately 20.4 MtCO₂e
- Total credits issued since 2014: approximately 6.96 million
- Total credits retired since 2014: approximately 3.65 million
- Share of total VCM issuances: approximately 0.35%
- Number of actively issuing projects: approximately 10

The supply constraint is structural. Blue carbon represents well under 1% of voluntary
carbon market issuance despite premium pricing. This means corporate demand is far
outstripping project pipeline development.

Financial viability assessment (Frontiers 2025 cost modeling at $30/tCO₂e):

- Only 12% of modeled projects could recover full costs at $30/tCO₂e
- Financially viable projects were all mangrove initiatives, 80% conservation type
- Modeled cost ranges per tCO₂e: mangrove $12-270, tidal marsh $400-1,300, seagrass $2,400
- Conclusion: carbon revenue alone is typically insufficient. Blended capital or co-benefit revenues are required for most projects.

## Section 3: MRV Standards and Verification Framework

This section directly addresses the MRV and verification gap identified in the cTVF
gap analysis.

### Dominant Standard

**Verra VCS (Verified Carbon Standard)** accounts for approximately 99% of all blue
carbon issuances to date, predominantly mangrove projects.

### Active Methodologies

| Methodology | Scope | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| VM0033 v2.0 (revision in progress) | Tidal wetland and seagrass restoration AND conservation | Draft Step 3, Silvestrum Climate Associates developing |
| VM0007 REDD+ MF | Tidal wetland conservation (will be inactivated post-VM0033 v2.0 release) | Active with grace period |
| Gold Standard | Mangrove methodology (2024 launch) | Active |
| American Carbon Registry | Coastal wetland methods | Active |
| Plan Vivo | Community-led projects | Active |
| Australia ACCU Scheme | Tidal restoration | Active |

Key VM0033 v2.0 updates under development:

- New procedures to quantify leakage
- Updated methods to establish project baselines
- Revised procedures for quantifying soil organic carbon
- Revised additionality assessment
- Expanded coverage to include conservation activities (previously only restoration)

ICVCM Core Carbon Principles (CCP) guidance was updated in September 2025. Projects
with both registry certification and independent third-party CCP ratings are now
considered the highest-integrity supply pathway.

### MRV Technology Options

Per Springer 2026 review:

- Isotopic methods: high precision, high cost, limited scalability
- UAV remote sensing: good spatial resolution, moderate cost
- Satellite remote sensing: scalable, lower cost, lower resolution
- Machine learning: promising for automated monitoring
- Eddy covariance: gold-standard flux measurement, high infrastructure cost
- Biomass sampling: required for baseline, labor-intensive

Key uncertainties in MRV: methane (CH₄) and nitrous oxide (N₂O) emissions from
wetland soils can partially offset sequestration benefits. These must be accounted
for in net GHG calculations.

## Section 4: Blue Bond and Blended Finance Benchmarks

These examples directly inform the financial mechanism and coupon structure sections
of the cTVF gap analysis.

### Blue Bond Market Overview (Systemiq 2024)

- Total market since 2018: approximately $8.3 billion across 41 issuances
- Share of sustainable bond market: less than 0.2% of total GSS+ issuance in 2023
- Growth rate: doubled from 2022 to 2023
- Regional leader: APAC accounts for over 60% of global issuances by value
- Common structures: Use-of-Proceeds (UOP) bonds, Sustainability-Linked Bonds (SLBs), Debt-for-Nature Swaps (DFNSs)

Most common SLB structure: if the issuer fails to achieve targets, a coupon step-up
penalty applies. This is the standard structural penalty mechanism in current practice.

### Benchmark Transactions

| Issuer | Year | Size | Structure | Coupon | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Seychelles (sovereign) | 2018 | $15M | Use-of-proceeds, 10-year | 6.5% fixed | World Bank $5M guarantee, $5M concessional loan for interest support |
| Ørsted (corporate) | 2023 | EUR 100M | Use-of-proceeds, private placement | 3.625% fixed | First energy company blue bond; matures 2028 |
| Indonesia Coral Bond (World Bank) | 2024-2025 | $10M coupon channeled | Outcome-based financing | Coupon partly to conservation; success payments triggered by KPI | First outcome-based marine bond; KPI is coral reef biodiversity indicator (IUCN Green List) |

### Indonesia Coral Bond Detail (World Bank 2025)

This is the most relevant live precedent for an outcome-linked blue carbon instrument:

- Modeled after the Rhino Wildlife Conservation Bond
- Issued by World Bank with GEF and Government of Indonesia collaboration
- Approximately $10M of coupon payments channeled to conservation activities
- $13.76M from GEF non-grant instrument and $1.1M from BNP Paribas held in reserve
- Conservation Success Payments triggered and sized based on level of KPI achievement
- KPI: selected biodiversity indicator related to coral reef health (IUCN Green List certification)
- Three MPAs in Eastern Indonesia: Raja Ampat (2.2M ha total area)
- Monitoring: EVIKA national scorecard plus IUCN Green List process

### Coupon Structure Summary from Market Practice

| Type | When Applicable | Risk Level |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fixed coupon (use-of-proceeds) | Clear project plan, no KPI track record | Low (investor) |
| Blended / concessional tranche | Early-stage projects lacking revenue certainty | Low (project) |
| Guarantee-enhanced | Development bank or guarantor participates (as in Seychelles, Coral Bond) | Low (investor) |
| Step-up coupon | SLB with penalty for missing targets | Medium, requires defined KPIs |
| Outcome-linked | Success payments tied to verified KPI achievement (as in Coral Bond) | High, requires strong MRV |
| Step-down coupon | Reward for exceeding targets | High, requires strong MRV |

The market evidence confirms the cTVF analysis: outcome-linked and step-down coupons
require strong MRV, verified KPIs, and a track record. Fixed or blended structures are
appropriate for early-stage projects.

## Section 5: Policy and Market Access Context

### Article 6 Pathways

Global carbon pricing mechanisms mobilized over $100 billion in 2024. The Paris
Agreement Article 6 framework, if implemented, would open blue carbon projects to
compliance markets, substantially increasing demand and price.

### National Integration

Taiwan approved two blue carbon methodologies in July 2025 (mangrove afforestation
and seagrass restoration), signaling new domestic supply channels. Southeast Asia,
Africa, and Latin America are integrating coastal ecosystem protection into NDCs.

### Mangrove Breakthrough Initiative

Announced financing propositions with a goal to mobilize $4 billion by 2030 to
secure over 15 million hectares of mangroves globally.

### Blue Carbon Action Partnership (WEF, April 2026)

Operating in Indonesia, Philippines, and Vietnam through national blue carbon action
partnerships (NBCAPs). Focused on strengthening enabling conditions, improving
project readiness, and unlocking finance at scale. Targets early-stage ventures
that are not yet commercially investable.

## Section 6: Key Findings for cTVF Application

The quantitative data above directly upgrades the gap analysis in the following ways:

1. **Carbon yield baselines now have public ranges.** Any Blue Carbon project can be benchmarked against 174 g C m⁻² yr⁻¹ for mangroves and 160-221 g C m⁻² yr⁻¹ for seagrass. Site-specific measurement is still required, but the ranges provide planning-level bounds.

2. **Revenue yield can be estimated in ranges.** At $26-32/tCO₂e for mangrove credits and 600-770 tonnes CO₂/yr per 100 ha (Coastal Wiki example), a 100 ha restoration project generates approximately $15,600-$24,600/yr from carbon credits alone at current voluntary market prices. This is typically insufficient to cover project costs without blended capital.

3. **The MRV standard is clear: Verra VM0033.** A project developer does not need to design an MRV methodology from scratch. The gap is project-level baseline measurement, third-party verifier selection, and VVB engagement.

4. **Coupon structures have live precedents.** The Seychelles, Ørsted, and Indonesia Coral Bond provide concrete design anchors for the financial mechanism layer.

5. **Blended capital is structurally necessary, not optional.** The Frontiers cost modeling confirms that carbon revenue alone covers full project costs for only 12% of projects. This supports the cTVF recommendation for a blended or concessional structure at early stage.
