# Blue Finance 3Cs And Blended Finance Source Note

## Source Context

This note extracts finance, ecosystem-services, and investor-readiness information from a LinkedIn article by Ben Tovell for Blue Economy Pulse, published on 2026-04-23. The article frames blue finance through Character, Capacity, Collateral, and a fourth top-down element: Consistency or Certainty.

## Extracted Information

- Blue finance is defined as financial instruments, policies, and investment strategies that support sustainable use, conservation, and restoration of ocean and coastal ecosystems.
- The article frames blue finance as relevant to Global South Blue Economy development.
- The core investor-readiness principles are Character, Capacity, and Collateral.
- A fourth C, Consistency, is described as a top-down condition that creates investment Certainty.
- Character is described as the most important principle, covering ability and willingness to perform and repay funding.
- Character is tested by credit history, deal execution history, preparation for investor discussions, and clarity in answering investor questions.
- Capacity refers to technical feasibility or economic viability of a project to generate returns.
- Capacity includes identifying the financing need before investor engagement, including loan tenor, amount, and investment type.
- Collateral is treated as secondary to Character and Capacity because investors first assess whether repayment expectations are strong across scenarios.
- Blue finance responds to climate change, biodiversity loss, and pollution in ocean and coastal systems.
- Healthy oceans are framed as foundations for climate regulation, food security, and economic stability.
- UNESCO-aligned blue finance should be integrated into marine conservation and sustainable development programs rather than treated as isolated project funding.
- Blue finance is described as emerging, with uncertainty and confirmation bias contributing to investor hesitation.
- A weak track record contributes to a lack of investible pipelines.
- Capacity building is described as necessary for building capacity, track record, and character.
- Blended finance is described as transitional architecture that de-risks untested projects, business models, and revenue streams.
- Cost reduction pathways include standardized terms, standardized documentation, standardized credit and project diligence, digitalization, digital investment platforms, project aggregation, and AI-supported measurement.
- AI and technology examples include measuring above-ground and below-ground biomass to calculate carbon stock changes in mangroves.
- Emerging revenue sources include ecosystem services, natural capital benefits, adaptation value, resilience value, blue carbon credits, sustainable fisheries, eco-tourism, fishing fees, and diving fees.
- The article supports a landscape approach to blue carbon, combining mangroves, seagrass, coral reefs, and connected social, cultural, and economic systems.
- Mangrove interventions may produce nursery fish benefits and symbiotic revenue streams.
- Natural capital co-benefits include property flood protection, reduced pollution, enhanced fisheries, and increased tourism.
- Adaptation and resilience valuation can include avoided property losses, reduced CAPEX, reduced OPEX, and lower long-term climate risk.
- National climate strategies, nationally determined contributions, and national adaptation plans are named as possible policy integration points.
- Monetization of new revenue sources depends on consistent frameworks and standards, government ocean planning, legislation, policy, governance, credible science, transparent science, and partnerships.
- Coastal community capability needs include livelihood diversification, blue finance instruments, carbon-credit methodology eligibility, government pipeline development, financial statement training, investor-compliant materials, currency hedging, and guarantees.
- Local coastal and Indigenous communities are presented as central to credible ocean investment because project durability depends on wellbeing, rights, economic resilience, stewardship, and community buy-in.
- The article argues that coastal communities often have stewardship skills but lack market access.
- Strengthening market access and regional partnerships is framed as a major enabler of scalable blue finance.

## Relevance To Clarity TVF

This source supports the Clarity concern that investibility depends on more than monetized externalities. Character, Capacity, Collateral, and Consistency can be mapped as system prerequisites, while blue ecosystem-service revenues require careful separation between ecological function, value-chain interpretation, and financial-abstraction mechanisms.

## Handling Notes

Keep blue finance in the financial-abstraction layer unless the question explicitly asks for ecosystem-service phase mapping. When modeling physical or ecological systems, separate mangrove, fisheries, carbon, market-access, and financing mechanisms into explicit layers.
