🫒 Project Context: Spanish Olive Oil Supply Chain

Spain: world's largest producer (~51% global output), world's largest exporter (742,500t in 2023/24), and world's largest olive oil storage operator (Dcoop: 300,000MT). Yet Spain captures only EUR 3.22/kg average export price vs Italy's EUR 4.42/kg, because ~67% of exports leave as bulk.

Supply Chain Phases

Transparency ratings: High = institutional custody documentation, regulatory filings, standardised reporting. Medium = partial documentation, cooperative or state oversight. Low = no chain-of-custody documents, no standardised process at phase level.
Balance reference: π/4 = 78.5% (phases below this are operational bottlenecks by the Stage 1 standard). PHI zones: Coherent ≥ 61.8%, Failure 38.2–61.8%, Breakdown < 38.2%. All Olive Oil phases clear the π/4 reference. Phase 5 (81.1%) is the structural weak point; e-Trade Structure (62.0%) is borderline Stage 2 Coherent.

High-End Benchmark: Las Valdesas (Andalucia)

Aceite de Las Valdesas is a single-estate premium olive oil from Andalucia that represents a direct-to-consumer, short-chain alternative to the conventional export model. Key differentiators relevant to cTVF analysis:

CharacteristicLas ValdesasConventional Andalucian Export
Supply chain lengthEstate to consumer, 2-3 phasesFarm to retail, 8 phases
Delivery time~10 days direct from bodegaWeeks to months via intermediary
PackagingCardboard only, no foam or plastic insertStandard protective packaging
Provenance transparencySingle estate, fully traceableOften blended, PDO at region level
Phase 5 (bottling) controlOn-estate — no third-party bottler1,500 independent bottlers
Brand premium captureFull (retained by producer)Foregone (captured by Italian importers)
cTVF relevanceHigh: compresses chain by 5+ phases, eliminates Phase 5 bottleneck, eliminates bulk-export opacityBaseline against which benchmark premium is measured

This benchmark should be used as the high-end reference node in Phase 5 bond analysis: demonstrating the maximum attainable brand premium when a producer controls the full value chain from grove to consumer.

Phase Intervention Worksheet

PROVISIONAL: Recommendations are derived analytically from PDI phase-level N-D-C data and public sources. Field validation is required before capital commitment. Each intervention is grounded in the specific mathematical axis that identifies the type of failure.

How the Five-Model Constants Diagnose Failure: Spanish Olive Oil Supply Chain

Each coherence axis (pi, phi, sqrt2, ln2, e) measures a different dimension of systemic health. The N-D-C balance formula is balance = 100 - (|D - C| / max(D, C)) * 100. A failing phase has D significantly greater than C: the constraint load exceeds contribution capacity. Chain average balance: 89.1%.

ConstantChain ScoreWhat It Measures If Below 80: The System Has...Intervention Category
pi (π) 89.1 Operational balance: chain average across all phases Phases pulling the aggregate below the sustainable threshold Process improvement; technology adoption at the phase level
phi (φ) 80 Proportional value distribution: each phase receives value proportional to contribution Value trapped or lost at phase transitions Market structure reform; cooperative integration; direct-to-consumer models
sqrt2 (√2) 86 Structural overhead: physical or institutional friction raising D without adding C Physical or regulatory constraints are the binding factor Infrastructure investment; mechanisation; governance reform
ln2 82 Value transformation: how effectively each phase converts inputs to higher-value outputs Transformation blocked by a gap (time lag, capital absence, biological delay) Patient capital instruments; multi-cropping; phased transition
e 75 Financial abstraction: how well financial instruments are linked to real performance metrics Available funding is not operationally connected to the ecosystem Bond instrument design; KPI linkage; governance pre-conditions for finance activation

Phases 5 (81.1%) and 7 (85.5%) are both below the chain pi score (89.1%). phi (80) flags value distribution failure: Spain produces 51% of global olive oil but two-thirds of exports leave as undifferentiated bulk at EUR 3.22/kg vs Italy's EUR 4.42/kg for rebranded product. This EUR 1.20/kg gap represents ~EUR 890M/year in foregone revenue. ln2 (82) notes the bottling/branding transformation gap. e (75) reflects that EU CAP instruments exist but are not linked to origin differentiation KPIs.
