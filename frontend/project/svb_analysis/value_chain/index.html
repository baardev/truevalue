Scope of this page

This page addresses the forward-looking application of the SVB structural analysis to peer bank screening, post-failure regulatory reform tracking, and the general framework for applying N-D-C analysis to financial institutions using public data. Full value chain financial modelling (revenue attribution, margin analysis, fee income decomposition) is deferred pending additional data sourcing.

Post-SVB Regulatory Reform Scorecard (Status: June 2026)

The Federal Reserve and FDIC identified specific supervisory and regulatory gaps in their post-failure reviews. The table below tracks each proposed reform against the N-D-C phase it addresses and its implementation status as of mid-2026.

Reform N-D-C phase addressed What it fixes Status (June 2026)
Enhanced LCR for $100B+ banks Phase 4: Liquidity Restores binding liquidity constraint removed by 2019 tailoring. Extends LCR from $250B+ threshold to $100B+. Proposed 2023. Finalized partially 2024. Not fully enacted for $50-100B tier.
AOCI inclusion in capital for $100B+ banks Phase 1: Capital (Deception Fix) Forces regulatory capital to reflect mark-to-market losses. Eliminates the Phase 1 deceptive signal pattern. Finalized 2024. Applies to banks with $100B+ in assets. Multi-year phase-in.
Enhanced stress testing for $100B+ banks Phase 3: Interest Rate Risk Requires annual stress tests including interest rate shock scenarios for mid-tier banks. Proposed. Implementation timeline under review as of 2026.
Enhanced supervisory escalation framework Phase 7: Regulatory Compliance Addresses the Fed's finding that supervisory concerns were identified but not escalated to formal enforcement. Requires faster escalation timelines. Internal Fed guidance updated. No formal rulemaking.
Uninsured deposit concentration reporting Phase 0: Deposit Quality Enhanced reporting of uninsured deposit concentration and sector concentration in Call Reports. Proposed. Not yet finalized.
Long-term debt requirement for $100B+ banks Phase 6: Market Confidence Requires sufficient long-term debt to absorb losses in resolution without systemic risk exception, reducing contagion risk. Proposed 2023. Final rule issued 2024 for $100B+ banks.

N-D-C assessment of reform completeness: The two most critical gap-closers (AOCI inclusion for Phase 1, LCR extension for Phase 4) have been partially enacted. The Phase 3 gap (mandatory hedging coverage, HTM concentration limits) has not been addressed by any enacted rule as of June 2026. A bank replicating SVB's Phase 3 imbalance today would still not face a binding regulatory constraint on that specific failure mode.

Banks with similar structural profiles (as of available 2024 data)

The N-D-C screening framework can be run against the full FDIC universe. The following characteristics identify institutions with structural similarity to SVB's Q4 2022 profile. Any institution meeting three or more criteria warrants a full phase-level structural analysis:

  • Assets $50-250B (mid-tier, historically exempt from full LCR)
  • Uninsured deposits above 60% of total deposits
  • HTM securities above 30% of total assets
  • Loan-to-deposit ratio below 55%
  • NIM declining for two or more consecutive quarters in a rising rate environment

Note: This page does not name specific current institutions as peers. The screening criteria are provided for the user to run against current FDIC data. FDIC BankFind returns all qualifying institutions when the field filters above are applied.

Five-Model Value Context — Implications for Financial Institutions

Axis SVB Score What this means for a financial institution Value chain implication
π (Operational) 61.3% Institution is at the coherence boundary. Four of eight operational phases are in failure or breakdown. The bank is functionally under stress across its core activities. Revenue model is structurally compromised. NIM compression, deposit outflows, and illiquid assets are converging.
φ (Distribution) 38% Value is concentrated in compliance and reporting phases, not in operational phases. The institution presents as healthy while it is structurally failing. This is the deception pattern. Book value overstates franchise value. Earnings multiples based on reported metrics will be misleading for any analyst using only regulatory capital ratios.
√2 (Structural) 30% Constraint infrastructure is absent. The institution removed the mechanisms (hedges, concentration limits) that previously provided structural D weight. The failure is one of under-governance, not over-governance. Risk-adjusted returns are not adjusted: the "high" NIM achieved by removing hedges is not compensation for risk, it is compensation for concealed structural deterioration.
ln2 (Transformation) 45% Capital transformation is partially blocked. Deposits cannot be converted to liquidity without catastrophic loss events. The institution's core function — intermediating between depositors and borrowers — is structurally impaired. The institution's business model is no longer generating value through transformation. It is generating reported earnings by deferring loss recognition.
e (Financial Abstraction) 12% The institution has no meaningful financial instruments linking its risk exposure to its capital position. This is the most extreme score in the dataset. Hedges were removed. The capital structure does not reflect the risk the institution is carrying. Bond pricing and equity valuation based on standard metrics will be structurally wrong. The e axis score of 12% is a direct signal to investors and counterparties that the institution's financial instruments have decoupled from its real risk profile.
