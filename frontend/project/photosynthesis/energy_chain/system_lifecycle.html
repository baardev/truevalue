Lifecycle bottleneck: Phase 3 (Sustain and Assure). Balance 90.4%. D = 260, C = 235. The D excess reflects the ongoing demand of the repair apparatus: D1 protein in PSII is the most rapidly turned-over protein in the plant proteome under high light. When photodamage rate exceeds D1 synthesis and insertion rate, progressive photoinhibition accumulates. This is the lifecycle equivalent of infrastructure maintenance debt: the system performs at full capacity only when repair demand (D) is matched by repair capacity (C).

Structural Interpretation

The photosynthesis system lifecycle shows a characteristic pattern: high operational coherence during development and operation (Phases 1-2), with a declining balance in the sustaining phase (Phase 3). This mirrors the pattern seen in the gold supply chain lifecycle, where the sustaining and closure phases are consistently the weakest.


In biological terms, the sustaining bottleneck has a precise molecular basis: the D1 protein of PSII is the target of reactive oxygen species generated by PSII itself during charge separation. D1 must be removed from the PSII complex, degraded by FtsH proteases, and replaced by newly synthesized D1 within the same complex. This entire cycle occurs in minutes under high light and represents a continuous maintenance cost. When light exceeds repair capacity, PSII centers become inactivated (photoinhibition) and the chain efficiency declines from its optimum.


The tholonic implication: Phase 3 (Sustain) has a D-C imbalance where D (repair demand) > C (repair delivery). Interventions that increase C — for example, overexpression of FtsH protease or D1 synthesis rate — can in principle recover the lifecycle balance. This has been demonstrated experimentally in transgenic plants with elevated FtsH levels.
