Photon Budget Waterfall — 100 Incident Photons

Tracking how incident PAR is transformed, lost, or converted to net carbon at each phase. Values are representative for C3 leaf at saturation, 25°C, 380 ppm CO2.

Theoretical Maximum Efficiency (11%)

Theoretical maximum is ~11% of incident solar radiation converted to chemical energy (glucose). This assumes perfect quantum yield at all phases, no photorespiration (0% O2), minimal thermodynamic losses, and no dark respiration. In practice, this is never achievable in a C3 plant under ambient conditions.

Key thermodynamic losses are unavoidable: the red-drop (photons beyond ~680 nm have insufficient energy for water splitting), fluorescence from PSII (~2%), and heat dissipation in the antenna (~5%). These alone reduce max theoretical efficiency to ~11%.

Actual C3 Efficiency (~6%)

~6% incident solar → fixed carbon

The gap between 11% theoretical and ~6% actual is primarily caused by: (1) photorespiration at Phase 5 consuming ~25% of fixed carbon, (2) non-photochemical quenching (NPQ) dissipating excess light as heat at Phase 0, (3) the ATP/NADPH mismatch requiring cyclic electron flow (additional photons per CO2), and (4) the CO2 diffusion limitation through stomata and mesophyll.

C4 Efficiency Advantage

C4 plants achieve ~8-9% efficiency under high temperature because: (1) the CO2-concentrating mechanism suppresses photorespiration (Vo/Vc ~0.02 vs 0.25 in C3), recovering ~20% of the lost photons; (2) Rubisco operates at near-saturating CO2 inside bundle sheath cells. Under cool conditions (<20°C), C4 efficiency advantage is reduced because the CO2-concentrating mechanism itself costs ATP.

Phase 5: The 11% vs 6% Gap

The entire gap between theoretical (11%) and actual (6%) efficiency is essentially attributable to Phase 5 and its downstream consequences. If photorespiration were eliminated (as in C4), actual C3 efficiency would approach ~8-9%. The N-D-C balance score of 85.2% at Phase 5 directly reflects this energetic cost: D exceeds C by 40 index points, corresponding to ~25% of gross carbon fixation being consumed by the oxygenation pathway.

The √2 model measures whether what is structurally required at each boundary (D) matches what is structurally delivered (C). A perfectly symmetric boundary has D/C = 1.0 (= √1). The natural overhead of any boundary crossing approaches √2 (1.414) as a minimum cost.
