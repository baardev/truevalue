System Overview

This project applies the Tholonic N-D-C framework to canonical C3 photosynthesis, treating the photosynthetic energy flow as a phase-resolved physical chain analogous to the gold supply chain. The reference organisms are Spinacia oleracea (spinach) and Arabidopsis thaliana, which represent well-characterized C3 systems with extensive published data.

The chain runs from photon absorption at the antenna complex (Phase 0) through the light reactions (Phases 1-4), the Calvin cycle (Phases 5-6), and carbon export (Phase 7). All quantitative values are derived from published primary literature under standard conditions: 25°C, 380 ppm CO2, saturating light, and unstressed conditions.

The primary analytical finding is that Phases 0-4 and 6-7 are coherent (balance 94-98%), while Phase 5 (Rubisco carbon fixation) is in the stressed zone (balance 85.2%) due to the structural constraint of photorespiration. This is not a failure of the system; it is a well-understood evolutionary constraint reflecting the ancient origin of Rubisco in an O2-free atmosphere.

Tholonic N-D-C Framework Application

The Tholonic framework maps each phase to a triadic structure of Negotiation (N), Definition (D), and Contribution (C). The photosynthetic chain is well-suited to this analysis because every phase has precisely measurable inputs (D-type constraints) and outputs (C-type contributions).

Example: Phase 1 (Photosystem II)

N: Negotiation (emergent state)
Functional PSII complex operating at maximum quantum yield. N = 241 (balance 96.1%). The stable, coherent instantiation at this phase level.
D: Definition (constraints)
Excitation threshold ~1.82 eV; Mn4CaO5 cluster geometry and stoichiometry; QB plastoquinone binding site; tyrosine Z intermediate requirements; D1 protein structural integrity; pH gradient requirement for water oxidation.
C: Contribution (outputs)
O2 evolution (4 electrons per O2); 2 plastoquinol per 4 photons; 4 H+ released into thylakoid lumen per 2 H2O split; excitation passed to electron transport chain.

D-C Balance Formula (used across all phases)

balance = 100 - (|D - C| / max(D, C)) × 100
N = sqrt(D × C) × (balance / 100)
sustainability = C / D (ratio; 1.0 = perfect balance)

Why Phase 5 is in the Stressed Zone

Phase 5 has D = 270 and C = 230, giving a balance of 85.2%. The D excess reflects the structural constraint of Rubisco's bifunctionality: the enzyme is constrained by both the carboxylation requirement (positive C contribution) and the oxygenation reaction (which adds to D without contributing to C). The O2 competition constraint is not removable by optimization within the C3 framework; it is a direct consequence of the enzyme's active site geometry.

In tholonic terms: the Parent N of Phase 5 is a productive Rubisco complex fixing CO2. The D constraints include both the legitimate carboxylation requirements and the unavoidable O2 competition requirement. The C outputs are net carbon fixed (reduced by ~25% relative to gross carboxylation). The Child N (the actual operational instantiation) is thus limited by the inherited D imbalance.

How the Five-Model Constants Diagnose Failure: Photosynthesis Energy Chain

Each coherence axis (pi, phi, sqrt2, ln2, e) measures a different dimension of systemic health. The N-D-C balance formula is balance = 100 - (|D - C| / max(D, C)) * 100. A failing phase has D significantly greater than C: the constraint load exceeds contribution capacity. Chain average balance: 95.4%.

5
Rubisco Carbon Fixation and Photorespiration
Balance 85.2% | D=270, C=230, gap=40.0 | Primary failure axis: ln2 and sqrt2 (√2)
Bottleneck

Why ln2 and sqrt2 diagnose this failure: D=270 reflects the constraint load of the Rubisco enzyme system: Rubisco (ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase/oxygenase) is simultaneously the most abundant protein on Earth and the most catalytically inefficient enzyme in the photosynthesis pathway. Its dual specificity for CO2 and O2 means that at current atmospheric CO2:O2 ratios (0.04%:21%), approximately 20-30% of reactions produce 2-phosphoglycolate instead of 3-phosphoglycerate, initiating the photorespiratory bypass that consumes ATP and releases previously fixed CO2. This wasted reaction accounts for the entire D-C imbalance: D=270 (constraint: competitive oxygenation + regulatory overhead of photorespiration) vs C=230 (net carbon fixed per unit Rubisco active site). sqrt2 (81) identifies structural overhead: Rubisco's catalytic rate is physically constrained by its molecular architecture (3 reactions/second vs 1,000+ for most enzymes), raising D through the large quantity of enzyme required. ln2 (65.5) is the most diagnostically significant axis: it identifies the transformation failure as a kinetic gap. The input (atmospheric CO2) is present; the conversion machinery (Rubisco) exists; but the transformation pathway cannot operate at full efficiency because the enzyme confuses substrate identity.
