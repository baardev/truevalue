Mainstream Medical Consensus Validation
The four Stressed-zone phases (3, 5, 6, 7) correspond directly to the processes that mainstream aging medicine identifies as the primary drivers of age-related decline. The table below maps each low-balance phase to its consensus recognition in the field, confirming that the Tholonic N-D-C analysis is in agreement with current scientific understanding. Where the N-D-C model adds to the consensus is in the cascade direction and leverage point: most clinical programs target Phases 6 and 7 (senolytics, anti-inflammatories), while the model identifies Phase 3 as the upstream structural leverage point whose degradation initiates the cascade.

Where the N-D-C model diverges from (and extends) mainstream consensus: The field treats the four Stressed phases as parallel hallmarks of roughly equal clinical status. Most therapeutic programs target Phase 6 (senolytics) and Phase 7 (anti-inflammatories) because their outputs are the most visible. The cascade structure in this model predicts that Phase 3 is the upstream leverage point: proteasome and autophagy decline feeds Phase 5 epigenetic drift (misfolded enzymes degrade chromatin maintenance), which accelerates Phase 6 senescence (epigenetically dysregulated cells hit their division limit earlier). This cascade prediction is consistent with the observation that rapamycin (a Phase 3 intervention) produces larger lifespan extension in mice than any senolytic (Phase 6 intervention) tested to date in the Interventions Testing Program. The model does not contradict the Hallmarks framework: it sequences and weights it.
