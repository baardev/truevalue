Scope for Value Chain Layer

When the physical supply chain mapping is complete, the value chain layer will address:

  • Economic cost of aging: Global healthcare expenditure attributable to age-related disease (estimated $4.1T annually); productivity loss from frailty and cognitive decline; longevity therapeutic market scale ($30B+ and growing rapidly)
  • Market structure per phase: Which phases have active commercial intervention markets (Phase 3: rapamycin, spermidine, urolithin A; Phase 6: D+Q senolytics, fisetin); where value is captured vs. where it flows to patients
  • Value distribution analysis: How much economic value accrues at each intervention phase; where the Phase 3 / Phase 6 bottlenecks create rent-capture opportunities for correctly positioned therapeutic products
  • Financial abstraction layer (e-axis): Clinical trial funding structures; longevity bond instruments; health outcome-linked finance; insurance risk pools indexed to biological age rather than chronological age
  • What-if simulator: How does improving Phase 3 balance from 79% to 89% translate to extended healthspan, reduced healthcare cost, and market value at the population level
  • Evidence-tier cost modeling: T1 interventions (caloric restriction, exercise) have near-zero marginal cost; T3–T4 interventions (spermidine, urolithin A) have moderate supplement-market cost; T3-pipeline (rapamycin, CAR-T) have pharmaceutical cost structures that differ by 3–4 orders of magnitude
