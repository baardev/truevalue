Manufacturing Stage Concentration

The solar PV manufacturing chain runs through five discrete production stages before reaching installation. China's share exceeds 80% at every stage, a level of geographic concentration that has no parallel among major globally traded manufactured goods. China invested more than USD 50 billion in new PV supply capacity since 2011, roughly ten times more than Europe, creating more than 300,000 manufacturing jobs across the solar PV value chain.

Policy and Trade Instrument Landscape

Multiple governments have introduced instruments intended to diversify manufacturing capacity away from the current geographic concentration, and to restrict imports linked to forced labor. None of these instruments are yet formally linked to a phase-level physical diversification metric, which is the structural basis for the e-axis (financial abstraction) score of 55.0.

Phase Intervention Worksheet

PROVISIONAL: Recommendations are derived analytically from PDI N-D-C data and public sources. Field validation is required before capital commitment. Each intervention is grounded in the specific mathematical axis that identifies the type of failure.

How the Five-Model Constants Diagnose Failure

Each of the five coherence axes (pi, phi, sqrt2, ln2, e) measures a different dimension of systemic health. When a phase has a low balance score, the five-model analysis identifies which type of failure is driving it. This determines what category of intervention can actually close the D-C gap, rather than applying generic solutions.

The N-D-C balance formula is: balance = 100 - (|D - C| / max(D, C)) * 100. A failing phase has D significantly greater than C: the constraint load (D) exceeds the system's contribution capacity (C). Each constant axis explains a different reason why D is elevated or C is suppressed.

Four of the five axes (pi, phi, sqrt2, e) are below 80%, and phi (60.0) sits closest to the 61.8% Failure boundary. Phi and sqrt2 converge on the same two bottleneck phases: 1 and 0.

1
Ingot and Wafer Manufacturing
Balance 65.0% | D=300, C=195, gap=105 | Primary failure axes: phi (φ) and sqrt2 (√2)
Bottleneck

Why phi and sqrt2 diagnose this failure: The phi axis measures whether value is distributed proportionally to contribution. China holds approximately 95-98% of global ingot and wafer capacity while accounting for only 36% of global PV demand, the single sharpest value-concentration gap of any stage in the chain. The sqrt2 axis measures structural overhead: one out of every seven solar panels produced worldwide comes from a single facility, an extreme single-point-of-failure concentration that raises D (supply-risk exposure, geopolitical constraint load) without adding any offsetting C. The combination has pushed D to 300, the second-highest in the chain, while C (diversified capacity, alternative supply routes) remains at only 195. This is a structural and market-concentration problem, not a technology problem: the wafer-sawing and ingot-pulling process itself is mature and well understood outside China.

0
Polysilicon Production
Balance 67.7% | D=310, C=210, gap=100 | Primary failure axis: sqrt2 (√2), secondary phi (φ)
Bottleneck

Why sqrt2 diagnoses this failure: Phase 0 carries the highest D-value in the entire chain (D=310). The sqrt2 axis measures structural overhead: friction that raises D without adding to C. Here the structural overhead is compliance cost and sourcing opacity. Xinjiang province alone accounts for approximately 40% of global polysilicon manufacturing capacity, and UFLPA (US Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act) import restrictions targeting Xinjiang-sourced material since 2021-2022 have created a rebuttable presumption of forced labor that importers must actively rebut with documentation most facilities cannot supply. This raises D (compliance burden, legal exposure, customs delay) without directly adding C (verified, freely tradable polysilicon supply). The phi axis reinforces the same finding at the chain level: value concentrated in a jurisdiction with the least third-party verification access in the chain.

Combined effect: traceability systems plus IRA 45X diversification plus public disclosure would bring Phase 0 from 67.7% to an estimated 82-85%, above the chain pi score (79.0) and into the Good zone (80-94%). At that point Phase 5 (73.1%) becomes the chain's weakest phase, which is addressable through take-back policy rather than trade or capacity investment.

Phase 5 (End-of-Life and Recycling, 73.1%): Secondary Priority

Phase 5 is in the stressed zone (73.1%, below the chain pi of 79.0) and is signalled primarily by the sqrt2 axis (66.0): the absence of mandated take-back infrastructure in most markets is a pure structural overhead problem, not a technology gap. Under 10% of retired panels are formally recycled through mandated take-back or recycling infrastructure in most jurisdictions (PROVISIONAL estimate). The recovery technology for panel glass, silicon, and silver already exists; what is missing is the collection mandate that would route retired panels to it.

The one direct Phase 5 intervention that does not depend on Phases 0 or 1: extension of the EU WEEE Directive framework, or an equivalent mandated take-back scheme, specifically to solar PV modules in markets that currently lack one. This raises C directly (formal collection volume) without requiring any change to upstream manufacturing concentration.
