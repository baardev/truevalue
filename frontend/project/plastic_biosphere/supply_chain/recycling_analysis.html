TVPCI, TVPCI-R, and B_chain: The Three Return Scores

TVPCI (Primary Chain)
The D role in the holon: the primary plastic dispersal chain score measuring how well each phase manages the physical flow of plastic through the environment. Derived as the phase-average N-D-C balance. A high TVPCI means the dispersal chain is well-monitored and partially correctable; a low score means the chain is opaque and uncontrolled.
69.2
TVPCI-R (Return Chain)
The C role in the holon: the ecological return chain score measuring how well each phase generates corrective mechanisms — bacterial degradation, human cleanup, upstream reduction — relative to what would be needed to restore balance. TVPCI-R = sum of (omega_p × R_p) across all phases. All R_p scores are PROVISIONAL.
33.3
B_chain (Holon Balance)
The N role in the holon: the emergent balance between primary chain and return chain. B_chain = 100 × exp(-2 × |TVPCI - TVPCI_R| / max(TVPCI, TVPCI_R)). A score of 35.5 is in the Breakdown zone (below 38.2%), indicating severe misalignment: the corrective capacity is less than half of what the accumulation rate demands.

Theoretical Framework

Bacteria and Humans as a Unified Corrective C

The Core Principle

The tholonic model does not distinguish between biological and human corrective responses in the return chain. Both Ideonella sakaiensis and the human ocean cleanup engineer are products of the same parent N system (the biosphere) and operate under the same N-D-C sustainability constraints. When the parent N is stressed by a massive externally-imposed C overload, it generates corrective C through every mechanism available: biological evolution, human technology, and human policy simultaneously. These are additive components of a single corrective capacity, not separate competing tracks.

This framing has a direct analytical consequence for the TVPCI-R calculation: the return score at each phase includes all corrective mechanisms regardless of whether they are biological or human in origin. The R_p score for Phase 6 (Biological Uptake) includes both the bacterial degradation rate and any human collection effort at that phase. They are summed, weighted by relative scale.

The current TVPCI-R of 33.3 reflects the combined corrective capacity across all mechanisms. The fact that it is far below the primary chain score (69.2) means the combined natural plus human corrective system is generating approximately 48 cents of corrective value for every dollar of accumulation. The race condition between accumulation and correction is currently strongly unfavorable to system recovery.

Three types of corrective C are modeled per phase: (1) natural biological correction (bacterial degradation, photodegradation, biological processing); (2) human technological correction (cleanup operations, barriers, collection technology); and (3) human upstream correction (production reduction, EPR, design standards). All three are included in the R_p scoring.

Failure (PROV.)
Omega weight: 0.05 (low: well-monitored, lowest waste intensity per phase)
EPR legislation covers approximately 35% of global production. Recycled content share is below 10%. Chemical recycling capacity is below 1 Mt/year globally. 1
Use and Fragmentation
28.0
Breakdown (PROV.)
Omega weight: 0.10 (high fragmentation volume; most micro-input generation)
No viable capture mechanism at fragmentation scale. Beach cleanup removes approximately 150,000 tonnes/year globally, less than 2% of the estimated fragmentation input. Tyre filter technologies (PlanetCare, Cora Ball) cover a small fraction of synthetic textile microfibre release. No remediation exists for in-situ fragmentation of littered plastic. This is the lowest R_p phase for which a corrective mechanism category even exists.
2
Terrestrial Dispersal
35.0
Breakdown (PROV.)
Omega weight: 0.12 (very high stock; agricultural exposure; human food chain entry)
Advanced wastewater treatment achieves 95-99% microplastic removal, but removed particles concentrate in biosolid sludge that is then applied to agricultural land, redistributing rather than removing the burden. Soil remediation for microplastics is at the earliest research stage; no scalable technology exists. R_p is slightly above Breakdown because regulatory pressure on biosolid management is increasing in EU and North America.
3
Freshwater Transport
45.0
Failure (PROV.)
Omega weight: 0.10 (key ocean pathway; river barriers are the main deployed technology)
The Ocean Cleanup Interceptor program has deployed 11 river barrier systems globally as of 2024, removing approximately 1,800 tonnes since 2019. This is approximately 0.04-0.4% of annual river input. R_p is the highest among dispersal phases because: river barriers are technically proven, the top 10 rivers produce 88-95% of total input (concentrated target), and investment is scaling. If 1,000 river barrier systems were deployed (vs. 11 today), R_p could plausibly reach 65-70%.
4
Marine Surface Dispersal
52.0
Failure (PROV.)
Omega weight: 0.18 (highest current intervention investment; gyre concentration point)
The Ocean Cleanup System 03 has removed approximately 3,000 tonnes from the North Pacific Gyre (2019-2024). The total ocean surface stock is 268,940 tonnes; at current removal rates, removing the existing stock would take approximately 450 years (ignoring ongoing input). R_p is the highest monitored phase because: technology is deployed, removal is measurable, and the 5 gyres are identifiable targets. Natural photodegradation is active at surface but converts macro to micro rather than removing mass. Investment scaling is the primary R_p lever.
5
Deep-Sea Sediment
12.0
Breakdown (PROV.)
Omega weight: 0.20 (largest stock; deepest failure; highest evolutionary stress concentration)
The lowest R_p phase. No human remediation technology exists for deep-sea microplastic extraction. Natural biological degradation by benthic organisms (sea cucumbers, polychaetes, deep-sea bacteria including Amobacter and Roseovarius) is documented but functions on decade-to-century timescales. R_p is non-zero only because: (1) deep-sea plastisphere communities are confirmed and evolving, representing an emerging corrective biological C; and (2) monitoring programs are beginning to establish baseline data (Kane et al. 2020). This phase carries the highest omega weight because its stock size and zero-correction combination generate the greatest D-C divergence.
6
Biological Uptake
38.0
Breakdown edge (PROV.)
Omega weight: 0.15 (direct ecosystem health impact; plastisphere bacteria most active here)
The most tractable Breakdown-zone phase. Natural biological correction is measurable and developing: Ideonella sakaiensis degrades PET at 0.13 mg/cm2/day; Pseudomonas spp. and Bacillus subtilis are documented on plastic surfaces at depth. R_p of 38.0 (just at the Breakdown threshold) reflects: corrective mechanism exists and is confirmed, but operates at orders of magnitude below accumulation rate. Marine protected area expansion (increasing ecosystem resilience) and bacterial engineering (increasing degradation rate) are the two primary R_p levers at this phase. R_p is the highest of the biological phases because the corrective mechanism is already active.
7
Trophic Transfer
22.0
Breakdown (PROV.)
Omega weight: 0.10 (human health endpoint; human awareness generates highest corrective pressure)
No operational corrective mechanism for in-situ trophic bioaccumulation. Fecal excretion is documented for some particle sizes (Schwabl et al. 2019) but retention fraction and long-term tissue effects are unknown. R_p is non-zero only because: (1) growing human awareness of tissue contamination generates the most powerful corrective pressure of any phase (political and regulatory response); and (2) upstream source reduction is the only available lever, and this phase's human endpoint is the primary political driver for that reduction. Human corrective capacity here is entirely upstream, not in-phase.
