Boundary Structural Symmetry — Chain View
The shea √2 profile is highly distinctive: the early boundaries (collection, first sale, bulking) are under-specified — D is very low (informal market) while C is high (informal delivery). The later boundaries (export, manufacturing) are over-specified — D is high (formal documentation) while C is low (documentation largely OPAQUE). Two entirely different failure modes across the chain.

Two Structural Failure Modes in the Shea Chain

⬆ Under-specified (D < C): P0→P1, P1→P2

The early chain operates with almost no formal structural requirements. Collectors and bulking traders have minimal formal D (no quality specification, no provenance documentation) but deliver physical quantities — high informal C. This is not "healthy" symmetry: it means the first two boundaries are entirely unregulated, creating invisible quality and provenance gaps that cannot be resolved downstream.

⬇ Over-specified (D > C): P3→P4, P4→P5

The export and manufacturing boundaries have extremely high D (phytosanitary, REACH, import quality standards) but low C (documentation is incomplete, often OPAQUE). This is the structural toll-gate failure mode: high formal requirements that small producers cannot meet create an effective export barrier. The structural asymmetry here is the direct mechanism of value concentration in the hands of large exporters and manufacturers.

⚠️ Data Note — Structural Transfer Data for Shea

The shea chain's structural data remains significantly opaque at transfer boundaries. The D and C values are inferred from field reports (RONGEAD, Acorn, EP Carbon), import documentation requirements (EU Cosmetics Regulation, REACH), and known industry practices. P4→P5 (Export → Manufacturing) is the most opaque boundary in the entire chain — the B2B import documentation is private commercial data. The shea_custody_and_flow.csv contains schema rows but all critical fields are marked OPAQUE. Populating this file from supplier contracts, export records, and manufacturer quality agreements would directly raise the structural coherence score.
