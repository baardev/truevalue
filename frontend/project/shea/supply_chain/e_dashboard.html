What the e Score Reveals About Shea

The shea e-model system score of 54 reflects certification gaps: abstract claims about social and environmental practice that are not fully verified at the farmgate. The worst failure point is Fair Trade certification at Phase 1 (score 18): research consistently shows only 10–30% of Fair Trade certified shea volume actually delivers the farmgate premium to collectors. The brand buyer counts 100% certified volume in their sustainability reports while the collector receives the standard market price.

The second major decoupling is sustainability brand claims at Phase 5 (score 42) and consumer labels at Phase 6 (score 45). Companies marketing "sustainable shea" cosmetics rarely have traceability beyond their tier-1 aggregator. The $1.9B in "sustainable shea" branded products is only ~$0.8B verifiable back to farm level. The abstract brand claim floats above a smaller physical foundation of verified practice.

The e model makes the invisible visible: the EU Green Claims Directive (2024) is directly targeting this gap. When the Directive takes effect, brands making unsubstantiated sustainability claims face legal liability. The "expansion" scenario models this: if blockchain traceability and mandatory claim substantiation bring the sustainability and consumer label coupling to 0.70+, the system e-score rises from 54 to ~77. This represents the single largest improvement lever in the shea chain's abstract layer.
