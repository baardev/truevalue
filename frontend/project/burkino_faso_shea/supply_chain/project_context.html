Sector Overview

BAU: Traditional Production

  • ~200 million shea trees across BF; ~5-6 kg dry kernel per tree/yr
  • 6,000+ women per province in informal collection and processing cooperatives
  • Traditional method: 15 kg fuelwood per kg butter; ~25 kgCO2e per kg butter
  • Boiling, manual churning with water; >18 hours/day labour
  • Kernel price to women: 200-300 CFA/kg (USD 0.30-0.46/kg)
  • Butter price (local): 500-1,000 CFA/kg (USD 0.76-1.52/kg)
  • Women's income: ~USD 150-200/season
  • No export documentation; no certifications; no carbon accounting
  • Parkland degradation: fallow loss, tractor tillage, drought aging
  • 80-90% of harvest retained for home consumption; 10-20% marketed

Serious Shea: Eco-Processing Model

  • Incorporated 2018; shareholders: Women's Shea Union 20%, Golden Organics 45%, ImpactAgri 35%
  • Phase 1: 12 collection sites + 3 processing centres; 6,000+ women engaged
  • Full scale: 50 collection sites + 10 eco-processing centres across BF
  • Processing cluster: 5 sites → 1 centre → 3,750t kernels → 1,000t butter/yr
  • Kernel price paid: USD 0.50-0.70/kg (50-100% premium over BAU)
  • Butter output (Phase 1): 3,206 t/yr for international export
  • Women's income: min USD 450/season (2.5x BAU)
  • 10% profit share to community fund (~USD 300k/yr for schools + clinics)
  • Renewable energy: biogas (fruit waste), solar PV, syngas (nut shells)
  • GHG savings: >32,000 tCO2e/yr per processing centre

Processing Cluster Architecture (TARA/AfDB Design)

The TARA technical report (AfDB Component 4, September 2020) defines a two-tier processing model. Five collection sites feed each eco-processing centre. The collection site handles fruit-to-kernel conversion in rural areas near women's groups. The processing centre handles kernel-to-butter in a nearby town.

Cosmetic buyer survey findings (June 2016, 40+ companies assessed):

  • Typical cosmetic buyer purchase: 50-140 t/yr per company
  • Key specification: melting point 32°C preferred (28-29°C too low; product melts in summer shipping)
  • Price driver: sustainability story valued but price remains primary; buyers will pay small premium only
  • Market trend: "field to product" traceability increasingly demanded by consumers; brands facing social media pressure on unsourced ingredients
  • Growing interest in halal certification for Asian markets and waterless/liquid shea formats

Bottleneck Phase Worksheets

Two BAU phases fall below the 61.8% coherence floor: Phase 5 (Packaging + Export Preparation, balance 0.48) and Phase 6 (Certification + Carbon Registry, balance 0.50). Both are structural — not operational — bottlenecks, driven by the complete absence of export infrastructure and certification capacity in the BAU model.

BOTTLENECK — Phase 5 Packaging + Export Preparation BAU Balance: 0.48 (48%)

D/C imbalance cause: Export packaging and documentation requirements (FFA certificates, moisture specs, origin documentation, buyer-specific quality grades, melting-point testing at 32°C) constitute a high D-constraint load. C (contribution) is near zero in BAU because women sell exclusively to local travelling traders using gourds and informal containers: no export drumming, no lab testing, no quality certification, no buyer relationships. The constraint-to-contribution gap is near-maximal. This phase is the primary structural barrier blocking value from reaching international buyers.

Axis signal: sqrt2 (structural overhead from absent export infrastructure) + phi (value trapped at the Phase 5-to-buyer transition) + e (no financial instruments to fund the infrastructure gap).

BOTTLENECK — Phase 6 Certification + Carbon Registry BAU Balance: 0.50 (50%)

D/C imbalance cause: External certification frameworks (Ecocert/Soil Association organic, REDD+, FairWild, ISO 16128) impose high D-constraint loads on any shea supply chain. In BAU, women's groups have no traceability infrastructure, no auditor relationships, no park registration, and no carbon accounting. D is non-zero (certification constraints exist at the system level, imposed by buyer requirements) but C is effectively zero: no certifications, no credits, no registered carbon. This represents an e-layer failure: a potentially high-value financial abstraction layer exists (REDD+ credits, organic price premium) but is entirely uncoupled from the physical chain.

Axis signal: e (financial instruments absent despite viable mechanism) + ln2 (long time-lag to first credit: REDD+ assessment done but revenue not yet flowing) + sqrt2 (structural absence of certification bodies and registry accounts).

This phase has the highest e-layer upside in the BF Shea supply chain: an estimated USD 1.6–4.8M/yr carbon revenue stream at full scale.
