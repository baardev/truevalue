⬡ Tholonic Neural Architecture

Applying the tholonic N-D-C triadic framework to neural networks. Three subprojects spanning visualization (inference as a 4-phase supply chain: expansion, scaling, equilibrium, compression), empirical measurement (data-driven phase boundary detection across 14 models in 6 families — 78% pass rate), and training experiment (the tholonic balance regularizer). All derive from Milton (2026).

N · Negotiation
The emergent stable output — the activated value at each node, the layer representation, the model's final claim
D · Definition
Weights, normalization, residual connection — constrains which inputs produce significant output
C · Contribution
Weighted input sum, attention mechanism, pooling — integrates and accumulates distributed signal
Source paper: Milton, J.W. (2026). "Neural Networks as Tholonic Systems: A Structural Framework for Architecture, Scaling, and Alignment-by-Design." Clarity Coalition. — Three claims: (1) symbolic AI is D-dominant, (2) connectionist AI is C-dominant and therefore structurally vulnerable to alignment failure, (3) a fully tholonic architecture converges toward cooperative stability as a structural consequence.

Falsifiable Predictions (paper Section 12)

  1. Data-driven phase boundaries: norm ratios match a tholonic constant within 8% at ≥67% of detected boundaries, with each constant in its predicted role (e · expansion, √2 · scaling, ln2 · compression, φ · equilibrium) — RESULT: 78% (51/65), 14 models, 6 families ✓
  2. Functional phase boundaries: R1 = ‖hL‖/‖hembed‖ follows φk and R2 = ‖logits‖/‖hL‖ follows (ln 2)k, both within 8%, across 20+ architectures — pre-registered replication pending
  3. Training quality: φ-deviation at R1 correlates negatively with training quality — Spearman r < −0.55, p < 0.05 on ≥30 models — pending
  4. Convergence speed: tholonically-balanced networks converge faster than matched unbalanced networks — pending (training experiment)
  5. Alignment: tholonic constraint reduces reward hacking in RLHF — smaller gap between reward model score and human preference — pending

Data-Driven Phase Boundary Detection
14 models · 6 families · 78% pass rate · φ, ln2, √2, e at predicted roles · Pre-registered threshold passed

Tests whether tholonic constants (φ, ln2, e, √2) govern norm scaling at neural network phase boundaries. Methodology evolved through three stages: original layer-by-layer φ-ratio (invalidated by residual connections), fixed functional phase boundaries (R1/R2, partial support), and data-driven phase detection using four independent metrics per layer. Final result: 78% pass rate across 14 models in 6 architecture families — above the pre-registered 67% threshold, with each constant appearing in its theoretically predicted role.
