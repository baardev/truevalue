How to Read These Charts

Each model is graded on five axes derived from the tholonic framework. The spider chart shows how close a model sits to the theoretical ideal — a perfect score on every axis would form a full pentagon. What these scores predict about real-world behaviour is explained below.

Virial balance — the universal bottleneck

All 14 models score near zero here. Every architecture is C-dominant: attention and MLP projections carry activations 3–10× larger than LayerNorm sublayers, when the tholonic prediction is D/C = 0.5. A balanced model would train faster (more even gradient flow), hallucinate less (overconfidence is tied to C-dominance), and generalise better under distribution shift (LayerNorm acts as a stabiliser).

ln2 compression — output stage

ln2 ≈ 0.693 is the information-theoretic compression ratio for the final hidden state → logits projection. When this is clean the vocabulary distribution has optimal binary structure: better top-k diversity, less probability mass wasted on irrelevant tokens, and more coherent multi-step reasoning.

φ equilibrium — mid-network stability

φ at mid-network (layers 45–80%) is a self-similar stability checkpoint: the network is neither expanding nor collapsing its representations, just refining them. A stronger φ equilibrium predicts more consistent behaviour across prompt lengths, less coherence degradation in long contexts, and better compositional generalisation.

√2 scaling — early feature expansion

√2 governs how the network expands its representational space in the first third of processing (layers 20–55%). A clean √2 signal predicts a structured feature space, which should improve few-shot learning and analogy/pattern-completion tasks — the representational expansion follows a predictable geometric ratio.

Boundary fidelity — overall phase structure

The percentage of all data-driven phase boundaries that fall within ±8% of any tholonic constant. This is the broadest measure: a high score means the model has discovered self-organising transitions during training, regardless of which constant governs them. Low scores indicate either too few transitions or transitions at irrational ratios.

Testable predictions — what a green model should show

These are falsifiable predictions, not proven results. A model scoring green (≥ 75) across all five axes is predicted to: converge in fewer training steps for equivalent validation loss; assign better-calibrated output probabilities; degrade less in coherence over long contexts; and generalise better out-of-distribution. No existing model achieves this. The virial imbalance is the primary structural gap — confirmed universally across all 14 architectures — that the tholonic regulariser (paper §11.2, §13.4) is designed to close through targeted training.

Full methodology, the complete results table, and the virial bottleneck analysis are documented in the research paper as Section 13.4 — Structural Health Grading. All interpretations above are testable predictions pending controlled training experiments (paper §12, rows 2–5).
