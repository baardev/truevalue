Ecosystem Services (PDI Module 1, A0)

1. Provisioning Services

  • Fisheries: marine and coastal waters, ~600,000 people, 10.2% of exports (sardinella, artisanal fleets)
  • Agriculture: pearl millet, sorghum, maize, rice, groundnuts, legumes, sesame — Groundnut Basin (Kaolack, Fatick, Thiès, Diourbel, Louga)
  • Agroforestry: moringa leaves/oil/seeds, dried mango, shea butter, groundnut oil; traditional parks (Faidherbia albida)
  • Timber and fuel: forests and mangroves provide vital timber, fodder, firewood
  • Native trees: néré (Parkia biglobosa, soumbala seasoning; N-fixing), detarium (Vitamin C fruit; drought-tolerant)

2. Regulating and Cultural Services

  • Coastal and flood protection: mangroves in Sine Saloum and Casamance deltas
  • Water management: Niayes and Casamance ecosystem restoration (saltwater intrusion prevention)
  • Carbon sequestration: inland forests and mangrove restoration; Acorn/Mirova pathway
  • Biodiversity nurseries: mangroves and seagrass as fishery nurseries
  • Djoudj National Park: UNESCO World Heritage, millions of migratory birds, ecological tourism
  • Delta du Saloum National Park: biodiversity and ecotourism

Sovereign Risk Context (Moody's Downgrade, Oct 2025)

Bond Finance Risk: Moody's Triple Downgrade (Oct 2025)
Moody's downgraded Senegal's sovereign credit rating three times in one year, with the third downgrade in October 2025 triggering an immediate week-long sell-off in Senegal's Eurobonds. The 16-year bond traded at a 40% discount to face value. Total public debt: ~US$42B. Moody's primary concern was Senegal's use of WAEMU regional bond markets to raise US$5B in 2025 at ~7% interest (versus double-digit rates on international markets), which Moody's characterised as dependence risk. S&P took the opposite view, recognising regional financing as a strength.

Implication for agroforestry bond finance: The sovereign credit context creates a structurally complex environment for Sustainability Linked Bonds (SLBs) and nature-based finance instruments tied to this project. Regional WAEMU investors have strong appetite (bond issues were oversubscribed in 2025 at lower rates than Eurobonds), which is favourable for domestically-structured nature bonds. However, the Moody's downgrade creates headwinds for international institutional investors required to hold investment-grade securities. The project's affiliation with Mirova (Natixis subsidiary) and EP Carbon provides credibility anchoring that partially offsets sovereign risk for the carbon credit pathway. Source: The Conversation, "Senegal's credit rating: Moody's latest downgrade was questionable," 2026.

Five-Axis Failure Diagnosis

Chain π = 78.0 (system average across 8 phases). No phase falls below the 61.8% coherence floor; therefore bottleneck threshold is set to the chain π (78.0). Three phases fall below it: Phase 1 (70), Phase 4 (65), and Phase 5 (70). All five axes show scores below 80, indicating system-wide stress that is structural rather than phase-specific.

Bottleneck Phase Worksheets

Three phases fall below the chain π of 78.0. Phase 4 (Transformation, balance 65) is the primary bottleneck; Phases 1 (Harvest + Nursery, 70) and 5 (Refining + Export, 70) are secondary. All three share a D-overload pattern driven by structural complexity rather than operational failure.

PRIMARY BOTTLENECK — Phase 4 Transformation (Grinding / Pressing / Biogas) Balance: 65% · D=260 · C=244

D/C imbalance cause: D=260 vs C=244 (gap=16). The D-load arises from the simultaneous co-production of multiple unrelated commodity types (moringa powder, shea paste, groundnut oil, biogas, biochar, organic fertilizer) within 18 distributed centres, each requiring different processing protocols. This creates institutional and operational complexity that raises constraint load. C is depressed by complete opacity in throughput data: no published records exist for individual centre outputs, conversion ratios, or energy yields. The absence of data is itself a structural gap, not an operational one.

Axis signal: ln2 (conversion efficiency unknown without throughput data) + sqrt2 (18-centre distributed system without standardised reporting creates structural overhead) + phi (biogas/biochar co-product value not captured in published market accounts).

Intervention Description Estimated D/C Effect
Open throughput reporting standard Mandate monthly throughput data publication from all 18 centres: kg per commodity type, biogas m3, biochar kg, fertilizer kg. Aligned with Acorn MRV requirements and EP Carbon methodology. C +20 (verifiable outputs); D −10 (removes audit-uncertainty overhead). Net: ~+20 pts balance.
Standardised biogas/syngas measurement protocol Install calibrated gas flow meters at each centre. Required by EP Carbon methodology to quantify fuel-substitution credit. Parallel benefit: verifies avoided-emissions claim for REDD+/Acorn pathway. C +15 (documented energy output entering carbon account); D −5 (removes methodology-uncertainty D-load). Net: ~+15 pts balance.
Phase-resolved LCA study for each commodity Commission a commodity-specific LCA covering moringa, shea, and groundnut processing at the 18 centres. This directly fills the data gap flagged in the EP Carbon xlsx and Serious Shea Financial Model xlsx. Enables transition from PROVISIONAL N-D-C values to confirmed scores. C +15 (documented conversion ratios); D −10 (eliminates per-phase measurement uncertainty). Net: ~+18 pts balance.
IoT centre-level digital monitoring Deploy low-cost IoT energy and weight sensors at each centre. Real-time throughput data removes the primary source of opacity and enables cross-centre benchmarking. C +10 (real-time contribution data); D −5 (removes manual-audit overhead). Net: ~+10 pts balance.
Combined effect estimate: Applying all four interventions lifts Phase 4 from 65% to an estimated 82–86%, moving it from Stressed to Good. The binding constraint is the LCA study timeline (12–18 months from commission to report). IoT monitoring and gas flow meters can be deployed in parallel and immediately improve C. The throughput reporting standard has the highest leverage per cost.
SECONDARY BOTTLENECK — Phase 1 Harvest + Nursery Establishment Balance: 70% · D=260 · C=228

D/C imbalance cause: D=260 vs C=228 (gap=32, the largest gap in the chain). D-load: informal harvesting across multiple cooperatives with no centralised records, seasonal variability, multiple commodity types, and concurrent nursery management without survival rate tracking. C is suppressed because harvest volumes are unverified and nursery survival rates are undocumented, so outputs cannot be attributed to any specific cooperative or phase boundary. The chain's most D-overloaded transition in relative terms.

Axis signal: sqrt2 (structural absence of cooperative record-keeping systems) + ln2 (biological delay: nursery-to-established plantation is 3–5 years; carbon baseline cannot be confirmed until canopy is established).

Intervention Description Estimated D/C Effect
Cooperative harvest recording system GPS-logged weight records at point of collection, per cooperative, per commodity type. Required pre-condition for Acorn MRV additionality assessment. Low-cost: paper log + mobile data entry scales to 18 centres. C +20 (verifiable harvest output); D −15 (removes audit-variance D-load). Net: ~+25 pts balance.
Nursery survival rate monitoring protocol Monthly head-count of surviving seedlings per nursery site, per species. This data feeds directly into the carbon baseline calculation (Acorn requires documented survival rates for tree carbon sequestration claims). Source: Acorn programme eligibility checklist (available in shea project data). C +15 (documented plantation establishment output); D −10 (removes reforestation-uncertainty D-load). Net: ~+18 pts balance.
Lot-level harvest traceability Assign lot IDs linking each harvest batch to a cooperative, a collection site, and a GPS polygon. Enables organic and fair-trade certification at Phase 6 and supports chain-of-custody for premium pricing. C +15 (traceable output enabling certification); D −10 (removes traceability-audit D-load). Net: ~+18 pts balance.
Seasonal harvest forecast model Simple regression model from 3 years of harvest records to forecast seasonal yield variation by site. Reduces off-take planning uncertainty and improves processing centre capacity utilisation. C +10 (predictable output flow); D −5. Net: ~+10 pts balance.
Combined effect estimate: Applying all four interventions lifts Phase 1 from 70% to an estimated 83–87%. The cooperative harvest recording system has the highest leverage (C+20, D−15) and is low-cost relative to impact. The nursery survival protocol is a hard pre-condition for Acorn carbon credit registration; it cannot be deferred without blocking Phase 6 carbon revenue entirely.
SECONDARY BOTTLENECK — Phase 5 Refining + Export Preparation Balance: 70% · D=280 · C=256

D/C imbalance cause: D=280 is the highest constraint load in the entire chain. The D-overload arises from multi-commodity export preparation: four distinct product streams (shea sterin, shea olein, moringa oil, groundnut oil) each with different buyer specifications, quality grades, documentation requirements, and logistics pathways, all funnelling through only 2 shea fractionation clusters. The 2-cluster bottleneck is a structural capacity constraint: it is not a data transparency issue (like Phase 4) but a physical throughput ceiling that caps C regardless of operational efficiency.

Axis signal: sqrt2 (structural capacity binding at 2-cluster throughput ceiling) + phi (value of fractionated shea sterin for confectionery vs raw shea paste not fully captured at 2-cluster scale) + e (export-linked bond instruments not yet in place to finance cluster expansion).

Intervention Description Estimated D/C Effect
Scale from 2 to 4 shea fractionation clusters Add 2 additional shea fractionation clusters to relieve the physical throughput ceiling. This is the primary intervention for Phase 5 and the primary use of proceeds for an SLB or green bond instrument. Directly doubles certified export output capacity. C +25 (doubled certified output capacity); D −10 (removes throughput-constraint D-overhead). Net: ~+28 pts balance.
Multi-commodity export documentation platform Centralise export documentation for all four commodity streams into a single platform (FFA certificates, moisture specs, phytosanitary docs, origin certificates). Removes the per-commodity documentation overhead that inflates D. C +10 (export-ready output across all commodity types); D −20 (eliminates per-stream document fragmentation). Net: ~+22 pts balance.
Buyer-specific quality specification library Develop and maintain a shared specification library for all confirmed buyers (cosmetic, confectionery, food, nutraceutical segments). Reduces per-shipment renegotiation overhead and improves batch planning. C +10 (pre-approved quality outputs to confirmed buyers); D −15 (eliminates specification-uncertainty D-load). Net: ~+18 pts balance.
Cold-chain logistics partnership Establish a cold-chain partnership for moringa oil and high-grade shea olein, which are temperature-sensitive. Reduces shelf-life D-constraint and enables premium nutraceutical market access for moringa. C +8 (temperature-stable output for premium channels); D −8 (removes cold-chain uncertainty). Net: ~+10 pts balance.
Combined effect estimate: Applying all four interventions lifts Phase 5 from 70% to an estimated 86–90%. The cluster expansion (intervention 1) is the structural fix and is capital-intensive; it is the natural use-of-proceeds anchor for an SLB instrument. The documentation platform and specification library can be deployed immediately at low cost and independently improve Phase 5 D-load before cluster expansion is funded. Note: Phase 5 improvements also have a direct upward effect on Phase 6 (certification) by increasing the volume and quality of certifiable output.
