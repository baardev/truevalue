⚠️ Data Note — Gold Transformation Phase Data

The D and C values for gold transformation phases are synthetic estimates based on publicly known cost structures and industry documentation. Real data (actual royalty rates, processing costs, refining margins) exists in corporate annual reports, mining jurisdiction tariff schedules, and LBMA/COMEX filings, but is scattered and not yet integrated into the project schema. The monetary multipliers (value of ore vs. refined bar) are proprietary — refinery margins and mine-to-market cost structures are not publicly disclosed in detail. Populating a value_chain_transformation.csv with actual per-phase revenue and cost data would allow these scores to be computed from real data.

What the ln(2) Score Reveals About Gold
All five of gold's transformation phases score below 50. The system score of 40.4 places the gold chain in the "systematic institutional conduit capture" zone. This means: across every phase where gold's physical nature changes and value should naturally multiply, over half of the constraint burden is institutional rather than natural.

This is not surprising. Gold is one of the most heavily regulated commodities globally — every transformation step is surrounded by AML/KYC requirements, jurisdiction-specific tax structures, LBMA certification costs, and export/import licensing. The natural doubling process (ore becomes bars, bars become jewelry) is physically efficient but institutionally loaded.

The ln(2) model makes the invisible visible: it separates the physical necessity of transformation constraints (D-natural) from the contingent institutional overhead that has been layered on top (D-institutional). When D-institutional exceeds D-natural, the phase has been captured — not in the sense of a conspiracy, but in the structural sense that artificial gates are suppressing natural growth conduits.
