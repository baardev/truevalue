Physical and abstract values are drawn from public filings, analyst testimony, and WGC/BIS/COMEX reports. What the e Score Reveals About Gold

The e model uses D=1, C=1 — the identity case of the tholonic triadic structure. In a perfectly coherent system, every abstract financial claim corresponds to exactly one unit of physical gold. The mathematical constant e emerges from continuous compounding: e = lim(n→∞)(1 + 1/n)ⁿ. This is precisely the dynamic at work when paper claims keep re-referencing the same physical gold — each iteration of re-lending or re-pledging compounds the abstraction while the physical backing remains fixed.

The current system e-coherence score of 37 means the gold supply chain's abstract layer operates at roughly 37% coupling efficiency with its physical substrate. The dominant failure modes are: metal leases at Phase 4 (coupling 0.40 — same gold simultaneously claimed by 2–3 counterparties), LBMA unallocated positions at Phase 6 (OPAQUE — estimated 0.01–0.10 coupling), and COMEX futures at Phase 7 (coupling 0.133 — only 13% of open interest is backed by registered deliverable inventory).

The e model makes the invisible visible: it draws a boundary between the physical supply chain (Phases 0–8) and the abstract claims layer that has grown around it. The physical chain produces roughly 3,300–3,500 tonnes of gold per year. The abstract layer — metal leases, unallocated accounts, ETF shares, and futures contracts — references a multiple of this physical quantity. The system remains stable as long as delivery demands stay within registered physical capacity. Under stress, the gap between abstract claims and physical delivery capacity becomes the primary systemic risk.
