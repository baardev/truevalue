⚠️ Data Note — Boundary Structural Values

D and C values for gold supply chain boundaries are synthetic estimates derived from published LBMA Good Delivery Rules, COMEX warehouse receipt procedures, industry documentation standards, and qualitative assessment of regulatory burden. Real per-boundary documentation requirements exist inside corporate systems, mining jurisdiction permit processes, and private refinery contracts but are not published in machine-readable form. The sqrt2_engine.py hardcodes a structural baseline; future work would replace these with a gold_boundary_structural.csv populated from documented sources.

What the √2 Score Reveals About Gold
The gold chain has a bimodal structural profile. Two boundaries are perfectly symmetric (P3→P4 and P5→P6 both score 100), both anchored by published standards: LBMA Good Delivery at the doré-to-refinery crossing and COMEX warehouse receipt procedures at vaulting-to-exchange. These are the most documented, most institutionally mandated, and structurally most coherent transitions in the chain.

The failure is at the front end. P0→P1 (Prospecting to Mining) scores 28.7 — the worst boundary in the chain. Requirements (geological survey specifications, environmental impact assessments, licensing conditions) are highly formalized on the D side, but the C-side deliverables (the actual geological data, ore body estimates, and permit evidence) are commercially sensitive and not publicly disclosed. The boundary formally demands much; it publicly delivers little. This is a structural asymmetry created by information asymmetry — not by physical impossibility.

The √2 model makes this visible: a boundary is a neutral conduit only when D=C. When D≫C, the boundary has become a toll gate — the excess overhead between what is required and what is delivered either disappears into institutional friction or is captured by whoever controls the documentation gap. At P0→P1, the gatekeepers are licensing jurisdictions and exploration company legal structures.
