Primary structural failure: community and household uptake (Phase 5, 58.3%)

Bristol has built an exceptionally well-defined delivery system at the institutional level: the One City Plan has strong mandates (Phase 0, 88.9%), coordinated governance (Phase 1, 84.0%), contractually-grounded strategies (Phase 2, 90.6%), and robust outcome measurement (Phase 6, 91.9%). The failure is at the point of contact with communities. Only 750 of 470,000 residents participate in the Bristol Climate Action Investment. The 10.7-year male life expectancy gap between deprived and affluent wards is not improving. Scope 3 (consumption-based) emissions account for 60–75% of a typical Bristol household's carbon footprint and 70–90% of the city's corporate footprint, yet remain structurally untracked in the delivery chain. The D-C gap at Phase 5 is 100 points: the widest in the chain. Capital deployment (Phase 3, 70.9%) is the secondary bottleneck: £1bn+ City Leap institutional framework vs. £2m mobilised through citizen mechanisms against a city economy of £22.8bn GDP.

Corporate Scope 3 (GHG Protocol)
Represents 70–90% of an average company's total GHG inventory. In Commercial Real Estate and Construction, indirect embodied carbon and tenant energy push this to 85–95%. Framework: GHG Protocol Corporate Value Chain Standard (15 categories). Estimation: spend-based modelling, activity metrics, or LCA.
Residential Scope 3 (Household)
Approximately 60–75% of a Bristol household's personal carbon footprint comes from indirect (Scope 3) sources: embedded emissions of food, clothing, purchased goods, and services. Direct gas heating = Scope 1; electricity = Scope 2. Framework: PAS 2070 or territorial consumption-based accounting. Estimation: household expenditure surveys, localized utility and consumption data.
