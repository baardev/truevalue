Jackson, Mississippi Water System Context

Why This Project Exists

This case represents a visibly broken common infrastructure system. It contrasts with high-functioning water models by showing what happens when a built potable water system is not sustained by maintenance, staffing, capital renewal, and emergency repair capacity.

Physical Chain

  • Source water and watershed condition
  • Raw water intake and pumping
  • Coagulation, sedimentation, and filtration
  • Disinfection and chemical treatment control
  • Clearwell, storage, and finished-water pumping
  • Distribution network pressure
  • Household delivery and access
  • Wastewater collection and conveyance
  • Wastewater treatment and discharge
  • Stormwater and flood stress interaction
  • Regulatory monitoring and public notice
  • Emergency water distribution and service restoration

N-D-C Interpretation

The defining failure is not source-water scarcity. It is imbalance between high definition constraints, including safe-drinking-water rules and pressure requirements, and weak contribution capacity, including treatment reliability, pump performance, pressure maintenance, repair execution, and public service delivery.

Source Categories

Recommended source categories include EPA emergency orders, DOJ consent decree materials, Mississippi State Department of Health notices, city operational reports, public infrastructure assessments, and outage or boil-water notice time series.

5
Distribution Network Pressure
Balance 43% | D=335, C=145, gap=190.0 | Primary failure axis: sqrt2 (√2)
Bottleneck

Why sqrt2 diagnoses this failure: D=335 is the highest in the chain: distribution system D-flags include main breaks (215 main breaks in 2021 alone, vs industry benchmark of ~15/100 miles/year), pressure zone failures, ageing cast iron mains with >50% of the system over 40 years old, and a pumping station (OB Curtis) with failed primary pumps. C=145 is near-zero: the distribution network cannot deliver water pressure to most service areas when treatment is operational because the physical pipes and pumps are failing. sqrt2 diagnoses structural overhead: the infrastructure is generating D-flags faster than any operational intervention can compensate.

RankInterventionD-C MechanismProjected Balance
1 IIJA funding deployment: OB Curtis pump replacement EPA IIJA allocation to Jackson: USD 600M announced 2022. Priority 1 use: replace failed primary pumps at OB Curtis treatment plant. Each operational pump adds a critical C-flag. Estimated C increase Phase 5: +40 to +60. 43% to 60-65%
2 Main break prioritisation programme (worst-first replacement) Use GIS-mapped break rate data to prioritise replacement of the highest-break-rate segments. Each replaced segment removes a pressure-failure D-flag. Target: 25 km/year of highest-risk mains. Estimated D reduction: -15 to -20 per replaced segment group. 43% to 58-63%
3 Pressure zone boundary valve rehabilitation Restore pressure zone isolation valves to working condition. This is a low-cost intervention (USD 5,000-15,000 per valve) that prevents zone-wide pressure loss from a single main break. Each functional valve adds a system-resilience C-flag. 43% to 50-55% (valve rehab alone)

Combined effect: Phase 5 from 43% to 60-65% with pump replacement and main prioritisation. Full Coherent zone (above 61.8%) requires sustained IIJA investment over 5-10 years. Phase 3 Disinfection (50%) requires parallel treatment investment.

3
Disinfection and Chemical Treatment
Balance 50% | D=320, C=160, gap=160.0 | Primary failure axis: ln2
Bottleneck

Why ln2 diagnoses this failure: The disinfection phase is the critical transformation from raw to potable water. D=320 reflects the constraint load of: failed chemical dosing systems, unreliable chemical supply contracts, operator shortage (Jackson lost 30% of certified operators 2015-2022), and absent process control instrumentation. C=160 is critically low: the treatment plant was producing water that periodically met no potable standard at all (EPA Emergency Administrative Order, August 2022). ln2 identifies this as a transformation failure: the input (raw reservoir water) cannot be converted to potable water because the chemical and physical transformation pathway is non-functional.

RankInterventionD-C MechanismProjected Balance
1 Chemical feed system replacement and automated dosing Replace manual chemical dosing with SCADA-controlled automated systems. Each automated dosing point removes a manual-failure D-flag and adds a process-reliability C-flag. Estimated C increase: +30 to +50. 50% to 65-70%
2 Certified operator recruitment and retention programme EPA consent decree requirement: hire and retain Grade 4 certified operators. Each certified operator shift adds an operational-competence C-flag. Operator shortage is a direct ln2 failure: the transformation agent (human expertise) was absent. 50% to 62-68% (with chemical system)
3 Backup chemical storage (30-day minimum supply) Maintain 30-day on-site chemical inventory (chlorine, coagulant, fluoride, pH adjustment). Each day of reserve removes a supply-chain disruption D-flag. 50% to 58-62% (storage alone)

Combined effect: Phase 3 from 50% to 65-70% with automated dosing + certified operators + chemical storage. This is the highest public health priority intervention in the chain.

How the Five-Model Constants Diagnose Failure: Jackson MS Water System (Infrastructure Failure 2022)

Each coherence axis (pi, phi, sqrt2, ln2, e) measures a different dimension of systemic health. The N-D-C balance formula is balance = 100 - (|D - C| / max(D, C)) * 100. A failing phase has D significantly greater than C: the constraint load exceeds contribution capacity. Chain average balance: 59%.

Eight of 12 phases are in the Failure zone (below 61.8%). This is a systemic infrastructure collapse. sqrt2 (30) is the primary diagnostic: physical infrastructure (treatment plant built 1950s-1970s, distribution pipes avg >50 years old) accumulated D-flags from deferred maintenance for decades. ln2 (35) identifies treatment process failure: chemical dosing, filtration, and disinfection processes were non-functional. e (45) identifies that available federal funding (EPA WIFIA, SRF, IIJA) was not operationally linked to performance obligations or maintenance schedules.
