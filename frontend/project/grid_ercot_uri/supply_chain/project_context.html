Why This Project Exists

This case represents a common infrastructure system that became dysfunctional under stress. Unlike a normal supply chain, an electric grid must balance supply and load continuously. When fuel, generation, balancing, and distribution all degrade together, the failure propagates immediately.

Physical Chain

  • Weather forecast and demand expectation
  • Natural gas production and fuel supply
  • Fuel transport and gas-electric dependency
  • Generation winterization and plant readiness
  • Generation dispatch and fleet availability
  • Transmission and interconnection limits
  • Control room frequency balancing
  • Load shedding and emergency operations
  • Distribution feeder outage management
  • Critical services and dependent infrastructure
  • Customer outage exposure and public communication
  • Emergency restoration and black-start readiness

N-D-C Interpretation

The grid had strong definition constraints: frequency stability, reserve requirements, cold-weather demand, and critical-load obligations. Contribution capacity collapsed when fuel supply and generation availability failed, forcing emergency load shedding to preserve the remaining system N-state.

Recommended source categories include FERC/NERC Uri reports, ERCOT operational records, PUCT filings, EIA energy data, outage reports, critical-load studies, and post-event weatherization compliance data.

Generation Winterization and Plant Readiness
Balance 37% | D=350, C=130, gap=220.0 | Primary failure axis: sqrt2 (√2) and ln2
Bottleneck

Why sqrt2 and ln2 diagnose this failure: D=350 is the highest in the chain: simultaneously, generators lacked heat tracing on instrumentation, fuel supply systems froze, and lubrication oils gelled. Each unprotected system added a D-flag. C=130 is among the lowest: generators could not dispatch because they were physically inoperable. sqrt2 identifies structural overhead: the physical infrastructure was operating without winterization standards that were technically feasible (the same generators were winterized after Uri at an average cost of USD 1.6M per unit per PUCT order). ln2 identifies the transformation failure: the generation asset could not convert fuel input into electrical output because the conversion pathway (turbine bearings, control systems, fuel lines) had frozen.

RankInterventionD-C MechanismProjected Balance
1 Mandatory weatherization to NERC Cold Weather Standard (TPL-001-5) Require all ERCOT generators to meet the NERC cold-weather standard retroactively. PUCT ordered this in SB 3 (2021): each winterized plant removes multiple D-flags (frozen fuel system, frozen instrumentation, lubricant failure). Post-Uri analysis shows average cost USD 1.6M per unit; benefit: each winterized unit adds C-flags for all cold-weather hours. Estimated C increase: +80 to +100 per winterized unit. 37% to 72-78% (full weatherization compliance)
2 Firm gas supply contracts for generators (dispatchable capacity) Require gas-fired generators to hold firm transportation contracts on intrastate gas pipelines (Texas Railroad Commission authority). During Uri, interruptible gas was curtailed to industrial loads; generators had no firm rights. Firm contract removes the primary D-flag. Estimated D reduction: -50 to -70 per plant with firm contracts. 37% to 58-65%
3 Dual-fuel or backup fuel capability requirement Require generation units to have 30-day on-site backup fuel (distillate for gas turbines, coal stockpiles for existing coal). Texas has fewer firm fuel requirements than PJM or MISO. On-site fuel removes the fuel-supply D-chain entirely. 37% to 68-74%

Combined effect for Phase 3: balance from 37% to 72-78% with full weatherization + firm gas + backup fuel. This is the minimum viable reform. Phases 1, 2, 4, 6-10 all require analogous structural interventions.

Natural Gas Production and Fuel Supply
Balance 45% | D=330, C=150, gap=180.0 | Primary failure axis: sqrt2 (√2) and ln2
Bottleneck

Why sqrt2 and ln2 diagnose this failure: D=330 reflects the interconnected failure of wellhead freeze-offs (~40,000 MMcfd curtailed), compressor station failures, and pipeline pressure drops. C=150 is low because the gas supply network, which normally has high contribution capacity, was operating at near zero output. This is a parallel sqrt2 failure to Phase 3: the physical infrastructure was not designed for the recorded temperatures (-2 to -20 F across the Permian Basin). ln2 identifies the transformation failure: wellbore gas could not be converted to pipeline-quality gas because processing facilities froze.

RankInterventionD-C MechanismProjected Balance
1 Wellhead and gathering-system weatherization Texas Railroad Commission TRC Rule 3.92 (enacted post-Uri) requires critical gas infrastructure to weatherize. Each weatherized wellhead and compressor station removes a D-flag. Estimated D reduction: -60 to -80 per field with full compliance. 45% to 62-68% (TRC 3.92 full compliance)
2 Critical producer designation with curtailment protection Designate critical gas producers serving electricity generators as protected from curtailment (FERC Order 2222 framework for Texas intrastate). Each protected producer adds a C-flag for cold-weather dispatchability. 45% to 58-63%

Combined effect: Phase 1 from 45% to 62-68%. Both Phases 1 and 3 must be addressed simultaneously; fixing one without the other leaves the chain broken.

How the Five-Model Constants Diagnose Failure: ERCOT Texas Grid (Winter Storm Uri 2021)

Each coherence axis (pi, phi, sqrt2, ln2, e) measures a different dimension of systemic health. The N-D-C balance formula is balance = 100 - (|D - C| / max(D, C)) * 100. A failing phase has D significantly greater than C: the constraint load exceeds contribution capacity. Chain average balance: 47%.

Every phase except Phase 0 (77%) and Phase 5 (68%) is in the Failure zone (below 61.8%). This is a system-level coherence collapse. sqrt2 (30) and ln2 (25) are the primary diagnostic axes: the entire physical infrastructure layer (gas wells, pipelines, generation plants) lacked winterization, causing simultaneous D-elevation across all phases. phi (35) confirms no phase was receiving proportional C during the crisis. e (15) is the lowest in the dataset: no financial instruments were operationally linked to cold-weather performance obligations.
