Primary Bottleneck: Phase 5 — Packaging and Bottling (Balance 81.1%)

Spain exports approximately 67% of its olive oil as undifferentiated bulk at EUR 3.22/kg average. Italian companies (Salov/Filippo Berio, Deoleo/Bertolli) blend and rebrand this oil at EUR 4.42/kg, capturing EUR 1.20/kg in brand premium. This is not a logistics problem but a market positioning failure: D (brand fragmentation, 1,500 independent bottlers, no coordinated export brand strategy) significantly exceeds C (contribution from Spanish brand investment). On 742,500 tonnes shipped in 2023/24, this gap represents approximately EUR 890 million in foregone annual revenues.

Climate Constraint: Phase 0 Production — Drought as Structural Bottleneck

Climate stress (drought frequency, heat extremes) is a structural constraint at Phase 0 (Cultivation), not an exceptional event. The 2022 drought reduced Spanish harvests by approximately 48% below historical average (Europe's worst drought in 500 years). Dcoop reported daily yields falling from 8,000 kg/day to 1,000 kg/day, an 87.5% intraday reduction at peak heat. This structural climate risk depresses the Phase 0 D-C balance because the C dimension (ecological contribution from rainfall and climate system) is becoming systematically less reliable. Any supply chain analysis that treats 2019-2021 production volumes as the baseline is overstating the chain's structural capacity. Climate risk must be modelled as a permanent Phase 0 constraint, not a shock scenario.
