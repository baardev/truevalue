      Export
      Balance 64.0% | D=210, C=165, gap=45.0 | Primary failure axis: ln2 and e
      Bottleneck

      Why ln2 and e diagnose this failure: The export phase is the chain's value-transformation chokepoint. D=210 reflects documentary requirements (phytosanitary certificates, EUDR forest-risk compliance, quality grading standards). C=165 is low because exports predominantly leave as undifferentiated bulk butter or kernels: no certified-origin premium, no traceability documentation passed to EU buyers, and no sustainability credential that commands a price premium. The ln2 axis identifies this as a transformation gap: the input (processed shea) could command 30-50% higher prices with origin documentation, but the conversion pathway does not exist. The e axis (68) confirms that EU green finance instruments (SFDR Article 9 funds, EUDR transition finance) are not operationally linked to shea export KPIs.

      RankInterventionD-C MechanismProjected Balance
      1 Certified origin programme with EUDR pre-compliance Establish village-level geo-polygon sourcing documentation meeting EUDR Article 3 requirements. Each documented collection zone adds a C-flag to Phase 4. Target: 50% of export volume with full polygon coverage by Year 2. Estimated C increase: +20 to +30 points. 64.0% to 72-76%
      2 SheaTech quality grading and differentiated pricing Implement ASNAPP/GIZ quality grading at aggregation points. Grade A certified butter earns 20-35% premium. Each graded consignment removes a quality-uncertainty D-flag and adds a premium C-flag. Estimated D reduction: -10 points. 64.0% to 74-78%
      3 Sustainability-linked export finance Structure a trade finance facility with interest rate step-down linked to EUDR compliance rate and collector welfare KPIs. Activates e axis. Each disbursement linked to a verified KPI adds a financial C-flag. 64.0% to 78-82% (with all interventions)

      Combined effect: Phase 4 balance from 64.0% to 78-82%, above the chain pi (81.6%). This would shift the primary bottleneck to Phase 0 Collection (68.2%).

      0
      Collection
      Balance 68.2% | D=235, C=175, gap=60.0 | Primary failure axis: phi (φ) and sqrt2 (√2)
      Bottleneck

      Why phi and sqrt2 diagnose this failure: D=235 reflects the physical access constraints of dispersed wild shea tree populations across Burkina Faso, Mali, and Ghana: long travel distances, manual harvesting, and no cold storage at collection points (C-suppressing). C=175 is low because collector women have no formal negotiating infrastructure, no quality measurement at the point of first sale, and receive below-market prices. The phi axis identifies this as a value distribution failure: collectors bear the highest physical burden in the chain while receiving the smallest share of final retail value. sqrt2 confirms the structural overhead: physical terrain and infrastructure absence raises D independently of market conditions.

      RankInterventionD-C MechanismProjected Balance
      1 Village-level collection cooperatives with cold storage Establish 50-litre cold storage at village aggregation points using solar-powered units (GreenPath Energy model, ~USD 800/unit). Cold storage removes quality degradation D-flag and adds a C-flag (higher-quality output). Estimated C increase: +15 points. 68.2% to 74-76%
      2 Mobile phone market-price access (Esoko/SSNIT integration) Provide collectors with real-time market price data via SMS. Reduces information asymmetry D-flag. Each price-informed collector transaction adds a market transparency C-flag. Estimated D reduction: -8 points. 68.2% to 72-75%
      3 Fairtrade/WFTO certification for collection cooperatives Formal certification raises C (institutional recognition, documented minimum price, social premium payments). Each certified cooperative adds C-flags across Phase 0. Estimated C increase: +10 to +20 points. 68.2% to 76-80%

      Combined effect: Phase 0 balance from 68.2% to 78-82%. With Phase 4 also addressed, the chain reaches full Coherent status across all phases.

      Phase Intervention Worksheet

      PROVISIONAL: Recommendations are derived analytically from PDI phase-level N-D-C data and public sources. Field validation is required before capital commitment. Each intervention is grounded in the specific mathematical axis that identifies the type of failure.

      How the Five-Model Constants Diagnose Failure: Shea Supply Chain (West Africa to EU Cosmetics)

      Each coherence axis (pi, phi, sqrt2, ln2, e) measures a different dimension of systemic health. The N-D-C balance formula is balance = 100 - (|D - C| / max(D, C)) * 100. A failing phase has D significantly greater than C: the constraint load exceeds contribution capacity. Chain average balance: 81.6%.

      ConstantChain ScoreWhat It Measures If Below 80: The System Has...Intervention Category
      pi (π) 81.6 Operational balance: chain average across all phases Phases pulling the aggregate below the sustainable threshold Process improvement; technology adoption at the phase level
      phi (φ) 74 Proportional value distribution: each phase receives value proportional to contribution Value trapped or lost at phase transitions Market structure reform; cooperative integration; direct-to-consumer models
      sqrt2 (√2) 76 Structural overhead: physical or institutional friction raising D without adding C Physical or regulatory constraints are the binding factor Infrastructure investment; mechanisation; governance reform
      ln2 66 Value transformation: how effectively each phase converts inputs to higher-value outputs Transformation blocked by a gap (time lag, capital absence, biological delay) Patient capital instruments; multi-cropping; phased transition
      e 68 Financial abstraction: how well financial instruments are linked to real performance metrics Available funding is not operationally connected to the ecosystem Bond instrument design; KPI linkage; governance pre-conditions for finance activation

      Two phases in the Failure zone: Phase 4 Export (64.0%) and Phase 0 Collection (68.2%). phi (74) confirms value is not proportionally distributed: collector women receive less than 5% of final retail value. ln2 (66) diagnoses the export transformation gap: nuts and kernels lose traceability and quality differentiation at the border, blocking premium value realisation. e (68) reflects no sustainability-linked bond has yet tied financial returns to Phase 0 or Phase 4 KPIs.
