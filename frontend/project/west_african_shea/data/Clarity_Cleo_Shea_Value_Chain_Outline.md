### Gaps vs a phase-based supply chain model

| Criterion | Gap |
|-----------|-----|
| **Phases** | No discrete phases (e.g. collection → aggregation → processing → export → manufacturing → retail). Steps are implied but not named, no phase_id, boundaries unclear. |
| **Physical flow** | No end-to-end path: where nuts are stored, how they move collector → trader → processor; where butter is produced (village vs central); how butter reaches Europe; where Cleo manufacturing and filling happen. Chain is fragmented. |
| **Custody / control** | No distinction between who owns, who holds, who can mobilize at each step. Transfers not specified (ownership vs custody vs physical movement). |
| **Schema** | No structure of the form Phase → Metric → Unit → Source → Custodian. Not tabulated by phase; not CSV/schema-ready. |
| **Transparency** | No tagging of phases by transparency (high/medium/low) or structural reasons for opacity. |
| **Missing data** | Gaps (e.g. transport legs, storage nodes, processing locations) not marked explicitly as OPAQUE. |
| **Separation of concerns** | Document is dominated by price, income uplift, margins, and carbon — value and impact, not physical supply chain. Physical flow is secondary. |

### Verdict

The document gives **useful fragments** for building a supply chain (actors, conversions, geography, some quantities, sources) but **does not define the supply chain** in a phase-resolved, custody-aware, schema-first way. It is primarily a **value and impact** summary (income multiplier, CO₂e, retail price) with some physical and actor data mixed in. To use it for supply chain modeling, phases would need to be defined, custody and transfers specified, missing steps marked OPAQUE, and metrics assigned to phases with units and sources in a tabular schema.
