PHASE DISCOVERY INSTRUMENT

Africa Blue Economy Initiative

Mangrove Ecosystem Services Supply Chain

Material / Product Analysed

African mangrove ecosystem services supply chain: the full arc from degraded coastal ecosystem through data extraction, specification, certification, and commercialisation to restored and protected blue economy asset

Primary Sources Consulted

Geospatial land cover assessments via ODIN dMRV

Geographic scope

Seven AU coastal member states: Nigeria (Niger Delta, Burutu LGA / Forcados Estuary), Cameroon (Douala Estuary and Rio del Rey), Mozambique (Zambezia, Sofala, Nampula), Madagascar (West Coast), Guinea (Boffa-Koba Ramsar Complex), Tanzania (Rufiji Delta), Benin (Seme-Kpodji and Oueme Delta)

Programme scale

150,000 ha targeted restoration and conservation; 10-year implementation (Phase I March 2026 to March 2031; Phase II April 2031 to April 2036); total budget USD 1 billion

Module 1: Anchor the Chain

A1. Pre-Commercial State

Degraded and threatened mangrove ecosystems occupying the coastlines of seven African Union member states, before any conservation intervention, measurement framework, or financing structure. The material exists as living but functionally compromised coastal forest, with continuing net loss driven by fuelwood extraction, agricultural conversion, urban expansion, oil extraction (Nigeria and Cameroon), and cyclone damage.

Physical condition at origin: a mosaic of land cover classes across the 150,000-hectare programme envelope. Intact native mangroves (LC-01) with continuous canopy and functioning hydrology. Degraded mangroves (LC-02) with native species present but thinned or disturbed. Converted and severely degraded areas (LC-03). Carbon-rich intertidal sediments and peaty soils (LC-04), representing 75 to 90% of total ecosystem carbon stock. Settlements and infrastructure (LC-05), excluded from carbon accounting but mapped for leakage management.

Ecosystem carbon stock at origin, by country: Nigerian Delta intact systems exceed 2,500 Mg CO2e per hectare, with sediment organic carbon of 622 to 640 Mg C per hectare in the top metre. Cameroon Douala Estuary sediment 500 to 800 Mg C per hectare. Mozambique intertidal sediments 450 to 600 Mg C per hectare. Madagascar 450 to 600 Mg C per hectare. Guinea 500 to 700 Mg C per hectare. Tanzania 400 to 600 Mg C per hectare. Benin 300 to 500 Mg C per hectare.

Without-project baseline emissions from ongoing degradation: programme-level baseline approximately 6.5 to 10.4 MtCO2e per year.

Biodiversity state at origin: Africa's mangroves harbour at least 17 mangrove tree species and serve as nursery grounds for over 1,500 species of fish, birds, and mammals. Up to 80% of commercial fish biomass in West Africa is mangrove-dependent. Approximately 1.5 million coastal dwellers depend directly on these ecosystems.

A2. Point of Final Market Delivery

A restored, protected, and measurably verified mangrove blue economy asset: 150,000 hectares of mangrove ecosystem across seven countries, generating independently verified outputs across four value streams simultaneously:

Verified carbon sequestration: net annual sequestration of approximately 5.64 million tCO2e per year after 20% non-permanence buffer, registered under an internationally recognised carbon standard (e.g., Verra VM0007/VM0033, Gold Standard, Plan Vivo, ICR, ACR) and held on the corresponding standards registry

Recovered fisheries and coastal protection: measurably improved fisheries yields, biodiversity recovery, and coastal protection for over 1.5 million coastal dwellers

Community blue economy livelihoods: documented income from fisheries, benefit-sharing, women and youth employment

Sovereign NDC fulfilment: integration of restoration outcomes into nationally determined contributions

The financial mechanism delivering investment to this end state is the African Union Blue Economy Bond (AUBEB), a DFI-issued conservation milestone-linked instrument whose coupon is serviced from sovereign general revenues, with blue carbon revenues contracted as a derisking reserve layer above the coverage floor. The bond and its associated revenues are mechanisms within the commercialisation phases of the supply chain. They are not the end state. The end state is the restored ecosystem and its sustained multi-value outputs.

A3. Unit of Measurement at Origin

Hectares of mangrove ecosystem by land cover class (LC-01 through LC-04), with associated carbon stock measurements in Mg C per hectare and without-project baseline emissions in MtCO2e per year. Biodiversity expressed as species presence and nursery function per site.

A4. Unit of Measurement at Market

Verified tCO2e sequestered per year (net of buffer), registered on a standards registry. Hectares restored and under active protection, verified by ODIN dMRV remote sensing. Fisheries productivity index and species recovery indicators. Community livelihood metrics: households benefiting, women and youth employment figures. NDC contribution confirmed by host government.

A5. Unit Change

Yes, multiple unit changes occur. The primary conversion is from area-based physical measurement (hectares and Mg C per hectare) to verified output units (tCO2e per year, fisheries yields, employment figures). A secondary conversion occurs within the commercialisation phase when tCO2e credits are priced in USD. The first unit change occurs at Phase 3 (specification and quantification). The second occurs at Phase 5 (commercialisation).

Notes

Registry and standard flexibility: This PDI does not commit to a specific carbon registry or standard. The Africa Blue Carbon Initiative will select the most appropriate certification pathway based on cost, efficiency, and market access at the time of issuance. Credible options include Verra (VM0007/VM0033), Gold Standard, Plan Vivo, the International Carbon Registry (ICR), and the American Carbon Registry (ACR). All listed registries can issue credits eligible for CORSIA and Article 6.2 with corresponding adjustment where host countries authorise. The PDI refers generically to "internationally recognised standards" and "standards registries" to reflect this flexibility. ICR and ACR are noted as particularly cost-effective and efficient options with full CORSIA/Article 6.2 eligibility.

GUD's role in this PDI and the bond structure: GUD is a co-developer of the Africa Blue Carbon Initiative alongside the AUC, bringing finance stakeholder relationships (including MUFG), ODIN dMRV digitisation capability, carbon sector expertise and strategy, and community equitable revenue structuring. For the AUBEB bond instrument, the legal counterparty to the DFI issuer is the AUC and host governments. GUD's relationship is with the AUC and carbon credit buyers, not on the bond's signature line. This PDI therefore includes GUD as an actor class (A7) and as a custodian where GUD performs specific functions (data extraction, restoration design), but GUD is not a bond counterparty. This structure is standard for DFI-issued sovereign instruments where commercial co-developers sit outside the bond covenant chain.

Phase 5 pricing transparency: Phase 5 achieves High transparency by defining its measurable output against publicly documentable conservative floor pricing rather than bilateral contract terms. The VCM floor of approximately USD 15 per tCO2e and the CORSIA compliance floor of approximately USD 20 per tCO2e are independently verifiable from public market data and scheme design parameters. Credit volume is registered on the public standards registry. Both volume and floor price are externally confirmable without requiring disclosure of bilateral sovereign contract terms. Revenue above these floors is treated as upside. Current market signals show supply of biodiversity-linked blue carbon credits structurally below demand, with strong price inclination above the floor.

Phase 8 opacity: The one Medium transparency phase is Phase 8 (community custodial), where the quality and depth of FPIC processes and community governance structures are not independently verifiable from outside the local context without dedicated field assessment. This is common to all nature-based investment instruments. GUD ODIN remote sensing can verify physical restoration outcomes at Phase 9 independently, but governance quality requires field-level verification that is inherently less transparent than satellite data.

Carbon data source: Carbon sequestration data across Phases 3, 4, 9, and 10 is drawn from the AUC/GUD concept note Annex II and country-specific factsheets. It represents indicative planning metrics annualised over a 30-year crediting period. The figures are appropriate for supply chain structural quality assessment by TrueValue Analytics and are not presented as financial projections or bond coupon coverage calculations.

African Seed Bank: The African Seed Bank sub-process within Phases 7 and 8 preserves regionally differentiated seed populations for long-term ecosystem resilience. As the concept note states, repeated selection of high-performing climate-adaptive seed sources from outside local areas can progressively narrow genetic diversity across restored landscapes. The Seed Bank preserves regionally differentiated seed populations and locally adapted phenotypes for future reintroduction. This sub-process does not currently have a confirmed measurable output at programme level and is therefore classified as a sub-process rather than a confirmed phase boundary. It should be elevated to Phase status if the Seed Bank achieves formal establishment with defined governance and inventory.

Phase 0 opacity rationale: Phase 0 (pre-commercial origin) is correctly classified as Low transparency because no measurement framework existed at origin. However, the baseline carbon stock and emissions trajectory have been retrospectively established from satellite archive (2001-2024) and field survey data, as documented in the country-specific factsheets. This retrospective quantification is sufficient for additionality under applicable carbon methodologies and does not prevent the phase from being structurally defined. The Low transparency classification reflects the absence of a pre-existing measurement framework, not the absence of verifiable baseline data.
