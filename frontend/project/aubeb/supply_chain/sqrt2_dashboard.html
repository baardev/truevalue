⚠️ Data Note — Structural Transfer Data for Mangrove

The D and C values in this model are inferred from AUBEB PDI source material, including programme mandate, ODIN dMRV, carbon quantification, certification, fund deployment, CBEMR design, and community co-management requirements. The underlying raw data (site work packages, disbursement records, field validation records, and community governance records) is not yet integrated. The P0→P1 boundary is the most structurally asymmetric because the degraded ecosystem baseline is reconstructed retrospectively. Populating the project with actual custody, MRV, and restoration records would allow these scores to be computed from data rather than inferred.

What the √2 Score Tells You
Score 80–100: Symmetric
D requirements match C deliverables. The boundary is a neutral conduit, it imposes only the natural √2 overhead (41.4%) and nothing more. AUBEB achieves this where carbon quantification, certification, and capital deployment handoffs are clearly specified.
Score 40–80: Asymmetric
Either D > C (requirements exceed deliverables — potential extraction point) or D < C (deliverables exceed requirements — informal bypass). Both indicate structural imbalance at the boundary.
Score 0–40: Critical asymmetry
The boundary has failed the √2 structural test significantly. P0→P1 (score 28.7) reflects baseline opacity: high formal requirements, low public data delivery. This is the structural basis for degraded-ecosystem origin opacity.
