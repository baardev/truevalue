Ivory Coast (44%) and Ghana (16%) supply over 60% of global cocoa from approximately 5 million smallholder farms averaging 2 ha each. Primary Bottleneck: Fermentation and Drying Opacity (Phase 1, balance 76.5%)

The most critical quality-determining transformation in the global cocoa chain — biochemical fermentation over 5-8 days followed by 7-14 days of sun drying — happens at farm level with no standardized process, no temperature or humidity monitoring, no chain-of-custody documentation, and no identity preservation. This is where approximately 85% of chocolate flavor precursors develop. It is also where child labor is most concentrated (estimated 1.5 million children in Ivory Coast and Ghana alone). Sustainability certifications issued at cooperative membership level (UTZ, Rainforest Alliance, Fairtrade) operate downstream of this opacity and cannot verify the practices they claim to certify. EUDR compliance requires deforestation-free geolocation polygon mapping at farm level, but the custody infrastructure to document what happens between polygon mapping and export certification does not yet exist at scale. The bond intervention point is here: CLMRS enrollment rates and verifiable fermentation-batch documentation are the measurable KPIs that address the root cause.
