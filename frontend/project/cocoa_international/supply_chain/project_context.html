Geographic Scope and Production Structure

The global cocoa supply chain is extraordinarily concentrated. Ivory Coast alone accounts for approximately 44% of global production at 2.2 million tonnes per year, and Ghana adds another 16% (800,000 tonnes), giving West Africa a combined 60%+ share. Ecuador (350,000 t), Cameroon (280,000 t), Indonesia (220,000 t), and Nigeria round out the major producers. This geographic concentration is unlike most global commodity chains and creates systemic fragility: a single mid-migratory crop disease event (swollen-shoot virus) or climate shock affecting West Africa would affect the majority of global chocolate production.

Ivory Coast has deliberately pursued a domestic processing strategy. Under CCC (Conseil Cafe-Cacao) policy, Ivory Coast now grinds approximately 700,000-800,000 tonnes per year domestically (35% of its harvest), producing cocoa liquor, butter, and powder for export. ADM, Cargill, Olam, and Barry Callebaut all have grinding plants in Abidjan and San Pedro. The stated national goal is 50% domestic processing by 2030. This shift is fundamentally restructuring the value distribution in the global chain.

Bond Opportunity Analysis

Bond Structure 1: Phase 1 Traceability and Child-Labor-Free SLB

Performance trigger: CLMRS household enrollment rate in Ivory Coast (baseline ~700,000 households; target trajectory toward full coverage of ~600,000 registered farms).

Secondary trigger: Share of cocoa covered by geolocation polygon mapping verified against deforestation databases (baseline ~24% of supply certified; EUDR requires 100% by 2025-12-30).

Use of proceeds: Funding for cooperative fermentation infrastructure (fermentation box standardization, temperature/humidity monitoring equipment), CLMRS digital enrollment platforms, and farm-level extension training.

Measurement basis: ICI (International Cocoa Initiative) CLMRS annual audit; ICCO certification statistics; satellite geolocation polygon registry (Rainforest Alliance, IDH Cocoa Compass).

Revenue basis: Processor and manufacturer sourcing contracts indexed to verified supply volumes. Barry Callebaut Forever Chocolate, Cargill Cocoa Promise, and Nestle Cocoa Plan all have published sourcing commitments that could anchor bond coupon structures.

Indicative scale: USD 200-500 million (aligned with major processor sourcing program investments).

Bond Structure 2: EUDR Deforestation-Free Sourcing Compliance Bond

Performance trigger: Share of total cocoa procurement covered by EUDR-compliant due diligence documentation (geolocation polygon, deforestation-free verification, risk assessment) as reported under EU Regulation 2023/1115.

Use of proceeds: Funding for satellite monitoring platforms, cooperative-level traceability system deployment (Trace platform, SourceTrace, Farmforce), and national registry development in Ivory Coast and Ghana.

Revenue basis: EU market access premium for EUDR-verified supply. Manufacturers who cannot demonstrate compliant sourcing risk losing EU market access after 2025-12-30.

Indicative scale: USD 100-300 million per major sourcing company; could be structured as a consortium bond across Barry Callebaut, Cargill, and Olam.

Climate stress on West Africa0 Rising temperatures and changing rainfall patterns projected to reduce suitable cocoa cultivation area in Ivory Coast and Ghana by 25-50% by 2050 without variety and shade adaptation. High
Swollen-shoot virus0 Cacao Swollen Shoot Virus (CSSV) is endemic in West Africa. An estimated 16 million trees infected in Ghana alone. No cure; affected trees must be removed. Systemic risk to 60% of global supply. Medium
Processor oligopoly5 Three companies (Barry Callebaut, Cargill, Olam) control >60% of global destination grinding. Single-actor failure or regulatory action would disrupt the chain. Medium
