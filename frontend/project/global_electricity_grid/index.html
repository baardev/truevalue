Structural Alert: Grid and Storage Investment Lags Generation-Side Progress

In 2024, approximately 700 GW of renewable capacity was added globally (nearly 80% solar PV), and generation from solar and wind rose by a record 670 TWh. But the physical infrastructure that would carry and balance that power has not kept pace. Phase 2 (Transmission, balance 74.1%) is constrained by interconnection queue backlogs and permitting delays documented in IEA Electricity Grids and Secure Energy Transitions analysis. Phase 3 (Balancing and Storage, balance 74.5%) is constrained by reserve margin and demand-response capacity that lags deployed storage, a gap visible in the curtailment statistics published by CAISO, ERCOT, and National Grid ESO. Both phases sit in the Stressed zone (61.8-80%), below the chain average of 81.8%. See the related ERCOT Winter Storm Uri case study for a documented example of what happens when grid balancing and winterization fail simultaneously under stress.

IEA Global Energy Review 2025: Generation Mix and NZE 2050 Pathway

Coal 35% to 1%
Coal's generation share falls from 35% (2024, largest source for 50+ years) to 1% under the IEA NZE 2050 pathway (28% by 2030 STEPS, 20% by 2030 NZE)
Wind 8% to 35%
Wind generation share rises from 8.3% (2024) to 13% (2030 STEPS), 18% (2030 NZE), and 35% (2050 NZE); solar rises from 7.0% to 33% over the same horizon
EU / US / China 2024
In the EU, solar+wind surpassed coal+gas combined for the first time in 2024. In the US, solar+wind reached 16% (overtaking coal). In China, solar+wind reached nearly 20%.
