Geographic Scope and Ecosystem Zones

The Marina Alta comarca occupies the northeastern interior of Alicante Province, characterised by terraced dry-stone agricultural systems on rocky limestone slopes. The region contains approximately 13,150 hectares of active farmland (9.5% of the provincial useful agricultural area) across three distinct elevation zones, each with its own principal commodity.

Phase Intervention Worksheet

PROVISIONAL: Recommendations are derived analytically from PDI phase-level N-D-C data and public sources. Field validation is required before capital commitment. Each intervention is grounded in the specific mathematical axis that identifies the type of failure.

How the Five-Model Constants Diagnose Failure

Each of the five coherence axes (pi, phi, sqrt2, ln2, e) measures a different dimension of systemic health. When a phase has a low balance score, the five-model analysis identifies which type of failure is driving it. This determines what category of intervention can actually close the D-C gap, rather than applying generic solutions.

The N-D-C balance formula is: balance = 100 - (|D - C| / max(D, C)) * 100. A failing phase has D significantly greater than C: the constraint load (D) exceeds the system's contribution capacity (C). Each constant axis explains a different reason why D is elevated or C is suppressed.

Constant Chain Score What It Measures If Below 80: The System Has... Intervention Category
pi (π) 86.3 Operational balance: chain average across all phases Phases pulling the aggregate below the sustainable threshold Process improvement; technology adoption at the phase level
phi (φ) 82.0 Proportional value distribution: does each phase receive value proportional to its contribution? Value is being trapped or lost at phase transitions (handoff problems) Market structure reform; cooperative integration; direct-to-consumer models
sqrt2 (√2) 78.0 Structural overhead: physical or institutional friction that raises D without adding C Physical constraints (terrain, labour intensity, access) are the binding factor Infrastructure investment; orchard redesign; mechanisation; governance reform
ln2 80.0 Value transformation: how effectively does each phase convert inputs into outputs of higher value? Transformation is blocked by a gap in the chain (time lag, capital absence, biological delay) Patient capital instruments; multi-cropping to bridge revenue gaps; phased transition
e 71.0 Financial abstraction: how well are financial instruments linked to real performance metrics? Available funding is not yet operationally connected to the ecosystem Bond instrument design; KPI linkage; governance pre-conditions for finance activation

Phi-derived zone thresholds: Coherent above 61.8% (1/phi); Failure 38.2-61.8%; Breakdown below 38.2%. Any axis below 80% represents a system under measurable stress. All three below-80% axes (sqrt2, ln2, e) converge on the same two bottleneck phases: 2 and 6.

2
Harvest and Field Processing
Balance 76.5% | D=255, C=195, gap=60 | Primary failure axis: sqrt2 (√2)
Bottleneck

Why sqrt2 diagnoses this failure: The sqrt2 axis measures structural overhead: physical or institutional friction that raises D (the constraint load) without adding to C (the contribution capacity). In Phase 2, the structural overhead is the intersection of three independent D-raising factors: (1) terraced mountain terrain that prevents mechanised access to most cherry plots; (2) the pollinator timing mismatch that makes fruit set unpredictable, so even when pickers arrive, the crop may not be there; (3) Drosophila suzukii, which attacks ripe fruit before the harvest window closes. Each factor alone is survivable; all three simultaneously has pushed D to 255 while C (cooperative infrastructure, PGI pricing) remains at 195. The sqrt2 chain score of 78.0 confirms this is a structural, not a market, problem. Market solutions (better prices, more buyers) cannot close a gap that originates in physical and biological constraints.

6
Crop Transition and Investment
Balance 75.0% | D=260, C=195, gap=65 | Primary failure axes: ln2 and e
Bottleneck

Why ln2 and e diagnose this failure: The ln2 axis measures value transformation across phases. Phase 6 is an extreme ln2 failure: a 5-7 year window in which inputs (land, labour, capital) are consumed with zero revenue output. The phase is not just stressed; it is structurally unable to transform value during the establishment period. This is the highest D in the entire chain (D=260) because the combination of establishment time, capital requirement, and biological delay stacks constraints without any compensating C contribution until Year 5+.

The e axis failure (71.0) is the second diagnosis: EUR 83M of EU Horizon funding and EUR 49,701 per young farmer in Generalitat grants exist in principle, but are not yet operationally linked to measurable ecosystem performance outcomes. An e-axis failure cannot be fixed by technology; it requires a governance and financial instrument design response.

Combined effect: sequencing (carob first) + multi-cropping bridge + bond activation would bring Phase 6 from 75.0% to an estimated 83-85%, just above the AA- bond grade threshold (82.0%) and above the chain phi score (82.0%). At that point Phase 7 (79.4%) becomes the weakest phase, which is addressable through governance reform (CASAMA reactivation) rather than capital.

7
Ecosystem Regeneration
Balance 79.4% | D=252, C=200, gap=52 | Primary failure axis: sqrt2 (√2) + governance
Stressed

Why sqrt2 diagnoses this failure: The CASAMA water governance body (inoperative since 1987) is a pure structural overhead problem: the administrative machinery needed to coordinate aquifer management exists in law but not in practice. Every action that would raise C (artificial recharge, reclaimed water distribution, storage pond permits) requires CASAMA to be functional. This is a governance-constrained sqrt2 failure, not a technology or capital failure. The drip irrigation dilemma illustrates this precisely: the technology exists, the water exists (24% recharge rate, highest in the province), but the governance framework to manage the draw-down trade-off does not.

A critical counterintuitive insight from the MDPI Land (2023) study: replacing agricultural land with natural forest reduces aquifer recharge by 32% in this karstic system (forests transpire more than they intercept). This means maintaining agricultural land use is itself an aquifer recharge service. Interventions that keep land in productive use (replacement crops, agrotourism, multi-cropping) also maintain the ecosystem's highest-value water regulation function.

Rank Intervention D-C Mechanism Funding Pathway
1 CASAMA reactivation (governance pre-condition): lobby Generalitat Valenciana to reconstitute the CASAMA water consortium with modern governance. This is a pre-condition for every other Phase 7 intervention. Without it, reclaimed water schemes, artificial recharge, and storage pond permits cannot be coordinated across the 33 municipalities. Removes the primary structural D-flag. CASAMA reactivation alone raises C by approximately 20 points (all currently blocked actions become possible), which would bring Phase 7 balance from 79.4% toward 86-88%. Political advocacy via ASAJA Alicante, Cireres Cooperative, Tierra Joven; linked to EU Horizon pre-condition requirement
2 Reclaimed water pilot: negotiate with one coastal municipality (Denia or Benissa) to pipe treated wastewater to mountain terrace drip systems for a 50-100 ha pilot. Removes the aquifer draw-down tension for that plot without reducing recharge from precipitation. Adds C directly (a new water source that does not deplete the aquifer) without adding D. Counter-intuitively, keeping these plots in active use preserves the 24% recharge rate; letting them reforest reduces it by 32%. Prevents a D increase. Each ha that naturalises reduces aquifer recharge and raises D in Phase 7. Maintaining 100 ha of abandoned terraces in productive use preserves approximately 19-20mm/year of aquifer recharge (100ha x 189mm x 10%, estimated terrace contribution). Generalitat young farmer grants; Tierra Joven platform (farmland access); carob and aromatic herb market revenue (self-financing within 3-4 years)

Phase 1 (Agricultural Production, 81.6%): Secondary Priority

Phase 1 is in the stressed zone (81.6%, below the chain pi of 86.3%) but is not an independent bottleneck: its failures are almost entirely upstream dependencies of Phases 2 and 6. Fix Phase 2 (pollination, labour) and Phase 6 (replacement crops established), and Phase 1 recovers automatically as the farming system stabilises. The phi axis (82.0) confirms that value flow between phases is proportional when production is functioning; the structural failures are at the harvest and investment layers, not in the growing system itself.

The one direct Phase 1 intervention that does not depend on Phases 2 or 6: introduction of self-pollinating, late-blooming almond varieties (Guara, Soleta) on plots abandoned from traditional varieties. These remove the pollination mismatch D-flag for almonds independently of the cherry pollination programme, at relatively low cost (variety substitution via grafting or new planting).

Reference Governance Model: Orange County Water District (OCWD)

Marina Alta discharges over 70% of its treated wastewater to sea while its CASAMA water governance body has remained inoperative since 1987. The Orange County Water District in Southern California represents the institutional and engineering endpoint of the pathway CASAMA reactivation must eventually reach: a single consolidated authority managing indirect potable reuse, aquifer injection, and seawater barrier infrastructure at basin scale. OCWD is not a peer ecosystem in the N-D-C model; it is a governance reference target. Its three operating pillars map directly onto Marina Alta's Phase 7 intervention priorities.

Sources: MDPI Sustainability 2025 (Sanchez-Perez / Lopez-Ortiz); ocwd.com; ResearchGate Marina Alta water distribution 2014; CA Coastkeeper desalination analysis; MDPI 2071-1050/17/12/5512.

Pillar 1: Unified Water Governance (Phase 7 map: CASAMA reactivation)

DimensionOCWD ModelMarina Alta Application
Governance structure Single consolidated Board of Directors actively manages, replenishes, and protects the overarching groundwater basin across all municipalities. No fragmented municipal-level management. CASAMA (Consorcio de Abastecimiento y Saneamiento de Aguas) has legal existence but has remained largely inoperative. Pillar 2: Advanced Water Reuse (Phase 7 map: reclaimed water pilot)

DimensionOCWD ModelMarina Alta Application
System Groundwater Replenishment System (GWRS): world's largest water purification project for indirect potable reuse. Treated wastewater undergoes microfiltration, reverse osmosis, and ultraviolet light, then is injected into basins or coastal barriers. No expensive coastal desalination dependency. Marina Alta treats 15.1 hm3 annually; 70.87% is discharged to sea; 1.82% is reused for agriculture. Local plants (Teulada-Moraira) should upgrade to advanced reverse osmosis and UV treatment so reclaimed water can safely recharge aquifers or irrigate agricultural land, following the GWRS indirect reuse model rather than relying on desalination.
Immediate priority GWRS delivers approximately 130 million gallons per day (492,000 m3/day) of purified water to the basin. A 50-100 ha pilot piping treated wastewater from Denia or Benissa to mountain terrace drip systems is the priority entry point: no new water source required, no aquifer draw-down, direct C addition to Phase 7.

Pillar 3: Aquifer Protection and Seawater Barriers (Phase 7 map: MAR and monitoring)

DimensionOCWD ModelMarina Alta Application
Monitoring infrastructure 400 district-owned monitoring wells. Scientific modeling prevents over-extraction. Injection barrier wells maintain hydraulic pressure to keep saltwater out of freshwater zones. Prolonged droughts and tourist-season demand spikes have overexploited coastal aquifers. Implementing a monitoring network allows tracking of subterranean water tables. Purified wastewater injection can maintain hydraulic pressure, keeping saltwater intrusion out of freshwater wells: the same function as OCWD's injection barriers but applied to the karstic limestone geometry of Marina Alta.
MAR technique fit OCWD uses direct basin injection and surface spreading at scale. The Prado Spreading Basins cover 230 acres for surface recharge. The karstic limestone system supports three MAR options evaluated in the Phase 7 intervention: (1) direct injection wells (Llíria karstic precedent, 1,000 L/s capacity); (2) check dams and surface spreading in upper catchments (~200 Mm3/year Mediterranean Arc additional recharge estimate); (3) traditional acequia-type infiltration networks. CASAMA reactivation is the governance pre-condition for all three.
Key counterintuitive constraint OCWD operates on imported Colorado River water and local stormwater, both managed within a unified basin authority. Marina Alta's karstic system has an additional counterintuitive constraint absent in OCWD: replacing agricultural terraces with natural forest reduces aquifer recharge by 32% (MDPI Land 2023). Keeping terraces in active use is itself a recharge service. The OCWD analogy holds for governance and reuse, but the land cover equation is inverted relative to typical Mediterranean reforestation assumptions.

OCWD as Phase 7 endpoint: If Marina Alta follows the full OCWD pathway, the terminal state of Phase 7 would be: a reactivated CASAMA operating as a consolidated basin authority; a GWRS-style indirect potable reuse plant at Teulada-Moraira scale; an injection barrier network protecting coastal aquifers; and 400+ monitoring points across the 33 municipalities. That is a 15-25 year institutional trajectory, not a single project. The Phase 7 interventions in this analysis represent the first two steps on that trajectory: CASAMA reactivation and a reclaimed water pilot. Neither requires waiting for the full OCWD infrastructure build-out to deliver measurable N-value improvement.
