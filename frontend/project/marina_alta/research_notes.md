## 2. Geography and Ecosystem Zone

- **Region:** Marina Alta, Alicante Province, Valencian Community, Spain
- **Active farmland:** approximately 13,150 hectares (9.5% of provincial UAA)
- **Elevation zones:**
  - Upper mountain valleys (500-900m): Cherries (Vall de Gallinera, Vall d'Alcala, Beniarres, Alfafara, Penaguila)

- **UNESCO City of Gastronomy:** Denia (designated December 2015)
- **Closest infrastructure:** Alicante (1hr drive), Alicante Airport (1hr), Denia Beach (50 min from Planes)

## 3. Commodity 1: Cherries (Anchor Commodity)

### Status and Varieties
- **PGI:** Cerezas de la Montana de Alicante (Protected Geographical Indication)
- **Varieties:** Burlat (early), Prime Giant (mid-season), Starking (late)
- **Climate trials underway:** Cold-hour-adapted varieties requiring fewer chilling hours

### Production Collapse Timeline
| Year | Volume | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 2017 | ~800,000 kg stable | 2,889 ha Valencian Community |
| 2021 | 800-1,000 tons | Last stable year |
| 2022-23 | -14% area | 2,486 ha; Alpatro cooperative closed 2023 (zero production) |
| 2024 | 25% grower abandonment over 3 years | Insured volumes down 23% in one year |
| 2025 | 40,000-50,000 kg in Vall de Gallinera (5% of normal) | Province-wide: 1,500 of 5,000 potential tons (-70%); sector losses EUR 10M |
| 2026 | 3-4 million kg expected | First good season in 6 years; EUR 25M revenue forecast |
| Vall de Gallinera 2017-2022 | Lost 122 ha non-citrus fruit crops | 371 ha to 249 ha |

Sources: Cherry Times, SmartCherry World, La Marina Alta, FreshPlaza, TodoAlicante

### Pricing Data
- Spanish wholesale average: EUR 5.93/kg (2025), EUR 4.74/kg (2024), EUR 5.00/kg (2023)
- Farm gate peak May: EUR 2,509/T; August: EUR 1,364/T (UN FAOSTAT)
- PGI retail box price: EUR 4-20/box depending on caliber and quality
- Wholesale 2025 range: EUR 4.63-22.86/kg

### Financial Model (from PPTX business plan)
- Production income range: EUR 5,456/Ha/year (lowest yield/price) to EUR 62,725/Ha/year (highest)
- 10 Ha target: EUR 54,560 to EUR 627,250/year gross income
- Production costs: approximately EUR 62,000/10 Ha/year (70% labour)
- Labour: 30 people per acre per day for picking; 50% reduction with transition to 2D pruning
- Scaling model: Production 1.5-4K T cherries; at EUR 1.3K/T = EUR 1.95M to EUR 5.2M; at EUR 2.4K/T = EUR 3.6M to EUR 9.6M

### Main Threats
1. Drosophila suzukii (spotted-wing drosophila) pest: attacks ripe fruit before harvest
2. Pollinator tree flowering mismatch: climate shift means pollinator trees flower at wrong time relative to cultivar trees, preventing cross-pollination - identified as the principal 2023 collapse cause
3. Extended drought in non-irrigated zones (non-irrigated farms bear the full impact; irrigated farms achieve 50-70% of normal in bad years)
4. High operational costs: diesel, self-employment fees, brush-cutters
5. Aging farmer demographics: Marina Alta is the second-oldest comarca in Alicante; low generational renewal

### Response Technologies
1. Artificial pollination: electrostatic pollen sprayer (stone fruit physiologist research, supplements bee pollination; University of Minnesota has active research programme)
2. 2D orchard design: vertical fruiting offshoot (UFO) system; one-year wood fruiting; compact wall of fruit trained on wire like vines; 50% faster picking; 10.6 T/acre achievable
3. Multi-function covers: EUR 28-30K/acre (unviable at project start; pilot one row Year 1 per PPTX)
4. Drip fertigation
5. Organic pest control: Neem (from Africa Agritech Group, results within days)
6. Real-time in-situ lab: measure pollination of stamens, pests, soil nutrients and water content
7. Bee hives and additional wild flowers

### Global Cherry Context (from email notes)
- Three biggest producing nations: USA, Chile, Turkey
- Chile the biggest supplier globally (last 5 years)
- Castellon province (adjacent) rebounding to 1,500 T (contrast to Alicante)
- Global cherry export values rising and significantly higher than Spanish average

## 5. Commodity 3: Almonds

### Threats
- Xylella fastidiosa bacterial blight: no cure; mass felling in Tarbena, Alcalali (hundreds of thousands of trees cleared)
- Drought and Mediterranean heatwaves
- Northern Alicante production: 99% collapse at worst in 2024/25

### Production Trend (Alicante Province)
| Period | Volume (in-shell or shelled) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Mid-2010s | 10,000-15,000 T (in-shell) | Non-drought years; before Xylella took hold |
| 2020-2023 | 6,000-12,000 T (in-shell) | High volatility; heatwaves and water scarcity |
| 2024/25 | ~8,000 T (in-shell) | Disastrous; Valencia region total only 4,490 T |
| 2025/26 | 16,500 T (in-shell) | Massive surge, doubled; abundant winter/spring rains |
| 2026/27 forecast | ~2,300 T (shelled) | La Unio forecast; Valencia Community exceeds 8,000 T grain |

Sources: BEXYL Project, TodoAlicante, Inc.nutfruit.org, BSPP

### Economics
- Farm gate revenue: EUR 3-6/kg shelled
- Compared to cherry (EUR 12-20K/Ha for almonds vs up to EUR 62K/Ha for cherries in good years)
- Quicker ROI than pistachios (4-5 year horizon vs 5-7 years)

### Recovery Varieties
- Guara: self-pollinating, late-blooming
- Soleta: self-pollinating, late-blooming; both reduce frost and pollination mismatch risk
- Research into Xylella-resistant lines ongoing (BIOVEXO project)

## 6. Commodity 4: Replacement Crops

### 6a. Pistachios (Pistacia vera) - "Green Gold"
- **Climate fit:** Semi-arid inland (Alto Vinalopo, Alcoia); cold winters, hot summers; 600m altitude ideal (replicates Iran conditions)
- **Water needs:** Exceptionally drought-tolerant vs stone fruits
- **Price:** EUR 7-12/kg (vs EUR 0.63-0.83/kg for olives; vs EUR 6-8 for pistachio in La Mancha CGTN data)
- **Spain national:** 70,000+ ha; 2,800 T/year harvest; world's 3rd biggest producer
- **Time to first meaningful harvest:** 5-7 years
- **EU Funding note:** Cannot be counted as carbon sequestration for carbon credits (different accounting from mitigation)

YouTube: "Spanish farmers dump olives for 'green gold' Pistachios"  
URL: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iOWqSyQTiwk  
Channel: CGTN Europe | Length: 2:17 | Published: October 2022  
Key content: La Mancha farmers (600m altitude, central Spain dry conditions) replacing olive trees with pistachios. One farmer doubled expected yield to 2,000 kg/Ha. Spain pistachio acreage: from 5,000 ha a few years ago to 70,000+ ha now. Pistachios EUR 6-8/kg vs olives EUR 0.63-0.83/kg. Growing interest in relocating to pistachio farming areas. Reporters call it the crop that is "bringing the land and economy back to life."

### 6b. Carob (Ceratonia siliqua - Algarroba)
- **Rainfall requirement:** As low as 350 mm/year (extremely drought-tolerant)
- **Soil:** Flourishes on marginal, shallow, degraded limestone soils (dominant in region)
- **Traditional status:** Historically "poor man's crop" for animal fodder; now major commercial revival
- **Modern uses:** Locust bean gum (pharmaceutical, cosmetic), health food (chocolate substitute)
- **Heritage:** Monumental specimens in l'Alfas del Pi, Algar, Serpis River banks
- **Commercial revival:** Viveros Hernandorena leading intensification towards modern high-density irrigated plantations; conference June 2025
- **Ecological value:** Deep roots prevent hillside erosion; natural firebreaks in mountain forests
- **Market demand:** High demand in pharmacological, cosmetic, health food industries

### 6c. Modern Almonds (Prunus dulcis - improved varieties)
- **Varieties:** Guara, Soleta (self-pollinating, late-blooming)
- **Frost resistance:** Better than traditional varieties
- **Revenue:** EUR 3-6/kg
- **ROI:** Quicker than pistachios (established trees, 4-5 year productive horizon)
- **Water needs:** Low; surviving well in rain-fed (secano) mountain plots

## 8. Aquifer and Water Data

**Primary source:** MDPI paper "Integrated Management, Circular Economy and Reclaimed Water: Keys to Restoring the Long-Term Water Balance in La Marina Alta (Alicante, Spain)" (Sanchez-Perez and Lopez-Ortiz, June 2025). DOI: 10.3390/su17125512

Key findings:
- Marina Alta has the highest groundwater recharge rate in Alicante province: 24% of precipitation (approximately 189 mm/year)
- Some carbonate outcrops in the mountains reach 30-40% recharge coefficients
- Despite high recharge rate, aquifers are overexploited due to: climate change impacts, insufficient water treatment technology, absence of storage/regulation infrastructure, high agricultural and tourism demand
- CASAMA (Consorcio de Abastecimiento y Saneamiento de Aguas de los Municipios de La Marina Alta) has been inoperative since 1987
- Marine intrusion affecting coastal aquifers
- Solutions proposed: artificial aquifer recharge, reclaimed water from coastal treatment plants, new storage ponds, integrated water resource management

**Additional sources:**
- "An Assessment of Groundwater Recharge at a Regional Scale" (MDPI Water, 2021): confirms Marina Alta has the highest recharge rate in the province
- "Land Cover Changes and Their Influence on Recharge in a Mediterranean Karstic Aquifer" (MDPI Land, 2023): when extensive agriculture is replaced by natural forests, total water entering the aquifer decreases by 32% (important for reforestation trade-offs)
- "Assessing Water Shortage through a Balance Model" (MDPI Water, 2019): agricultural water demand in SE Spain approximately 135 hm3/year; 50 hm3/year deficit in water-stressed areas

**Tajo-Segura aqueduct:** Young farmers in Alicante actively protesting water transfer cuts. The Tajo-Segura system is a major external water source for SE Spain agriculture.

**Drip irrigation note (from email):** Drip irrigation is identified as a key solution for tree crop production in mountain terraces but is complex to administer on terraced terrain, and creates drawdown tensions for potable water supply.

**Slide 11 (Pollination Research):**
Date palm pollination research cited as parallel: fresh pollen 96.3% viability, 85% germination. Stored at -30C retains highest viability. Timing: highest fruit set with pollination at 12:00-15:00, not 8:00-11:00.

**Slide 12 (Tree Training and Pruning):**

"Multi-purpose covers: high-density polyethylene roofs. Cost: Unviable at start EUR 30,000 per acre. Solution: experiment with 1 row in Year 1."

**Slide 14 (NZ Case Study):**
4 Ha UFO (Upright Fruiting Offshoot) cherry orchard in NZ. 1.5-metre rain covers. Picking UFOs 50% faster than leader trees. Cherry crop can return 5x the value of grapes per hectare in a good year.

## 13. Summary Assessment

The Marina Alta ecosystem is under severe and accelerating stress from climate change. The cherry industry has collapsed from 800,000 kg to 40,000-50,000 kg in Vall de Gallinera in under a decade. The 2026 recovery (3-4 million kg projected province-wide) is fragile and weather-dependent. Almonds face structural collapse from Xylella. Olive oil production is down from drought.

The ecosystem services framework (water retention, pollination, biodiversity, cultural heritage, ecotourism) is deeply interdependent with agricultural productivity. Increased biodiversity would improve ecosystem service scores for water and soil retention, stability, nutrients, reduced evaporation, pollination, and ecotourism.

The strongest investment thesis is: use the technology and institutional infrastructure for cherry recovery (artificial pollination, 2D orchard design) as the anchor, while diversifying into pistachios and carob for drought resilience, and positioning premium EVOO bodegas as the stable anchor income stream while cherries recover.

EU Horizon funding for Climate Change Adaptation (EUR 83M available) is the primary external capital source to pursue. The Los Grobos scaling model provides a commercial template. TrueValue Analytics' own presence in Denia positions the project for direct engagement.
