Concept: TVPCI-R and B_chain

TVPCI-R (True Value Pricing Convergence Index — Return) measures the waste recovery and circular economy performance of the supply chain alongside the forward TVPCI chain balance. It assigns each phase an R_p score (0-100) representing the fraction of waste streams that are recovered, valorized, or avoided, weighted by the relative waste volume of each phase (omega weight). B_chain (Holon Balance) combines the forward and return chains into a single coherence measure: a high B_chain requires both a balanced forward chain (high TVPCI) and a strong return chain (high TVPCI-R). The Netherlands chain has TVPCI-R=65.0, significantly better than the global cocoa average of 46.0, because Dutch industrial waste infrastructure (AEB Amsterdam waste-to-energy, Dutch EPR Packaging Decree, PPWR compliance) is among the best in the world. However, B_chain=57.3 remains in the failure zone because the 25.1-point gap between TVPCI (90.1) and TVPCI-R (65.0) indicates that waste recovery has not kept pace with the forward chain's institutional strength.

Formula: B_chain = 100 × exp(−2 × |TVPCI − TVPCI-R| / TVPCI) = 100 × exp(−2 × 25.1 / 90.1) = 100 × exp(−0.557) = 57.3

The Netherlands TVPCI-R of 65.0 is 19 points above the global cocoa chain (46.0). This is the most direct quantitative expression of what Dutch industrial waste infrastructure means in supply chain terms. The gap in Delta_chain (Netherlands: 25.1 vs global: 43.2) shows that the forward-return imbalance is less severe here — Dutch waste systems partially bridge the gap between a strong forward chain and a weak return chain.

Phase-Level R_p Scores (PROVISIONAL)

Phase 0 — Port Arrival and EUDR Clearance R_p = 72 · omega = 0.05
Port of Rotterdam sustainability program; Rotterdam Climate Agreement commitments; vessel emission tracking (shore power connections at Amsterdam/Rotterdam); ballast water management protocols. R_p=72 reflects good but imperfect port-level emission and waste management.
Phase 1 — Warehousing and Quality Inspection R_p = 68 · omega = 0.08
Bean debris and shell fragments recovered and sold to biofuel processors or AEB Amsterdam. Jute sacking reuse programs. Warehouse refrigeration not required for dry bean storage (lower energy footprint than other commodities). R_p=68 reflects good but not exceptional recovery.
Phase 2 — Industrial Grinding R_p = 75 · omega = 0.30 (largest waste volume: cocoa shell)
Highest-weight phase. Cocoa shell (approximately 10-12% of bean weight = 60,000-72,000 tonnes/year at Amsterdam) is partially recovered through AEB Amsterdam waste-to-energy processing. Barry Callebaut's Forever Chocolate commitment includes energy transition targets. Cargill Amsterdam reports renewable energy adoption. Wastewater treatment at Amsterdam Westpoort. R_p=75 is the strongest in the chain — shell-to-energy is a mature Dutch industrial pathway.
Phase 3 — CBE and Specialty Fat Manufacturing (includes shea residues) R_p = 62 · omega = 0.20
IOI Loders Croklaan at Wormerveer. Shea press cake (the residue after shea butter extraction) has uses as animal feed and fertilizer — valorization depends on sourcing country arrangements. Fractionation process in the Netherlands is dry or solvent-based; solvent fractionation waste is managed under Dutch environmental permits (Wet milieubeheer). IOI's sustainability commitments include RSPO certification pathway. R_p=62 reflects good process management but shea press cake valorization is partially outside Dutch control (occurs at West African origin).
Phase 4 — Dutch Chocolate Manufacturing R_p = 55 · omega = 0.22
Lowest R_p in the Netherlands chain. Chocolate manufacturing waste streams: non-conforming product (recipe deviations, tempering failures) goes to secondary uses (industrial chocolate input) or is landfilled. Multi-layer flexible packaging (typical for chocolate bars) is difficult to recycle; Dutch EPR Packaging Decree applies but compliance for multi-layer formats is imperfect. Tony's Chocolonely uses 100% recyclable or reusable packaging (committed target) — again demonstrating that a higher-R_p alternative exists within the same phase.
Phase 5 — Domestic Retail and EU Re-export R_p = 60 · omega = 0.15
Netherlands deposit system (statiegeld) for bottles but not for chocolate packaging. Dutch retail has some of the most aggressive EPR compliance in Europe. Albert Heijn and Jumbo have retail food waste reduction programs. Re-export of semi-finished products (cocoa butter, liquor, powder) generates minimal additional waste relative to the product volume. R_p=60 reflects above-EU-average but not exceptional recovery in the retail channel.

Priority Circular Economy Interventions

1. Phase 2: Cocoa Shell Biochar and Nutraceutical Extraction (Scale-Up)

AEB Amsterdam already processes some cocoa shell to energy. Upgrading this stream to biochar (carbon-sequestering soil amendment) or nutraceutical extraction (cocoa shell is rich in methylxanthines, polyphenols, and theobromine) would materially increase Phase 2 R_p from 75 toward 85-90. A Phase 2 R_p of 85 would increase TVPCI-R from 65.0 to approximately 68.0 and B_chain from 57.3 to approximately 60.5.

Estimated TVPCI-R uplift: +3 to +5 points · Bond candidate: Amsterdam Industrial Circularity SLB

2. Phase 4: Zero Non-Conforming Product to Landfill (Tony's Standard Extension)

Tony's Chocolonely has a commitment to zero-waste-to-landfill in its manufacturing operations. Extending this standard across the Dutch chocolate manufacturing sector (currently Verkade/Mondelez, private label) would lift Phase 4 R_p from 55 toward 65-70. Combined with Phase 2 improvement, this would push TVPCI-R above 70 and B_chain above 62.

Estimated TVPCI-R uplift: +3 to +4 points · Bond candidate: Dutch Cocoa Circular Manufacturing SLB

3. Phase 4-5: Monomaterial Packaging Transition (PPWR Alignment)

EU Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR) sets ambitious recycled content and recyclability requirements by 2030. Netherlands is already an EPR leader. A Dutch cocoa sector collective packaging transition bond — similar to the Tony's recyclable packaging commitment — could fund R&D and tooling for monomaterial confectionery packaging, lifting Phase 5 R_p from 60 toward 75 across the sector.

Estimated TVPCI-R uplift: +2 to +3 points · Bond candidate: Netherlands Cocoa Packaging Transition Green Bond

4. Phase 3: Shea Press Cake Valorization Tracking (Cross-Chain Integration)

The shea press cake generated in West Africa (at the shea processing Phase 3 in the TrueValue shea project) is a residue of the supply chain that enters cocoa_netherlands Phase 3 as an input quality constraint. A formal cross-chain waste valorization tracking system — connecting the West African shea press cake fate to the Dutch CBE manufacturing waste accounting — would improve the shea Phase 4 R_p from its current estimated value and propagate upward into the cocoa_netherlands Phase 3 composite.

Estimated indirect TVPCI-R uplift: +1 to +2 points · Bond candidate: Shea-Cocoa Cross-Chain Circularity Bond
