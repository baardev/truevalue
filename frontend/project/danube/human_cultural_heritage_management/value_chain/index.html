Abstract / Financial Coupling Score

Cultural heritage management has a partial financial abstraction layer (e = 35). ERDF grants provide input-based coupling between heritage condition and public capital: sites that apply for and receive ERDF co-financing are subject to expenditure reporting requirements. City bed taxes (Vienna EUR 3.02/night, Budapest EUR 4.00/night) create a revenue pool theoretically linked to heritage. However, neither mechanism is performance-linked: ERDF reports expenditure, not conservation outcome; bed taxes flow to city general funds, not to specific site maintenance.

A performance bond indexed to the conservation reinvestment ratio and site condition improvement would shift the e-layer from input-based to output-based, pushing e from 35 toward 55-60. The revenue basis is strong: EUR 2.8 billion in cultural tourism is an order of magnitude larger than the EUR 180M/yr conservation gap. The financial coupling problem is one of structure (covenant and measurement design), not of revenue availability.
