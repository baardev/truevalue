Section 1: System Overview

The Danube Delta and lower Danube floodplain support 2,800 km² of Phragmites australis reed bed, constituting the largest continuous reed ecosystem in Europe. The geographic distribution spans: 1,750 km² in the Danube Delta (Romania and Ukraine); 850 km² in the lower Danube floodplain (Bulgaria and Romania); 200 km² in scattered wetland pockets along the middle Danube and Sava floodplain.

Annual dry biomass production is estimated at 800,000 t. The sustainable harvest quota is 560,000 t/yr, but actual annual harvest is only 140,000 t: 25% of the quota. Mean stand biomass density is 9.8 t/ha. Total delta biomass stock is 17.2 Mt. The delta reed bed stores an estimated 8.5 Mt CO2 in soil organic carbon, with active sequestration of 0.65 Mt CO2/yr. The system supports 320 dependent species, 42 nesting bird species, and provides nursery habitat for 28 fish species.

Section 5: Key Threats

HIGH: Sediment deficit from Iron Gate I and II (57% reduction). The Iron Gate hydropower dams trap sediment that historically sustained the delta's accretion rate. The 57% reduction in sediment flux since dam construction creates progressive subsidence of reed-covered islands and embankments. Without adequate sediment supply, the physical substrate supporting reed stands degrades over multi-decadal timescales. This constraint is upstream of all five service cascade phases and cannot be resolved within the reed system itself.
HIGH: Warm winters reducing ice-access harvest windows. Winter reed harvest requires frozen water surfaces for machinery access to the 58,000 ha of harvestable area. Climate-driven warming reduces the number of days with adequate ice cover, particularly in the lower delta. This directly limits the 25% quota utilisation rate and suppresses access to the premium thatching-grade reed cut before spring regrowth. Bond proceeds must include investment in amphibious harvest equipment capable of operating without ice access.
MEDIUM: Nutrient eutrophication from upstream agricultural runoff. An annual nitrogen load of 380 kt/yr from the Danube watershed promotes eutrophication of delta waters. Elevated nutrient loading can shift the competitive balance from Phragmites to submerged aquatic vegetation or invasive species. Eutrophication also degrades water clarity and biodiversity value, reducing the non-harvest ecosystem service premium that underpins the bond revenue case.
MEDIUM: Absence of reed sustainability certification standard. No internationally recognised reed sustainability certification standard currently exists. This prevents differentiation of Danube delta reed from lower-quality or less sustainably harvested sources in the European thatching market (Netherlands, Germany, UK). Without certification, the 62% thatching-grade quality premium cannot be priced into the market. Developing the standard is a prerequisite for the bond's primary revenue mechanism.
