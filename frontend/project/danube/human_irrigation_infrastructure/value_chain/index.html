Irrigation infrastructure has the weakest value chain (Abstract score e=15)[1] of the three Danube water projects. This reflects a fundamental gap in financial abstraction: EU CAP payments are area-based, not performance-linked. Farmers receive support per hectare enrolled in agri-environment schemes regardless of irrigation efficiency. There is no mechanism currently connecting the volume of water saved to the financial return received. In non-EU Danube countries (Ukraine, Serbia, Moldova), approximately 60% of irrigated area, no equivalent mechanism exists at all. A performance bond would create precisely this link, but requires a binding extraction cap as a pre-condition to avoid the irrigation efficiency paradox.[1]

Value chain gap: EU CAP payments (the primary revenue stream at basin scale) are not performance-linked to irrigation efficiency. A farmer abstraction 22,000 m³/ha at 47% efficiency (flood irrigation) receives the same agri-environment payment as one using 4,400 m³/ha at 88% (drip irrigation). The bond instrument would internalise this externality by tying coupon rates to measurable efficiency gains.
