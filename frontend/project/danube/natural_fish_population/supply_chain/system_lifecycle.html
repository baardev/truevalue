Phase Lifecycle N-D-C

Structural Interpretation

Phase 1 (71%) is the primary structural bottleneck: D (migratory corridor requirements of 100 native fish species) far exceeds C (actual accessible migration range) because 60 dams restrict movement and only 16% of historical migratory range remains open. Iron Gate I and II, built 1970-1984, occupy the most critical bottleneck position on the entire river and have no functional fish passage for large migratory species. The D-C imbalance at Phase 1 propagates forward: without upstream spawning access, recruitment depends entirely on restocking, Phase 2 is structurally decoupled from natural reproduction, and Phase 3 adult biomass is trending downward despite legal catch limits.

Phase 3 (80%, D approximately equal to C) is the system resilience node: 420,000 t total fishable biomass provides a substantial stock base, and the 38,000 t/yr sustainable yield represents a real biological capital asset. The 10-year declining trend signals that Phase 3 resilience is being eroded from below (Phase 1 corridor failure) and from above (Phase 4 quota compliance and illegal catch). The 480,000 recreational fishing licences and EUR 85M recreational fishing value represent a social and economic asset that depends entirely on Phase 3 remaining above the critical biomass threshold.

A Fisheries Habitat Restoration Bond targeting Phase 1 fish pass construction would directly address the D-C imbalance at the system root. Raising the fish pass operational rate from 22% to 45% (year 5 target) would increase accessible migration range from 16% toward 30% (year 10 target). The e-score of 18 is the lowest of all built Danube subprojects: no outcome-linked financial instrument currently exists for freshwater fisheries habitat in the basin. LIFE grants and EMFAF are input-based. A verified outcome bond indexed to fish pass operational rate and migration range accessible would shift e from 18 toward 45-55, creating the financial abstraction layer the system currently lacks entirely.
