The Danube is the largest river in the European Union by catchment, draining 10 countries along the mainstream from Kelheim (Germany) to the Black Sea. The river receives 75% of its discharge from major tributaries (Inn, Drava, Sava, Tisa, Morava, Prut, Siret). The Alpine sub-catchment contributes 35% of annual flow; the Carpathian arc contributes 40%; groundwater and smaller tributaries provide the remaining 25%. Chain balance is 79.2%, reflecting serious structural degradation in Phases 2 and 3 from the Iron Gate dam cascade.

Permanent structural legacy: These degradation metrics are not oscillating around a pre-disturbance baseline. They are one-directional consequences of hydraulic engineering decisions made between 1954 and 1984. The rip-rap revetment and groin field system that defines 80% of the Austrian and Hungarian reaches has eliminated the natural channel-forming processes that created the gravel bar substrate and side-arm network. Restoration requires active intervention (gravel augmentation, side-arm reconnection, targeted dike breaching) and cannot occur passively.
