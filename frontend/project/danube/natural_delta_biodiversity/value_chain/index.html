Delta Biodiversity Habitat — Value Chain

Natural system · UNESCO/Ramsar · Natura 2000 · Biodiversity Performance Bond potential

Value Chain Overview

The Danube Delta biodiversity service generates value through four principal channels: (1) ecotourism (€28M/yr, 80,000 visitors — the most directly monetised stream); (2) wetland carbon sequestration (11 Mt CO2 reed stock — potential carbon credit revenue); (3) fisheries nursery function (60% of Danube fish species use the delta as nursery habitat); and (4) existence and cultural value (estimated >€120M/yr in non-market terms). The dominant challenge is that most value is non-market or unmonetised, resulting in the low e-score of 20.[1] A biodiversity performance bond would be the primary instrument to bridge this gap.

Value chain gap: The biodiversity service generates >€200M/yr in combined market and non-market value, but only ~€43M/yr flows through revenue mechanisms that could service bond debt. The performance bond must increase the share of measurable, performance-linked revenue (ecotourism growth, carbon credits, payment-for-ecosystem-services schemes) and reduce dependence on non-performance-linked Natura 2000 area payments.
