Structural reading: Phase 0 (78%) is the permanent structural bottleneck: the Iron Gate dams intercept 57% of upstream sediment, meaning the first step of the cascade operates at a fundamentally reduced D-C capacity. Every downstream phase inherits this deficit.

Phase 2 as the resilience node: Phase 2 (88%, D=C) is the system's resilience point. Despite the Phase 0 sediment deficit, the remaining sediment and nutrient load, combined with a still-functioning flood pulse (Phase 1), sustains extensive and diverse habitat formation. This represents the system's residual carrying capacity and the primary asset for a bond instrument.

Bond intervention logic: A biodiversity performance bond should invest in Phase 3-4 improvements (invasive species management, lake restoration, wetland reconnection) that can increase species population outcomes and service delivery within the existing Phase 0-2 constraints. Phase 0 sediment cannot be restored without dam modification, but Phase 2-4 services can be improved through targeted management interventions. The e-score of 20 reflects that Natura 2000 and ecotourism revenues provide a partial but underdeveloped financial abstraction layer.
