Structural Interpretation

Phase 0 (79%) is the primary structural bottleneck: D (flood pulse requirements for forest function) exceeds C (actual hydrological connectivity delivered) due to 2,800 km of embankments and a 40% reduction in flood pulse amplitude. Lateral connectivity has fallen from 0.92 to 0.55, severing the forest-river exchange that sustains soil organic carbon accumulation, tree regeneration, and nutrient cycling. Every downstream phase in the biomass production cascade inherits this hydrological deficit. Phase 4 at 74% is the secondary weakness: ecosystem service value of EUR 715M/yr is only partially captured, with carbon credit revenue at EUR 3M/yr against a EUR 70M/yr potential.

Phase 2 (86%, D approximately equal to C) is the system resilience node: despite the hydrological disconnection, 160,000 ha of floodplain forest maintains canopy cover, with 78% within Natura 2000 boundaries providing legal protection. The 8,500 ha of old-growth forest in Donau-Auen and Gemenc represents the highest-quality carbon stock. This phase represents the primary asset base for a bond instrument, providing measurable area and growing stock data through Copernicus and EFI monitoring.

A Floodplain Reconnection Bond should target Phase 0 restoration (embankment setbacks, side-channel creation) which directly addresses the D-C imbalance at the system root. Connectivity restoration to 0.70 lateral index would unlock full carbon productivity potential. Phase 4 improvement requires parallel investment in carbon project registration and monitoring: the 0.05 Mt CO2/yr actual capture vs 1.0 Mt potential represents a 20-fold gap that a bond instrument, combined with human Phase 3 (carbon market access), is specifically designed to close. The e-score of 22 reflects that 3 active carbon projects provide partial financial abstraction; a performance bond indexed to connectivity and verified carbon additionality would shift e toward 55-60.
