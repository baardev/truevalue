Structural Bottleneck — Phase 2: Aggregation & Inland Transport (62.9%)

Soybeans arriving at Rosario-bound silos from multiple Argentine provinces and from Paraguay (82% of imports), Brazil, and Bolivia are physically commingled at collection points. The public release of Sio-granos data aggregates flows by zone rather than by individual farm or shipment, making it impossible to verify whether a given export volume is free of deforestation-linked soy. The D-C imbalance (D=220 vs C=350) precisely captures this: the system integrates volumes efficiently (high C) but lacks the governance mechanisms to constrain their origin (low D). Twelve traders controlling 88% of Gran Chaco soy exports could resolve this bottleneck through voluntary disaggregated data disclosure.
