🌿 Gran Chaco

South America's second-largest forest system: 107 million hectares across Argentina (65 Mha, 60.4%), Paraguay (30 Mha), Bolivia (12 Mha) and Brazil — the largest dry forest in South America. Under accelerating deforestation pressure from soy and beef expansion. Five-phase soy supply chain analysis from land conversion through EUDR export compliance, with deferred beef and ecosystem services chains. Bond focus: sustainability-linked instruments tied to deforestation-free sourcing KPIs.

Structural Issue — Aggregation Transparency Gap

Soy from multiple farms and countries (Paraguay supplies 82% of imports) is physically mixed at Rosario-bound silos, severing the provenance chain. This is the binding constraint for EUDR compliance, deforestation-free certification, and sustainability-linked bond KPI verification. Twelve traders control 88% of Gran Chaco soy exports: voluntary disaggregated data disclosure from this concentrated group could resolve the bottleneck. The EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR 1115/2023) currently excludes the Chaco biome due to its canopy structure, creating an additional policy gap that removes the primary compliance incentive.

Regulatory Context — EUDR and Bond Opportunity
EUDR Status (Regulation 1115/2023)
Critical gap: The Gran Chaco biome is currently excluded from EUDR scope because its dry-forest canopy cover falls below the regulation's 10% dense-canopy threshold — despite being the second-most threatened biome in South America. The Cerrado faces the same exclusion.

This means Argentine soy cake exported to the EU is not subject to EUDR due diligence requirements, removing the primary compliance incentive for deforestation-free sourcing from the Chaco. Advocacy to amend the biome definition is ongoing and is a direct SLB covenant opportunity.
