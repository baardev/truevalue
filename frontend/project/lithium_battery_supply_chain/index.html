Structural Alert: Midstream Concentration and Export-Control Exposure

The lithium battery chain is not bottlenecked at the mine, it is bottlenecked at the refinery and the materials plant. Upstream mining is geographically distributed across Australia, Chile, and China (lithium), the Democratic Republic of Congo (cobalt), and Indonesia (nickel), together with China (graphite). But midstream processing has consolidated overwhelmingly in China: approximately 65% of global lithium refining, 75% of global cobalt refining, and over 90% of global graphite refining. Cathode and anode active material production shows the same pattern, with China holding roughly 85% of cathode and over 90% of anode capacity. This concentration is compounded by documented and ongoing Chinese export restrictions on battery cathode and lithium/graphite processing technology, a structural trade-policy risk that no amount of downstream cell-manufacturing diversification (already underway in the US, EU, Korea, and Japan) can resolve on its own.

Upstream Mining

Australia, Chile, and China together mined about 85% of global lithium in 2023. The DRC accounted for roughly 65-70% of global cobalt mining; Indonesia for over half of global nickel mining.

Midstream Refining and Materials

65-90%+

China refines approximately 65% of global lithium, 75% of global cobalt, and over 90% of global graphite. China also holds approximately 85% of cathode and over 90% of anode material capacity.

🔋

Cell Manufacturing and Deployment

75-76%

China produced more than three-quarters of global lithium-ion battery output in 2024 (up from 57% in 2021), though the US, EU, Korea, and Japan hold growing counterweight capacity.
