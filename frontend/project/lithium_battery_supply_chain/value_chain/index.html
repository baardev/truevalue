Lithium Battery Supply Chain: Value Chain Analysis

Cost differentials, price decline trends, and the financial abstraction gap between existing diversification funding instruments and measurable supply-chain KPIs. Sources: IEA Global EV Outlook 2025, IEA Global Critical Minerals Outlook 2024, and industry reporting (Shanghai Metals Market).

Financial Abstraction Layer (e-score)

60.0
e-score: Financial abstraction coupling (PROVISIONAL / Stressed zone)

The e-score of 60.0 indicates that diversification funding instruments, US IRA Section 45X credits, Defense Production Act Title III awards, EU Critical Raw Materials Act co-financing, and Minerals Security Partnership coordination, are active and disbursing capital, but are not yet formally linked to measurable, phase-level supply-chain diversification KPIs (such as non-China share of refining or materials capacity). This is a structural gap between the financial layer and the physical chain it is intended to reshape.

To activate the full financial abstraction layer, three conditions would need to be met: (1) publicly reported, phase-level non-China capacity share targets tied to each funding instrument; (2) standardized reporting of refining and materials plant qualification milestones across recipients; (3) a coordinated Minerals Security Partnership dashboard tracking stockpile levels and offtake agreement volumes against diversification targets.

phi (φ): Proportional 62.0 Value distribution is the weakest axis. Mining-country value capture (Chile, Australia, DRC, Indonesia) is disproportionately small relative to the value captured by midstream refining and materials processing concentrated in China, a resource-curse-pattern value trap.
