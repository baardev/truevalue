# Nature Journal Submission Requirements

Reference summary of submission requirements for **Nature** (the flagship journal at [nature.com/nature](https://www.nature.com/nature)). Based on official author guidelines as of June 2026.

## Choosing an article type

Nature is not a single generic submission channel. The article type determines both process and editorial bar.

| Type | Best fit | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| **Review** | Synthesizes a topical field; not primarily the author's own work | Mostly **commissioned**; unsolicited submissions start with a **synopsis** |
| **Perspective** | Forward-looking, opinionated discussion of models and ideas | Same as Review: mostly commissioned; unsolicited via synopsis |
| **Article** | Original research with new results and data | Full manuscript submission; highest bar for novelty |
| **Analysis** | New analysis of existing large datasets (no new primary data) | Synopsis first, with `Analysis:` in the title |
| **Commentary / World View / News & Views** | Opinion or commentary on science policy and society | **Commission-only**; no unsolicited submissions |

Non-primary article types (Reviews, Perspectives, Analysis, etc.) **must not include original (previously unpublished) results or data**, except minimal new supporting findings. They are not eligible for gold Open Access and are published via the traditional subscription route.

## How to submit

- **Online only** via the [Manuscript Tracking System (MTS)](http://mts-nature.nature.com). Email submissions are **not** accepted for Articles, Reviews, Perspectives, or Matters Arising.
- General editorial enquiries: `nature@nature.com`
- Articles, Reviews, Perspectives, and Matters Arising are submitted through MTS, not by email.

## Editorial criteria

Nature accepts only about **8%** of submissions. **Most manuscripts are rejected without peer review.**

### Primary research Articles

A paper must:

1. Report **original scientific research** (main results and conclusions must not have been published or submitted elsewhere)
2. Be of **outstanding scientific importance**
3. Reach conclusions of interest to an **interdisciplinary readership**

Additional criteria by paper type:

- **Large dataset papers**: report a fully comprehensive dataset with complete validation, or provide significant technical advance or scientific insight
- **Technical papers**: considered when the technique will have significant impact on communities of fellow researchers
- **Therapeutic papers**: without novel mechanistic insight, considered if the therapeutic effect provides significant impact on an important disease

### Desk review criteria

Editors look for work that is **novel, arresting** (illuminating, unexpected, or surprising), and has **immediate and far-reaching implications**. Technical soundness alone is not sufficient. Readability matters: a non-specialist editor must quickly grasp why the work belongs in Nature rather than a specialist journal.

The judgement about broad readership interest is made by **Nature's editors**, not referees.

### Reviews and Perspectives

- Peer-reviewed and substantially edited by Nature's editors in consultation with the author
- Should not focus primarily on the author's own work
- Language should be accessible; novel concepts defined; specialist terminology explained

## Review and Perspective articles

Most Review and Perspective articles are **commissioned**. Authors wishing to submit an unsolicited Review or Perspective must do so through MTS.

### Initial submission: synopsis (not a full paper)

Submit:

1. **Synopsis** covering:
   - Basic structure of the article
   - Material to be covered, with depth indicated
   - Logical arrangement
   - Recent key publications in the area

2. **300–500 word background outline** that:
   - Summarizes progress to date
   - Makes the case for publication in a topical, interdisciplinary journal

Synopses prepared at this level of detail enable editorial input before commissioning and can reduce the need for substantial revisions later.

Nature **invites presubmission enquiries for Reviews and Perspectives** and recommends this route before writing the full piece.

### Review format (if commissioned and accepted)

- **200-word maximum preface** (appears on PubMed and Medline; include essential keywords)
- **Up to ~9 pages** as a guideline: ~**5,000 words**, **4 moderate display items**, **≤150 references**
- Focus on **one topical aspect**, not a comprehensive literature survey
- Can be controversial but should briefly indicate opposing viewpoints
- **Highlighted references**: bold one-sentence significance note under the top 5–10% of references
- Author responsible for obtaining permission to re-use figures published elsewhere

### Perspective format

Perspectives follow the same formatting guidelines as Reviews. They are more forward-looking and/or speculative, may take a narrower field of view, may be opinionated but should remain balanced, and are intended to stimulate discussion and new experimental approaches.

## Primary research Articles

### Initial submission (flexible format)

Nature is **flexible on format at initial submission**. Length and style generally do not block initial consideration. Recommended practice:

- **Single PDF or Word file** with text and figures combined (up to 30 MB)
- **Line numbers** (automatic in Word; manual in PDF)
- Reference list includes **article titles**
- LaTeX: supply compiled **PDF** until acceptance stage; LaTeX accepted at acceptance stage
- Each figure legend on the same page as its figure

If revisions are requested, the editor provides detailed formatting instructions.

### Target length (typical published Article)

| Format | Main text | Display items | References (guideline) |
|---|---|---|---|
| ~6-page Article | ~2,500 words + fully referenced ~200-word summary paragraph | 4 modest figures/tables | up to ~50 |
| ~8-page Article | ~4,300 words + summary paragraph | 5–6 modest display items | up to ~50 |

A "modest" display item, with its legend, occupies about a quarter of a page (~270 words). Composite figures needing half a page may require reduced text length.

The **Methods** section (typically ≤3,000 words, may be longer if necessary) is separate from main text. **Methods-only references do not count** toward the main reference limit.

### Title and summary

- **Title**: ≤75 characters including spaces; must fit on two lines in print; avoid technical terms, abbreviations, and active verbs
- **Summary paragraph**: ~200 words, fully referenced, aimed at readers **outside the discipline**
  - 2–3 sentences of basic-level introduction
  - Brief account of background and rationale
  - Statement of main conclusions (often introduced by "Here we show" or equivalent)
  - 2–3 sentences placing findings in general context
- **Final-stage abstract** (post-acceptance checklist): ≤230 words, at least 1 reference

See [annotated summary paragraph example](https://www.nature.com/documents/nature-summary-paragraph.pdf).

### Required manuscript order (final submission)

1. Title, authors, and affiliations
2. Abstract
3. Main text
4. Main text references
5. Main figure legends
6. Methods
7. Methods references
8. Data and code availability statements
9. Mandatory statements (see below)
10. Any additional information (e.g. consortia author lists)
11. Extended Data legends

Main figures and Extended Data figures/tables are supplied as **separate files**, not embedded in the manuscript file (final stage). Only main tables are included in the manuscript.

### Mandatory statements

- **Author contributions** (with distinguishable author initials for each author)
- **Competing interests** (required for all content)
- **Funding statement** (separate, if work arose from a specific grant)
- **Data availability** (in Methods under "Data Availability")
- **Code availability** (in Methods under "Code availability", if custom code is central to claims)
- **Ethics statement** (if applicable)
- Corresponding author contact details (Materials & Correspondence)
- **Acknowledgements** (optional; keep brief)

### Authorship and LLM policy

- Corresponding author(s) identified with an asterisk
- Corresponding author is solely responsible for communication with the journal
- **Large Language Models (LLMs) cannot be authors** (e.g. ChatGPT). Authorship carries accountability that cannot be applied to LLMs.
- LLM use must be documented in the Methods section (or a suitable alternative section if Methods is unavailable)

### References

- Main text and Methods have **separate, continuously numbered** reference lists
- One publication per reference number
- Include the **title** of each cited article or dataset
- No footnotes in reference lists

### Figures (initial and final)

**Initial submission**: sufficient resolution for referees to assess data; figures may be embedded in the manuscript PDF/Word.

**Final submission** (if accepted):

- RGB color, **300 dpi or higher**
- Typeface: Arial or Helvetica; symbol font for Greek letters
- Avoid red/green contrast; use color-safe combinations
- Width: 90 mm (single column) or 180 mm (double column); max height 170 mm
- Vector files with editable layers preferred (.ai, .eps, .pdf, .ps, .svg)
- Figure legends <250 words; begin with a brief title sentence

### Statistical information

Methods must include a statistics section describing tests used (one- or two-tailed). Report exact n values, error bar definitions, and exact P values where relevant. For ANOVAs: F values and degrees of freedom. For t-tests: t-values and degrees of freedom.

### Extended Data and Supplementary Information

- **Extended Data**: up to 10 multi-panel display items online (integral data, not in print). At initial submission, may be included as regular display items or Supplementary Information.
- **Supplementary Information**: essential background too large or specialized for print (e.g. large datasets, calculations). Prefer Extended Data for figures and small tables.

### Data and code

- **Data availability statement** required in Methods
- Certain data types must be deposited in appropriate public repositories with accession numbers
- Custom computer code central to main claims must be available to editors and referees on request; at publication, code should be released so readers can repeat results

### Life sciences and behavioural/social sciences

Authors must provide a completed **reporting summary** (life sciences and behavioural & social sciences research articles). Use Adobe Reader to fill out the form.

### Human subject data

- Phase II/III randomized controlled trials: follow CONSORT guidelines
- Prospective clinical trials must be registered (e.g. clinicaltrials.gov) before patient enrollment
- Human biospecimens: BRISQ reporting guidelines recommended (at least Tier 1 characteristics)

### Related manuscripts and preprints

- Disclose any related manuscripts with overlapping authorship under consideration or in press elsewhere
- **Preprints allowed**: original submitted version may be posted at any time (arXiv, bioRxiv, etc.)
- Accepted version may be posted 6 months after publication
- Published version (copyedited, Nature format) may not be posted on preprint servers

### Double-anonymized peer review

Optional. Authors must anonymize the manuscript themselves; editors do not verify anonymization.

## Cover letter

Optional but recommended. Use it to:

- Briefly discuss the importance of the work and why it is appropriate for Nature
- Avoid repeating the abstract and introduction
- Provide confidential information (conflicts of interest, related work in press or submitted elsewhere)

The cover letter is **not shared with referees**.

---

## Presubmission enquiries

Optional service to assess whether a manuscript is likely to be of interest. **Not compulsory** and **does not guarantee** acceptance if a full manuscript is later submitted.

- Response typically within **two working days**
- Editors cannot discuss negative responses, but authors may still formally submit
- For **Articles**: presubmission enquiries are not required and can be difficult to assess; editors prefer a **full manuscript** if the work is ready
- For **Reviews and Perspectives**: Nature **invites** presubmission enquiries via synopsis through MTS
- Do **not** send entire papers as presubmission enquiries

For Article presubmission enquiries: include a fully referenced first paragraph in Nature format in the abstract box; list references in the manuscript comments box.

---

## Review process

1. **Initial editorial screening**: authors usually informed within ~1 week if the paper is not being considered
2. If sent for review: typically **2–3 referees**, chosen for independence, expertise, and availability
3. **Transparent peer review** (Articles submitted from February 2020): reviewer comments and author rebuttals may be published online as supplementary material (not applicable to Reviews)
4. **Decision options**:
   - Accept (with or without editorial revisions)
   - Accept in principle pending author revisions
   - Defer pending further experiments or technical work
   - Reject with or without invitation to resubmit
   - Reject outright
5. If rejected: **manuscript transfer** to other Springer Nature journals available via the decision letter link

### Appeals

Authors may appeal in writing with a point-by-point rebuttal. Decisions are reversed only if the original decision was made in error or critical new information has been added. Manuscripts cannot be submitted elsewhere while an appeal is pending.

---

## After acceptance

- Strict format requirements apply as advised by the handling editor
- Paper is subedited for clarity, length, terminology, and figure/table fit
- Authors receive proofs for approval before publication
- Embargo: research becomes publicly available at 16:00 London time / 11:00 US Eastern on publication day
- Authors may discuss their paper with media from six days before publication date

## Nature vs Nature Portfolio journals

This document covers the **flagship Nature journal** only. The Nature Portfolio includes many specialist journals (e.g. Nature Sustainability, Nature Human Behaviour, Nature Communications), each with its own author guidelines, scope, and format requirements. If the audience is primarily specialist rather than broad and interdisciplinary, a Nature Portfolio journal may be a better fit.

Consult the specific journal's "For Authors" page before submitting.

## Proposed paper: Tholonic model for governance and sustainability

This section applies the general requirements above to a planned Nature submission presenting the **Tholonic N-D-C framework** in the context of **governance** and **sustainability**, using the TrueValue Analytics project portfolio as cross-domain evidence (with more projects planned).

### Recommended article type: Perspective

Given the framing (a general model applied across governance and sustainability domains, forward-looking, portfolio of case studies still expanding), the strongest fit is a **Perspective**, not a primary research **Article**.

Nature defines Perspectives as:

> Intended to provide a forum for authors to discuss models and ideas from a personal viewpoint. They are more forward looking and/or speculative than Reviews and may take a narrower field of view. They may be opinionated but should remain balanced and are intended to stimulate discussion and new experimental approaches.

A **Review** is weaker fit unless the paper synthesizes an established external literature field rather than primarily presenting your own applied framework. Reviews also should **not be focused on the author's own work**. Your portfolio can appear as illustrative evidence, but the paper must read as a field-level argument, not a project catalogue.

**Commentary** and **World View** are commission-only and not available for unsolicited submission.

**First step:** submit a **synopsis + 300–500 word background outline** via MTS (presubmission enquiry route for Reviews/Perspectives). Do not write the full ~5,000-word piece until editorial interest is confirmed.

---

### Interdisciplinary hook (what Nature editors need to see)

Nature selects for work that interests **scientists outside your immediate subfield**. The cover letter and background outline must answer: why should a biologist, physicist, or policy scientist care?

Suggested framing:

1. **Problem:** Sustainability governance fails in structurally predictable ways. Ostrom documented successful CPR institutions and Hardin's tragedy of the commons; regulatory collapse is a distinct failure mode. Existing frameworks assess principle presence (binary) but not **continuous structural balance** between constraint (D) and participation/output (C).

2. **Proposal:** The Tholonic N-D-C model treats any coherent system (CPR institution, municipal delivery chain, commodity supply chain, ecosystem service chain) as a recursive triad where sustainability emerges when D ~ C. Imbalance degrades the emergent state N and propagates to adjacent phases.

3. **Evidence:** The same measurement grammar (phase-resolved D, C, balance score B, five model constants pi/phi/sqrt2/ln2/e) produces comparable diagnostics across domains that are normally analyzed in isolation: Ostrom CPR cases, Bristol municipal governance, Danube basin ecosystem services, commodity supply chains (cocoa, shea, olive oil), water institutional models (NEWater vs OCWD), and broken-system failures (Jackson MS water, ERCOT Uri, SVB).

4. **Claim for Nature:** This is not another sustainability index. It is a **cross-domain structural diagnostic** linking governance theory (Ostrom), supply chain transparency (TVPCI), and institutional failure modes through one formally grounded balance metric, with falsifiable predictions about collapse type (C-dominant vs D-dominant).

5. **Forward look:** The expanding project portfolio is presented as an open, schema-first evidence base (not a closed case study set), with explicit OPAQUE flags where data is missing.

Avoid leading with mathematical constant derivations or TVPCI pricing mechanics. Those belong in Methods, Extended Data, or companion papers. Nature's summary paragraph must be accessible to non-specialists.

### Risks and mitigations (Nature-specific)

| Risk | Nature concern | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Own-work focus | Reviews/Perspectives should not read as self-promotion | Lead with Ostrom, CPR literature, sustainability governance gap; projects are **validation cases**, not the subject |
| Too many domains | Looks like a catalogue | One unifying figure (recursive N-D-C phase map); 4–6 cases max; rest in Extended Data or companion site |
| Unpublished original data | Non-primary types cannot include substantial new results | Use published/ public data only; flag illustrative B-scores as derived from documented sources; no new field surveys |
| Methodological opacity | Editors screen for non-specialist readability | Define N, D, C in plain language before equations; one worked example figure |
| Competing submissions | Must disclose related work in press | Disclose papers 1–17 series, especially paper 17 (Ostrom governance) and paper 2 (TVPCI supply chain) |
| Specialist vs broad interest | Flagship Nature requires cross-field impact | Emphasize governance-sustainability bridge, not commodity pricing or gold supply chain specifics |

### Draft synopsis outline (for MTS presubmission)

Use this as the starting structure for the synopsis + 300–500 word background outline.

**Working title (≤75 characters):**

`Governance balance as a cross-domain sustainability diagnostic`

**Preface (≤200 words, for full manuscript):**

One paragraph: CPR governance, institutional failure modes, and the claim that D-C balance is measurable across domains.

**Proposed section structure:**

1. **Introduction:** Two failure modes of commons governance (C-dominant extraction collapse; D-dominant regulatory brittleness). Ostrom's principles as structural, not arbitrary. Gap: no continuous balance metric.

2. **The tholonic model (brief):** N as emergent state; D as constraint; C as contribution/flow. Balance score B and five axis constants as diagnostic axes (not derived in main text).

3. **Governance as structural balance:** Map Ostrom's eight principles to D-type and C-type roles. B-score on documented cases (Törbel, Alanya, open-access fishery, Atlantic groundfish).

4. **Cross-domain validation:** 3–4 applied cases from the TrueValue portfolio (municipal, basin, commodity, water institutional contrast), each one figure + one paragraph.

5. **Implications for sustainability policy:** Phase-resolved intervention (which side to move: D down or C up); connection to sustainability-linked bonds and MRV; falsifiable predictions.

6. **Outlook:** Open schema-first evidence base; planned extensions; limits and OPAQUE data flags.

**Display items (4 moderate figures/boxes):**

- Fig 1: Canonical N-D-C triangle schematic (N top blue, C lower-left red, D lower-right green)
- Fig 2: Ostrom principles mapped to D/C with B-score cases
- Fig 3: Cross-domain comparison panel (4 mini phase maps: Bristol, Danube, cocoa, water)
- Box 1: Five-axis failure diagnosis table (pi, phi, sqrt2, ln2, e) with intervention categories

**Key references to cite:** Ostrom (1990, 2010), Hardin (1968), recent Nature/Sustainability governance literature, UN SDG institutional frameworks, selected commodity sustainability certification critiques.
