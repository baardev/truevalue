How to Read This Document

Two Audiences, One Document

This report is written for two readers simultaneously. The non-technical reader — a business associate, a potential partner, or a stakeholder who needs to understand what questions remain unanswered — will find plain-language questions in Section 2, organised by theme. No technical background is required to understand or act on them.

The technical reader — the analyst or developer who needs to know exactly which data fields are synthetic, which formulas are incomplete, and which calculations contain internal inconsistencies — will find a precise field-level audit in Section 3. This section references specific variables, file paths, and formula definitions by name.

Both audiences share the same goal: understanding what needs to be done before the Investor Report can be presented to a sophisticated institutional investor who will ask hard questions.

Section 2 — For the Non-Technical Reader

The Plain-Language Questions We Need Answered

These are the questions a well-informed non-technical associate — or a non-specialist investor — would ask if they read the Investor Report carefully. They are organised by theme. Each question identifies a gap that, once resolved, directly strengthens a specific claim in the report.

Theme A: The Core Scores — Where Do the Numbers Come From?

  • A1
    The balance scores (37%, 54%, 61%) are the centrepiece of the investor argument. Are they real measurements, or are they calculated from a model?
    data Currently synthetic. The Appendix discloses this honestly. To make them real, we need C and G component data from at least one participant in each phase. What would we need to ask a mining company, refinery, or logistics operator for?
  • A2
    The report defines Constraints and Growth Capacity but never lists exactly what goes into them. What are the specific factors that make up, say, the Logistics phase Constraints score of 380? Could we explain this to an investor?
    validation This is the most important question for investor credibility. If we cannot show the sub-components, a sophisticated investor cannot validate the scores independently — which is a red flag.
  • A3
    We compare Shea's Phase 6 (91% balance) to Gold's Phase 6 (37%). Is the 91% score measured from actual Serious Shea or Acorn data, or is it also modelled?
    datashea This comparison is the emotional heart of the Shea section. If both scores are synthetic, the comparison loses its force. Do we have a contact at Serious Shea or Acorn who could validate or provide the underlying numbers?
  • A4
    We have set target balance scores for each phase (e.g., 85% for Mining, 50% for Logistics). How were these targets decided? Is there a reference for what a "good" target looks like in each phase?
    validation These targets shape how the whole scoring system is interpreted. If they were set by judgment alone, they need at minimum a documented rationale. At best, an analogy from another framework (Fairtrade certification standards, LBMA responsible sourcing thresholds) would validate them.

Theme B: The Gold Chain — What Do We Actually Know?

  • B1
    We state that approximately 90% of the world's newly mined gold is processed by five LBMA-accredited refineries. Is this number current? The LBMA Good Delivery list has more than five refineries on it — do we mean five specific dominant ones?
    data The WGC Gold Demand Trends report and LBMA Annual Report should have current refinery throughput data. We need a year-stamped citation.
  • B2
    We describe refinery fee schedules as "private" and "secret." Do we know even a ballpark range? Even a broad range ($2–8/oz?) would make the "hidden cost" claim more concrete without requiring disclosure of confidential data.
    data Publicly disclosed mining company financial reports sometimes contain treatment charge ranges. Court cases involving refinery disputes occasionally reveal fee structures. Industry analysts (Wood Mackenzie, Metals Focus) sometimes publish estimated ranges.
  • B3
    We describe the Logistics & Vaulting phase as "structurally architected for opacity." Has anyone on the team spoken directly with a vault operator, security logistics professional, or insurance broker who can confirm this characterisation from the inside?
    validation A single attributed or anonymised firsthand account from a logistics professional would transform this from a structural inference into a validated finding. Former Brink's or G4S employees, Lloyd's of London precious metals underwriters, or bullion banking risk officers are accessible sources.
  • B4
    What volume of gold actually flows through each of the nine phases annually? We describe the phases qualitatively but give no physical tonnage data. Could we add approximate volumes (using WGC supply statistics) to make the scale of each phase tangible?
    data The WGC publishes annual mine production (~3,600 t), recycling (~1,200 t), and COMEX/LBMA volumes. These would make the transparency table and phase descriptions significantly more concrete.

Theme D: The 10-Year Projection — Is +71% Defensible?

  • D1
    The projection showing a +71% cumulative TrueValue advantage at Year 10 is the most compelling number in the report. The Appendix honestly discloses that the growth rates come from a structural model, not from historical data. Is there any real-world supply chain case we can point to where a transparency-driven reform produced a measurable financial improvement?
    validation Fairtrade adoption data, CDP score improvements correlated with cost of capital, LBMA RGG adoption and subsequent gold price premium, FSC certification and timber price premiums — any of these could serve as an empirical anchor for the projection's direction, even if not the exact magnitude.

T-1  ·  CRITICAL: Balance Score Formula vs. Displayed Values ⚠ Internal Inconsistency

The Appendix states: Balance Score = min(C, G) / max(C, G) × 100. Applying this formula to the phase data objects reveals that the displayed balance scores are systematically lower than the formula would produce. This is not a rounding issue — the gap is 6–13 percentage points across all phases. Either the formula in the Appendix is incomplete (there is an additional factor not disclosed), or the balance scores and C/G values were set independently of each other.

Section 5 — Suggested Next Steps

A Practical Action Plan

The gaps identified in this document are not an indictment of the Investor Report — they are the normal condition of a first-generation analytical framework that has been built rigorously from the structural logic of its subject matter. The report is intellectually honest (the Appendix clearly discloses the synthetic data basis) and structurally sound. What it now needs is a targeted evidence-gathering effort to replace synthetic values with real ones, confirm its formulas are internally consistent, and anchor its projections in at least one empirical analogy.

Estimated effort to reach Tier 1 resolution: Most Tier 1 items can be resolved by one analyst in 4–6 weeks without requiring external data access. The formula inconsistency (T-1), sub-component definitions (T-2), and projection base index (D3/T-5) are all internal documentation tasks. The Shea data confirmation (A3/T-4) requires one outreach email to Serious Shea or Acorn. IP status (E3) requires one legal consultation.
